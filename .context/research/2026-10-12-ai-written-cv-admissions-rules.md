# Admissions rules on AI-written CVs and statements (master's, 2026/27 cycle)

Date: 2026-10-12. Scope: the priority list in the brief. Cambridge, Gates Cambridge and Oxford were not re-checked (already recorded; their wording was not re-fetched, so "unchanged" is **Unverified**).

## Answer

Five priority institutions publish a rule that covers the **CV itself**:

| Institution | CV covered? | Generation | Assistance allowed | Product action |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **TUM** (all degree programmes) | Yes. The style guide names the CV among "self-created" documents | Banned | None stated. "Refrain from using AI" | **Refuse** (or warn hard). Detection means rejection "for deception" |
| **Harvard Griffin GSAS** (all degree programmes) | Yes, "resume/CV and employment history" | Banned | Minor edits and grammar checks | **Refuse** generation. Grammar and edit help only |
| **UC Berkeley Law LL.M.** (a master's) | Yes. The ban covers "all other written components", which include the CV/résumé | Banned | The statement allows basic proofreading. Nothing is said for the CV | **Refuse** |
| **Stanford Knight-Hennessy Scholars** (scholarship) | Probably. The rule covers "all written materials", and the application has a résumé page | Banned (drafting, rewriting, polishing) | Brainstorming, organising, grammar and spelling | **Warn**, or refuse for KHS |
| **Stanford Energy Science & Engineering** (dept.) | Probably ("all written materials") | Banned | Brainstorming, minor spelling and grammar edits | **Warn** |

One more funder requires **disclosure** instead of banning AI:

| **DAAD** scholarships | Applies to the whole application | Allowed as an aid | Yes | **Require the label** "Produced with the aid of (tool used)" and a signed authenticity declaration |

The following rules cover **statements only, not the CV**. A rule like R-087 should be scoped to the statement for these:

- **Edinburgh (PG)**: using ChatGPT to write the personal statement "could be considered fraud".
- **LSE**: has an AI clause, but only on the **undergraduate** UCAS page. Nothing was found on the graduate pages.
- **KCL (PG)**: "own work", with no mention of AI.
- **Warwick (PG)**: "in your own words", with no mention of AI.
- **Berkeley Law** also has a separate statement clause: no help beyond basic proofreading.
- **TUM M.Sc. AI in Society**: a signed declaration that the letter was written independently.

**Advisory only** (AI is discouraged, not banned): Sciences Po and MIT departments (BE, Chemistry, HST).

**NOT FOUND** at admissions level: UCL, Imperial (PGT), Manchester (PG), LMU (central), Heidelberg, PSL, Campus France / Eiffel, TU Delft, Leiden, University of Amsterdam (central), ETH Zurich, EPFL, KTH, Lund (central), Aalto, Berkeley Graduate Division (central), MIT Office of Graduate Education (central), Stanford Graduate Admissions (central), and Erasmus Mundus (EACEA and the consortia).

## Per institution

### UK

- **LSE**
  - **Graduate: NOT FOUND.** Pages checked: Statement of academic purpose, Supporting documents, Rules and exceptions, The admissions process, How to apply.
  - **Undergraduate only:** LSE may reject a UCAS personal statement that "has been created with the use of Artificial Intelligence (using tools such as ChatGPT)".[^1] It allows AI to "proofread original writing".[^2]
  - Covers the personal statement only, not the CV. It does not apply to master's applications.
- **Imperial: NOT FOUND** for postgraduate taught.
  - The PGT personal statement page has no AI clause. It says only to write as you normally would and to avoid clichés.[^3]
  - A Business School admissions blog post (3 Dec 2025) says AI is fine for structure and proofreading but should not be "the main author", and says the school has "introduced new ways to detect AI". **Unverified**: it was read through Exa and not fetched live. It is a blog, not a policy page.
- **UCL: NOT FOUND.** Checked: graduate programme pages for several MSc programmes, the graduate application form PDF, and a Brave `site:ucl.ac.uk` search. Programme pages ask applicants to "use your own words", which is not an AI rule.
- **Edinburgh (PG): statement only.**
  - "You could be committing fraud if … you use AI applications such as ChatGPT to write your personal statement".[^4]
  - The personal statement page repeats this: using "AI apps such as ChatGPT could be considered fraud".[^5]
  - The CV is not mentioned. No assistance carve-out exists at PG level. The UG page allows spelling and grammar tools, but it is undergraduate.
- **King's College London (PG): general, not AI-specific.** "any personal statement, research proposal, or other written materials submitted as part of an application are your own work".[^6] It names similarity-detection software, not AI. "Other written materials" could reach a CV, but this is not an AI rule.
- **Manchester (PG): NOT FOUND.** Checked: How to apply for master's study, Assessing your master's application, and Alliance MBS supporting documents. The AI clause on the UCAS personal statement page is undergraduate only (**Unverified**: read through Exa, not fetched).
- **Warwick (PG): NOT FOUND for AI.** The PG personal statement guide says only "It must be in English and in your own words".[^7] Also checked: the PG FAQ and the PG Admissions Statement.

### Germany

- **TUM: ban, CV included, all programmes.**
  - The style guide applies to "documents you have created yourself, such as a CV, an essay, or statement of purpose".[^8]
  - The rule: "use of AI tools such as ChatGPT to create documents for an application at TUM is not permitted".[^9]
  - If detected, the applicant is "excluded from the application process, i.e. your application will be rejected for deception".[^10]
  - No assistance carve-out is stated.
  - The M.Sc. AI in Society programme also requires "a signed declaration of having prepared the letter independently and without outside help".[^11]
  - Contrast: TUM's Executive MBA guidelines (2025 PDFs) **permit** AI for the letter and essay if the applicant discloses it. **Unverified**: read through Exa, not fetched live.
- **LMU: NOT FOUND** centrally (guide to applying for a master's degree). The Graduate School of Systemic Neurosciences' motivation-statement PDF treats generative AI as a usable "template creator" and discourages relying on it. **Unverified**: the PDF could not be fetched as text.
- **Heidelberg: NOT FOUND** for AI. One programme's admission regulations ask for a motivation letter "personally written by the applicant", which is not an AI rule (Exa only, not fetched). Also checked: the master's application manual and the Scientific Computing how-to-apply and FAQ pages.
- **DAAD: allowed, with disclosure.**
  - "The use of artificial intelligence (AI tools) in application procedures is permitted as an aid provided that certain principles are observed".[^12]
  - "The use of AI tools must be clearly indicated by stating in your documents that text passages were created with the help of an AI tool."[^13]
  - Applicants also sign an authenticity declaration.[^14]
  - It covers all application documents, so the CV is included by implication.

### France

- **Sciences Po: advisory only.** On the graduate motivation letter: "Also, thread lightly if you decide to use AI tools."[^15] The CV is not mentioned. Also checked: the Graduate application file page and the Graduate FAQ.
- **PSL: NOT FOUND.** Checked: master's programme pages (AI and Society, Cognitive Science), the ENS cog-sci cover-letter guidelines and FAQ, and the research-project guidelines PDF.
- **Campus France / Eiffel: NOT FOUND.** Checked: the Eiffel programme pages, the 2026 call and the 2026 master's application-form PDF. The form asks for a CV and a "projet professionnel" with no AI clause.

### Netherlands

- **TU Delft: NOT FOUND** at admissions level. All AI pages found are teaching and assessment policies (Teaching Support, Mechanical Engineering, Geomatics).
- **Leiden: NOT FOUND** at admissions level. The Law & Digital Technologies "Required documents" page has no AI clause. The AI pages found are for coursework (International Studies, the Faculty of Governance and Global Affairs, the Faculty of Humanities).
- **University of Amsterdam: NOT FOUND centrally. One programme-level lead.**
  - A Graduate School of Psychology "Statement of motivation" form (2022 PDF) has the applicant tick: "I declare that I have written this letter of motivation myself, without the help of any other person or the use of any AI". It covers the motivation letter only.
  - **Unverified**: read through Exa; the PDF could not be fetched as text, and it may be out of date.
  - UvA's student AI page is about coursework, not admissions.

### Switzerland

- **ETH Zurich: NOT FOUND.** Results are teaching guidelines, library and integrity pages, and an admissions-system user manual.
- **EPFL: NOT FOUND.** The online application page lists the CV and statement of purpose with no AI clause. The AI pages found are about studying and assessment.

### Nordics

- **KTH: NOT FOUND.** Programme entry-requirement pages describe the CV and motivation letter with no AI clause. The student blog is not official policy.
- **Lund: NOT FOUND centrally.**
  - The School of Economics and Management's "Sharpen your Statement of Purpose and CV" page has no AI clause.
  - The Psychology master's application form (2024-10 PDF, for the 2025 intake) asks applicants to "write a SoP using your intelligence, not artificial intelligence". This is a discouragement, not a ban, and covers the statement of purpose and research proposal only. **Unverified**: the PDF could not be fetched as text.
- **Aalto: NOT FOUND.** Checked: the master's admissions FAQ and Apply to master's programmes. The careers PDF and student AI guide are not admissions pages.

### US

- **Harvard Griffin GSAS: ban, CV included.**
  - The rule lists the statement of purpose, short answers, personal statement and "resume/CV and employment history are expected to be your own work".[^16]
  - "Work may not be that of a third party nor that created by generative artificial intelligence."[^17]
  - Assistance allowed: using AI "to suggest minor edits or to identify grammatical errors". Any wider use is "forbidden".[^18]
- **Stanford: no central rule. Rules exist at the scholarship and department level.**
  - **Knight-Hennessy Scholars:**
    - "All written materials you submit must represent your own ideas, reflections and experiences".[^19]
    - Not permitted: generating draft responses, or rewriting or "significantly refine your writing".[^20]
    - Allowed: "Light editing that preserves your original meaning and voice (e.g., grammar, spelling)".[^21]
  - **Energy Science & Engineering:** applicants "may use AI tools in a limited, supportive way: brainstorm, organize early ideas, or make minor edits such as spelling or grammar".[^22] Same wording as KHS.
  - Microbiology & Immunology has the same wording (Exa only, not fetched).
  - The central Graduate Academic Policies say only that Stanford may "investigate the authenticity, accuracy, and authorship" of materials (Exa only, not fetched).
- **MIT: advisory, at department level, statements only.**
  - Biological Engineering: "You are free to utilize AI tools (e.g., ChatGPT) in the preparation of your statement of objectives and personal statement, however, we discourage you from relying too heavily on them."[^23]
  - The same text appears for Chemistry and HST (Exa only). HASTS "discourage[s]" AI (Exa only).
  - The central Office of Graduate Education procedures page has no AI clause.
- **UC Berkeley: no central rule. Ban at Berkeley Law LL.M., CV included.**
  - The required written components include "a personal statement (up to three, double-spaced pages), a curriculum vitae or résumé (no page limit)".[^24]
  - "While the use of generative AI is prohibited in all other written components of this application" (the only exception is an optional AI essay).[^25]
  - Personal statement: "written without assistance from others, including generative AI".[^26]
  - The Graduate Division pages (Requirements, Writing Your Statements, Admissions FAQ) were checked: NOT FOUND.

### Erasmus Mundus

- **NOT FOUND.** No EACEA rule and no consortium rule was found.
- Checked:
  - the EACEA "Questions received at the EMJM kick-off (updated January 2026)" PDF (Exa only; it has no AI item);
  - the EMAI (UPF) FAQ and the access and admission pages;
  - the DEAI (ULB) application manual;
  - the LCT FAQ;
  - two Exa searches and two Brave searches.
- Every "EMJM AI policy" statement found came from consultants or blogs, which do not count.

## Implications for the CV generator

1. **Refuse to generate, or allow only grammar and editing:** any TUM programme, Harvard GSAS, Berkeley Law LL.M.
2. **Warn:** Stanford KHS and ESE. Their wording covers "all written materials" but never names the CV.
3. **DAAD is a disclosure rule, not a ban.** A generated CV used in a DAAD application must carry the note "Produced with the aid of (tool used)". A warning that says only "AI not allowed" would be wrong here.
4. **Statement-only rules** (Edinburgh PG, Berkeley Law statement, UvA Psychology letter, TUM AI in Society letter) should attach to the letter engine, not the CV. Edinburgh does not cover CVs.
5. **The rules vary by programme within one university** (Stanford, MIT, UvA, Lund, TUM EMBA versus TUM degrees). A university-level rule for Stanford or MIT would be wrong.

## Method and tools

- **Search tools:**
  - Exa with `includeDomains` on each institution's own domain, the owner-domain step. Some searches were narrowed to the PG admissions path.
  - Brave `site:` searches for UCL and Berkeley, plus two open Brave searches for Erasmus Mundus.
- **Quotes:** every quoted page was fetched live with scrapling `get`.
- **Tools not used:** Tavily (available, not needed) and Context7 (not relevant to the question).
- **PDFs not fetched as text:** UvA Psychology, Lund Psychology and LMU GSN. scrapling returned unreadable binary output for them, so they appear as **Unverified** leads without footnotes.
- **Institution list:** the institutions came from the brief, not from discovery.

## Open questions

1. **Does a CV built from the student's own verified facts count as "created by generative AI"?** This needs a product or legal call.
   - Harvard bans work "created by" AI but allows "minor edits".
   - TUM bans using AI "to create documents".
   - The generator writes the CV's prose, so a cautious reading puts it inside both bans. No source addresses structured assembly of facts the applicant supplied.
2. **Does Stanford KHS's "all written materials" cover the résumé?** The KHS application has a résumé section, but the policy never names it.
3. **Cambridge, Gates Cambridge and Oxford wording** was not re-fetched in this pass, so it is not confirmed unchanged.
4. **Erasmus Mundus:** about 160 active consortia each publish their own admission rules. Two searches cannot rule out individual consortium clauses. A sweep would need the consortium list from the EACEA API.
5. **UvA, Lund and LMU programme-level PDFs:** their current (2026/27) wording is unconfirmed. The UvA form is dated 2022.
