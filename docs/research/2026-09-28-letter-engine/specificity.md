## US research programmes
- MIT EECS CommLab (PRAC — student-run comm lab of the dept; fetched; no date)
  https://mitcommlab.mit.edu/eecs/commkit/graduate-school-statement-of-purpose/
  "State which professors in the program you would plan to work with. Show how their research areas align with your
  background and your goals." / "If you have had a positive discussion with someone at the department, describe how those
  interactions made you think that you and the department may be well-matched." / "echo that language" of programme website.
- Berkeley EECS grad FAQ (OFF; fetched) https://eecs.berkeley.edu/academics/graduate/faq-3/
  "you do not need to contact faculty ... on the application, you will list faculty members who you would like to work with"
  -> faculty named in a FORM FIELD, not necessarily in the SOP.
- Iowa State "Advice from graduate faculty" pressbook (STAFF; snippet only, 403): "if the applicant lists two faculty and they
  are very, very different, that can raise a question of whether the applicant is focused enough"
  https://iastate.pressbooks.pub/statementofpurpose/chapter/advice-from-graduate-faculty/
- UW English "Writing the SOP: general advice" (OFF-dept; fetched; undated) https://english.washington.edu/writing-statement-purpose-general-advice
  "Are there particular professors with whom you want to study because of their area of expertise? Whatever the reason, explain it."
  "should not relate a life story or flatter either the applicant or intended readers." "Is the program noted for a particular
  emphasis, speciality, or orientation? ... show that you did your homework."
- Cornell Grad School ASOP (OFF; fetched) https://gradschool.cornell.edu/inclusion/recruitment/prospective-students/writing-your-statement-of-purpose/
  "If there are specific faculty you are interested in working with, check the program's ASOP instructions and determine how
  best to mention this in your essay." "why you are a good fit for the program and why the program is a good fit for you."
- Harvey Mudd CS SOP guide (OFF-dept advising own undergrads; fetched; undated) https://www.hmc.edu/cs/student-resources/cs-grad-school-information/cs-statement-of-purpose-guide/
  last paragraph customised; "it's wise to mention at least a few faculty members and projects"; readers are "professors of
  computer science and not ... admissions officers". Interests are "not a contract".
- Chris Blattman (STAFF, U Chicago Harris prof; fetched; 2022-01-11) https://chrisblattman.com/blog/2022/01/11/phd-applicants-writing-your-statement-of-purpose/
  name people who "Are tenure or tenure-track faculty", "Have their primary appointment in the department you are applying to",
  "Are actually there and take students (i.e. they didn't retire last year, etc.)". Delete "Generic or flattering statements
  about being excited to join a program, your admiration for the faculty". Research-proposal section "40-60% of your entire statement".
  Cite "recent and recognizable research papers" to locate the question (literature, not flattery).
- Adrian Sampson (STAFF, Cornell CS prof; fetched; 2023-12-06) https://www.cs.cornell.edu/~asampson/blog/gradstatement.html
  "It's a good idea to briefly mention the professors you might want to work with at each school." A handful of names (routing).
  Generic why-this-school ("large university") -> cut.
- Jason Eisner (STAFF, JHU CS prof; fetched; updated 2025-11-22) https://www.cs.jhu.edu/~jason/advice/prospective-students.html
  reader question: "Would you be a great collaborator for me on any of the topics that I'm considering working on next?"
  "Mentioning me by name may make me more likely to see your application and interview you." Not determinative.
  -> In US CS PhD, names are a ROUTING device.

## Germany / NL / CH

## Key admissions-faculty testimony (STAFF)
- Nathan Schneider, Georgetown CS+Linguistics faculty reviewer (STAFF; fetched via scrapling; 2019-07-23)
  https://nschneid.medium.com/inside-ph-d-admissions-what-readers-look-for-in-a-statement-of-purpose-3db4e6081f80
  Criteria Qualifications / Focus / Fit. "a master's program revolves around coursework, whereas for a Ph.D. student, the
  ultimate goal is success in research. So for master's applicants, grades in relevant courses and test scores are an
  important, if not primary, basis". Fit depends on "which faculty members are looking for new advisees that year or are
  about to retire". "it's a good idea to name 2 or 3 faculty members as potential advisors, and explain which aspects of
  their research appeal to you ... it will also be used ... to help route your application". "if you only name 1 person,
  there's a risk that they will not be taking new advisees, which will hurt your chances; and if you name a large number of
  faculty, it could make your interests look unfocused." tenure-track do bulk of advising. Argument form: "(Department/potential
  advisors) would be a good fit because…". 1-2 pages; no "flowery oratory".

## Peer-reviewed (PEER)
- Samraj & Monk, English for Specific Purposes 27(2):193-211, 2008 (online 2007) https://doi.org/10.1016/j.esp.2007.07.001
  abstract fetched from ScienceDirect: successful statements to 3 US master's programmes (Linguistics, MBA, Electrical
  Engineering) + departmental informants: "although statements from the three disciplines may contain the same rhetorical moves,
  they differ in the constituent steps used to realize some of the moves." Info on writing statements for specific master's
  programmes "not consistently available" ("semi-occluded" genre).
- Wang, J. English for Academic Purposes 2024/25, "These are my 'selling points'" https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jeap.2024.101469
  (abstract fetched) 100 successful US SoPs, Business/Engineering/Liberal Arts/Science + interviews incl. admissions committee
  members. Highlights: "Business SoPs: emphasizing professional training; Science: academic experiences." "Engineering: combining
  academic and professional experiences." "Liberal arts: mixing academic, professional, and extracurricular experiences."
  -> discipline decides which of the applicant's evidence carries the fit, not just which programme feature is named.
- MEXT 2027 research guideline (OFF; PDF fetched) https://www.studyinjapan.go.jp/en/_mt/2026/04/01-2027_Research_Guidelines_E.pdf
  Field of Study and Research Plan "should describe the applicant's past and present field of study and research theme and plan
  in Japan as it will serve as an important reference for placement of the applicant to a university." Embassy track: "Applicants
  should first contact the division of international student affairs at the university (not an academic advisor you wish to be
  supervised by)"; "It is not permitted to include in the Placement Preference Application Form the names of universities or
  supervisors ... that have declined the applicant's request." -> supervisors named in the PLACEMENT FORM, only if not declined;
  the plan itself must be placeable (not tied to one named person).
- GKS 2026 guideline (OFF, mirror) FAQ: Study plan: "describe the general direction and outlines of your study plans ...
  elaborate on your study objectives in Korea and tangible plans during and after" — no faculty-naming instruction found in text.
- Mor Harchol-Balter, CMU CS (STAFF; PDF fetched; "Last updated 2014") https://www.cs.cmu.edu/~harchol/gradschooltalk.pdf
  "what admission committees are really looking for is a research statement." Template: para 1 areas of interest ("helpful for a
  committee to determine which professors should read your application"); paras 2-4 projects; para 5 why PhD; "Sixth paragraph –
  Tell us why you want to come to CMU. Whom might you like to work with? What papers have you looked at from CMU that you enjoyed
  reading? Why is CMU the right place for you?" -> fit = 1 of 6 paragraphs (~15%). Choosing: subtract faculty "not actually present"
  and those "not taking on students". "A third of all applicants end up working in an area different from" their statement.
  Mistakes: "grade regurgitator", "boy genius" (childhood origin).
- Stanley Chan, Purdue ECE (STAFF; fetched; undated, 2015-16 example) https://engineering.purdue.edu/ChanGroup/write_statement.html
  Marked-up bad examples: "I would be interested in working in Professor ABC's lab, Professor EFG's lab, and Professor XYZ's lab."
  (too broad). "Professor Chan's research is intriguing, and I have long been interested in his work." -> reply: "Thank you for your
  kind words. But if you know what we do, you should explain specifically why topic ABC is interesting to you." Wants applicants to
  "Develop extensions of our current projects ... Bring in new problems that you and i2Lab are mutually interested in."
  CV restatement: "I can find this information on your CV already."
  => FLATTERY-NAMING (name + praise, no topic) is explicitly penalised by the named professor himself.
- Tom Alberts, Utah Math (STAFF; fetched; 2023-04-28) https://www.math.utah.edu/~alberts/grad_recruiting/personalstatement/
  "state which of these groups you see yourself most likely interacting with, and why." "If there are any specific faculty members
  whose research you find particularly compelling you should also list their names." "one or two paragraphs that you customize for
  each school." "Don't write more than two pages."
- Andy Pavlo, CMU CS (STAFF; fetched; 2015-10-01) https://www.cs.cmu.edu/~pavlo/blog/2015/10/how-to-write-a-bad-statement-for-a-computer-science-phd-admissions-application.html
  childhood openings read as weakness ("reaching back to childhood"). No naming advice.
- MIT EECS CommLab personal-statement page (PRAC; fetched) https://mitcommlab.mit.edu/eecs/commkit/graduate-school-personal-statement/
  "mention other departments or groups at the university that you might be interested in collaborating with"; "Yours must quickly
  convey that your goals and interests match one or more researchers in the program".
- WriteIvy, Jordan Dotson (PRAC consultancy; fetched; 2023-06-23) https://writeivy.com/writing-about-professors-in-your-sop-dont-screw-it-up/
  "It's not about what their research specialty 'is.' It's about the techniques they used, the questions they posed"; per professor
  "more than one sentence ... might not need more than two"; 2-3 professors. Dismisses lab-openings concern (contradicts Schneider/
  Blattman — weight faculty over consultancy).
- GradPilot "2-3 rule" (PRAC/aggregator; fetched; undated) https://gradpilot.com/news/how-many-professors-name-statement-purpose
  red flags: not accepting students, sabbatical/leave, no pubs 3+ yrs, emeritus, old work; don't mix professors of other
  universities; field variation on contacting faculty. Attributes "Don't necessarily pick the most famous professor" to MIT — not
  verified on the MIT page I fetched (FOLK until verified).

## SYNTHESIS (see final report for table + engine rules)
Professor naming is a ROUTING + FIT signal read by faculty in research admissions (US PhD/research MS; UK DPhil/MRes; DTPs).
Elsewhere the reader asks for a different device: specialisation/track/thesis topics (TUM, TU Delft), modules (LSE, UCL, Chevening,
DAAD), resources + goals + contribution (MBA), approach/practice (art & design), home-country/funder goal (DAAD, Chevening, Fulbright).
Fulbright forbids naming universities/faculty. MEXT routes supervisor names through the placement form, not the plan.
Space: fit ≈ one paragraph (Harchol-Balter 1/6; Alberts "one or two paragraphs"; TUM CSE Q4 200/750 words ≈27%; Stanford GSB
"why Stanford" 350/1000 ≈35% but that essay IS the fit essay). Per professor: 1-2 sentences (Dotson), 2-3 professors (Schneider).
Connect-don't-describe: Chan, TUM CSE ("relate ... do not simply list"), Chevening ("explain how it connects to your background"),
Oxford DTP ("which aspects of their work most interest you"), Schneider "(advisors) would be a good fit because…".
