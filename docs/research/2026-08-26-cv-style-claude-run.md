# Visual & Typographic Style for Master's-Application CVs (When No Format Is Dictated)

## TL;DR
- When a university or funder gives no format, the evidence-backed default is a **plain, single-column, reverse-chronological CV of one to two full pages, 11–12 pt body type, ~1 inch / 2–2.5 cm margins, no photo/color/icons**, with substance foregrounded — this is advisory convention, not rule, and it aligns across US, UK, Dutch, Nordic and Turkish university career/admissions guidance.
- **Almost nothing about CV style is binding.** The rare hard style rules that exist come from funders and format families (DAAD "tabular form, in accordance with EU standards," commonly ≤3 pages; Europass grids for some Erasmus Mundus/DAAD-EPOS calls; Japan's JIS rirekisho) — and destination conventions differ enough that a generic "international" CV reads as odd in Germany (no photo/DOB), Japan (no rirekisho form), or the US (photo/DOB included).
- The biggest design risk is importing **employment-resume dogma** — the one-page rule, ATS keyword optimization, and the recruiter "six-to-eight-second scan" (an Adobe Express figure, Dec 2024) — into an admissions document; committees read for academic fit, spend meaningful minutes per file, and reward clean skimmable structure over decorative "designed" templates.

## Key Findings

**1. The advisory consensus is remarkably tight.** Across independent university career services on three continents, the recommended visual defaults converge: reverse-chronological order within sections; education first for a master's applicant; 10–12 pt body type; ~1 inch (2.5 cm) margins; consistent heading style; bullet points; one or two *full* pages; simple formatting over decoration; name/page number on each page. Princeton, Oxford, Warwick, Cornell, Penn, Harvard, Leiden, Utrecht (UCU), and Turkish and German university pages all say versions of this.

**2. Binding style rules are rare and belong to funders/format-families, not universities.** DAAD's PRIME FAQ states the CV "should be prepared in a tabular form in accordance with EU standards," and named DAAD programs (Study Scholarships – Master Studies, ERP, Leadership for Africa) explicitly accept CVs up to three pages, hand-signed, with no chronological gaps; some DAAD calls (EPOS) require Europass. Many Erasmus Mundus consortia require or accept Europass, some impose a 2-page cap. Japan's rirekisho is a JIS-standardized fixed form. These are the exceptions to flag; everything else is advisory.

**3. Chevening does not require a CV at all** — its own application guidance confirms the online application "does not ask you to upload a CV." A useful reminder that "funder CV rules" must be checked per-program, not assumed.

**4. Destination conventions genuinely diverge** on photo/DOB, chronological direction (Japan runs *oldest-first*; the West runs reverse-chronological), date formats, and length when uncapped.

## Details

### TRACK A — Destination conventions (visual, not content)

**Germany / Austria / Switzerland — the *tabellarischer Lebenslauf*.** Visually this is a two-column table: **dates on the left, positions/descriptions on the right**, entries stacked so all date columns align, section titles (Bildung/Berufserfahrung) as bold labels. For a master's/study application, education is placed high, often before work. Reverse-chronological within sections is now standard. Length: typically ≤2 A4 pages (DAAD accepts up to 3 for its tabular CV). A German reader finds a narrative/prose CV odd, and expects a place/date line and signature at the foot (compliance item, out of scope). Note: most detailed German sources are commercial CV-builder or job-market sites (mygermanuniversity, die-bewerbungsschreiber, lebenslaufdesigns) — advisory, employment-tinged.

**France — university (*Mon Master*) and grande école.** Expectation: **one page**, reverse-chronological (*antichronologique*), clear aerated layout, professional font (Arial/Calibri/Times New Roman, 10–12 pt), sober colors because juries print CVs, minimal logos/icons, a *titre* naming the exact target program at the top. Zety France explicitly frames this against a jury "examining CVs of hundreds of candidates." All French sources found are commercial (Zety, ResumeLab, Modeles-CV, LiveCareer) — advisory only; no ministry/university primary *style* page surfaced (see Gaps).

**The Netherlands.** University guidance (Leiden Career Zone, Utrecht/UCU, UvA-PPLE) stresses **content over layout**, "clear and easy to skim," headings/bullets/spacing, and **1–2 pages (preferably 1)** for a master's application. UvA-PPLE explicitly reframes the "CV" as an educational-background timeline, "not a multipage CV as if you were applying for a job." Dutch *employment* CVs commonly include a photo, but that is a job-market convention (out of scope).

**The Nordics.** Convention: **clean, minimalist, reverse-chronological, 1–2 pages**; egalitarian culture disfavors "overselling" and overdesigned layouts; photo optional/neutral. Sweden follows **ISO date format (YYYY-MM-DD)**; anti-discrimination norms mean *personnummer*/DOB are omitted. Sources here are commercial international-CV guides plus culture notes — advisory, largely employment-oriented.

**UK & Ireland.** Strong convergence: **1–2 full A4 pages**, reverse-chronological, clean and uncreative, bullet points, **11 pt minimum** font, no photo (bias-avoidance). Oxford Careers Service checklist (guidance): *"Are fonts (type and size) consistent and not too small (11pt minimum)? Is the layout well balanced, with effective use of space, using the full width of the page? Broadly speaking, does the most relevant information occupy the most space?"* — and it advises placing "the role/position and the name of the organisation on the left-hand side of the page and the dates on the right-hand side." Warwick recommends Calibri 11 (10 acceptable minimum). Crucially, **Oxford's graduate admissions office** states most courses *don't* want a separate CV, and where required it should be "one or two pages… a summary of your recent achievements." Master's-specific guidance (King's, Bath, FindAMasters, Mastersportal) says admissions tutors do *not* expect PhD-style publication lists. Ireland-specific *admissions* style guidance was thin; UK guidance is the closest proxy (see Gaps).

**US & Canada — the academic-CV-vs-resume split at graduate level.** This is the key distinction. For most master's applications (especially professional master's/MBA/policy), programs want a **1–2 page resume-style document**; for research-track master's in the sciences/humanities they may want a **CV** that can run longer. Cornell, Pitt, Toronto (UTM), UCSB, Cal State Long Beach, and Princeton all describe the "grad-school CV" as a CV-resume hybrid. Formatting defaults: 11–12 pt, 1-inch margins, left-justified, bold/caps used consistently for headers, name larger, page numbers. Penn Career Services (academic/faculty CV guidance): *"Avoid putting dates on the left-hand margin, as this emphasizes when you did something rather than the experience you are trying to showcase."* Harvard's GSAS guide takes the same view — keep dates/locations ("less important information") on the right, substantive detail on the left.

**Japan — rirekisho.** For domestic academic/employment use the **rirekisho (履歴書)** is a **JIS-standardized fixed-form grid**: designated fields, a photo, and — unusually — **chronological order *oldest-first*** (education from high-school graduation upward, with paired 入学/卒業 entry/exit lines). It uses the Imperial-era calendar alongside Western dates. A Western free-form "designed" CV looks wrong to a Japanese reader expecting the form; the free-form *shokumu keirekisho* is the closer analogue to a Western CV. (Rirekisho sources are job-market oriented; graduate admissions in Japan for international programs — e.g. MEXT — frequently supply their own forms, a compliance matter out of scope.)

**China.** Chinese CVs (简历) run **1–2 pages**, commonly include a **professional photo, DOB, gender**, emphasize education and the university's prestige tier (985/211), and use **year-month-day (年月日)** dates. For international/MNC and many university-admission contexts an English CV is accepted; domestic contexts prefer Chinese. University of Birmingham's careers guidance for the Chinese labour market confirms name/gender/DOB/photo/contact as the expected header block.

**Türkiye.** Two distinct worlds. For academic/faculty and postgraduate-research contexts, universities demand the **YÖK/YÖKSİS-format academic CV** — a plain, black-and-white, single-column, reverse-chronological text document with publications/projects foregrounded and language proficiency shown as test scores (YDS/YÖKDİL/TOEFL), explicitly *not* the colorful Europass/skill-bar style, which would read as unserious to a committee. General student CV guidance (Fenerbahçe Üniversitesi) recommends Times New Roman/Arial 11–12 pt, ≤2 pages.

### TRACK B — The reader's side

**B1 — What admissions/committee sources say they want.** Oxford Careers (guidance, not requirement): fonts "not too small (11pt minimum)," layout "well balanced, with effective use of space, using the full width of the page," and "the most relevant information [should] occupy the most space." Oxford graduate admissions (requirement-level, program-specific): a required CV "should be one or two pages." University career/writing centers that review fellowship and admissions materials (Kansas CURF, Texas A&M, Binghamton) prescribe **10–12 pt legible fonts, ≤2 pages, reverse-chronological, error-free**. GoGrad summarizes that "admissions committees facing a stack of resumes often use errors as a way to sift through the pile" and recommends 12-pt font, bold headers, consistent spacing/alignment.

**B2 — How evaluators actually read (label: research/reported practice, not rule).** The best-sourced datapoints concern *US undergraduate* admissions and must be labeled as such. The University of Pennsylvania's shift to **committee-based evaluation (CBE)** is the anchor case: as reported in *The Chronicle of Higher Education* (Eric Hoover, "Working Smarter, Not Harder, in Admissions," March 2017), Dean of Admissions Eric J. Furda said initial reviews that "used to take at least 15 minutes, now happen in about four." *Inside Higher Ed* (June 2017) reported that under CBE two officers "are able to make a recommendation typically within 4–10 minutes," versus roughly 25–30 minutes for the former solo read; the *Wall Street Journal* (via Bucknell University) reported applications that "used to take 12 to 15 minutes to review… now completed in just six to eight minutes." These describe undergraduate holistic review, not master's admissions, and the newer per-file figures largely trace back to the same Penn/CBE reporting. The transferable inference is about *reading conditions* — documents are skimmed in bulk, on screen and sometimes in print — so scannable structure and legible type matter; the numbers themselves are not master's-admissions fact.

**B3 — What reads as "template-generated"/careless to an academic reader (label: opinion/professional literature).** Academic-CV guidance repeatedly names the tells: **colorful graphics, photos, icons, two-column Canva-style templates, mixed citation styles, decorative fonts, inconsistent formatting, broken DOIs, cramped pages, padding.** These are the consistent professional opinion of career-service and academic-editing writers; the Turkish YÖK guidance is unusually blunt that a "colorful Europass CV… risks being found unserious" by an academic committee. Label: professional opinion, corroborated across many independent guides, not a sourced admissions-office rule.

### TRACK C — Element-level conventions

**C1 — Section headers.** Dominant convention: **left-aligned, bold, often ALL-CAPS or small-caps, applied consistently** (Princeton: "if you make one heading in bold and uppercase… all the headings… should follow the same style"). Rules/underlines are optional and de-emphasized; simplicity preferred. Advisory.

**C2 — Entry layout & date placement.** Two coexisting, *contradictory* primary-source schools:
- **German/tabular school:** dates in a **left column**, detail right ("dates left, details right").
- **Anglo-American academic school:** push **dates to the right** so substance leads on the left. Oxford Careers advises "the role/position and the name of the organisation on the left-hand side… and the dates on the right-hand side." Penn Career Services: *"Avoid putting dates on the left-hand margin…"* Harvard GSAS: keep "dates and less important information on the right side… the left side should have important details like university, degree, job title." Harvard Law OPIA and McMaster likewise prescribe right-margin dates; the University of Arizona College of Medicine–Phoenix is a documented **exception** prescribing left-margin dates for faculty/medical CVs.
- **Bullets vs prose:** bullets dominate for employment/experience entries; **research positions** often warrant a short descriptive block (project, methods, contribution) because the reader is a specialist. Location typically inline with the institution or right-aligned near the date.

**C3 — Name/contact block & links.** Name largest (often 16–18 pt, or 4–6 pt above body), bold, at top; contact details evenly spaced below. For academic CVs, **ORCID** is recommended at the head of a publications list or in the contact header (University of Sydney); **Google Scholar / personal site / GitHub** links go in the header. In a PDF, links should be live hyperlinks and DOIs presented as resolvable links.

**C4 — Publications on a master's CV.** Strong cross-source convention *for those who have them*: **reverse-chronological, one consistent discipline citation style (APA/IEEE/Vancouver/Chicago), the applicant's own name bolded (or ALL-CAPS/underlined), author order preserved, DOI included, subsections by type, optional numbering.** Co-first authorship marked with dagger/asterisk + footnote. **But** UK master's-application guidance (Mastersportal, The Job Studio) stresses most master's applicants won't have publications and tutors don't expect them — so this section is conditional. Advisory but discipline-enforced: a mangled citation "reads as careless or… someone outside the discipline."

**C5 — Language skills.** Outside the Europass grid, the dominant layout is a **simple list pairing each language with a CEFR level (A1–C2)**, ideally **CEFR code + plain-language word** ("B2 (Upper-Intermediate)"), optionally with a recent **test score and date** in brackets (e.g., "C1 (IELTS 7.0)"). CEFR self-assessment is acceptable on a CV; the Council of Europe self-assessment grid is the underlying instrument. Turkish academic CVs use national test scores (YDS/YÖKDİL) rather than bars. Avoid decorative proficiency bars/percentages. Placement near the end unless the language is central to the program.

**C6 — Typography.** Named, sourced font guidance: **11–12 pt body is the near-universal floor** (Oxford: 11 pt minimum; Warwick: Calibri 11; Princeton, Penn, UW, Binghamton, TAMU, Turkish pages: 10–12 pt). Fonts actually recommended by university sources: **Times New Roman, Arial, Calibri, Cambria, Garamond, Verdana** — i.e., standard system fonts, serif or clean sans, chosen for legibility, not "academic feel." **Margins ~1 inch (2–2.5 cm).** Line spacing single with space between sections. **No source that actually reads applications was found asserting "serif looks more academic"** — that claim is designer taste, not admissions guidance (see Gaps).

## Recommendations

**Stage 1 — Ship a single evidence-backed default template.** Single column; reverse-chronological; education first; name 16–18 pt bold; headers left-aligned bold small-caps; **11–12 pt body (Calibri, Arial, Garamond, or Times New Roman); 2.5 cm margins; generous white space; live hyperlinks; name+page number in a footer; no photo, color, icons, or proficiency bars by default.** This satisfies UK, US, Dutch, Nordic and Turkish academic guidance simultaneously. Threshold to change: any program/funder that dictates a format overrides this entirely.

**Stage 2 — Build destination "skins" that flip only the variables that genuinely differ:**
- Germany/Austria/Switzerland: two-column tabular (dates left), photo/DOB block per destination norm, signature line.
- Japan (domestic): route to a rirekisho form, oldest-first, not the free template.
- China: header with photo/DOB, YYYY年MM月 dates.
- France (Mon Master): one page, target-program *titre* at top.
- Türkiye (academic/research): YÖKSİS plain-text order, test-score language block.

**Stage 3 — Date-placement toggle.** Because primary sources genuinely disagree, expose a toggle: **right-aligned dates (Anglo-American academic default, per Oxford/Penn/Harvard)** vs **left-column dates (German tabular default)**, keyed to destination. Do not hard-code one globally.

**Stage 4 — Counselor checklist enforcing the "careless tells":** consistent single citation style with own-name bolded; no mixed fonts; no widow/orphan pages; full pages only (no half-empty page 2); every DOI/URL resolves; zero typos. These map directly to what reviewers report using to cull.

**Benchmarks that change the recommendation:** (a) an explicit funder/program style rule → obey it verbatim and flag as binding; (b) a research-track master's in a publication-heavy field → enable the CV (longer, publications section); (c) a professional master's/MBA → enforce the 1–2 page resume-style variant.

## Caveats
- **Advisory vs binding:** With the narrow exceptions of DAAD (tabular, "in accordance with EU standards," commonly ≤3 pp, hand-signed), Europass-mandating calls (some Erasmus Mundus/DAAD-EPOS), and Japan's JIS rirekisho, essentially **every style point in this report is advisory convention**, not a stated requirement. Treat it as strong default, not law.
- **Source quality:** Track A (Germany, France, Nordics, China, Japan) leans heavily on commercial CV-builder and job-market sites, which are employment-oriented; university-primary *style* pages for these destinations were sparse. Track B/C and UK/US rest on stronger university-primary sources (Oxford, Penn, Harvard, Princeton, Cornell, etc.) and named journalism (*Chronicle*, *Inside Higher Ed*, *WSJ*).
- **Employment vs admissions contamination:** Much online "CV style" is recruiter/ATS advice (e.g., the six-to-eight-second scan). This report has excluded ATS/one-page-dogma reasoning from admissions recommendations.
- **Undergraduate vs master's:** The best "minutes per file" reading-time data is *US undergraduate* admissions and is labeled as such; do not present it as master's-admissions fact.
- **Gaps (no published guidance found):** (1) No admissions-office source endorsing serif-vs-sans on aesthetic grounds — this remains designer taste. (2) No master's-specific (as opposed to undergraduate) empirical study of reading time/skim patterns. (3) No university-primary French or Nordic *admissions* style page; conventions inferred from commercial + culture sources. (4) Ireland-specific admissions style guidance was thin; UK guidance is treated as the closest proxy and labeled accordingly.

## Deliverable 2 — Companion Design Note (for the template designer)

**The coherent picture.** Strip away the noise and the world's university career services and graduate-admissions offices are describing the same object: a **quiet, legible, structured document that a tired reader can skim in a couple of minutes and trust.** That object is single-column, reverse-chronological, education-first (for a master's applicant), set in an ordinary 11–12 pt face with ~1-inch margins and real white space between sections, and it never uses photos, color blocks, icons, skill bars, or two-column "designed" layouts. This is the picture Princeton, Oxford, Penn, Harvard, Cornell, Warwick, Leiden, Utrecht and the Turkish YÖK guidance all paint independently — which is why you can build **one default template and be right almost everywhere.** Build that first, and build it beautifully within those constraints (typographic hierarchy through weight and space, not ornament).

**Where the sources genuinely disagree — and you must expose a choice, not pick one silently.** The sharpest real conflict is **date placement.** The German *tabellarischer Lebenslauf* puts dates in a left column ("dates left, details right"); the Anglo-American academic tradition does the exact opposite and tells applicants to keep the left edge for the institution and role, banishing dates to the right so the reader's eye lands on *what* not *when* (Oxford, Penn, Harvard all say this in almost the same words). Both are primary-sourced; neither is wrong. So make date placement a **destination-keyed toggle**, defaulting to right-aligned for UK/US/Canada and left-column for the German-speaking countries. A second, softer disagreement is **length**: France wants one page for a master's, the UK/US allow two full pages, and DAAD allows three — so length should be a destination variable, not a hard global cap. A third is the **photo/DOB block**, which is expected in Germany, China and Japan and actively discouraged in the UK, US, Canada and Sweden (this is largely settled in the out-of-scope compliance layer, but the *visual* header shape changes with it).

**The rare hard rules — flag them loudly in the UI.** Style is advisory almost everywhere, so the handful of genuinely binding constraints deserve prominent, non-dismissable flags: DAAD's "tabular, EU-standards, ≤3 pages, hand-signed, no gaps"; Europass mandates on specific Erasmus Mundus and DAAD-EPOS calls; a given EM consortium's 2-page cap; and Japan's JIS rirekisho form for domestic use. And the instructive negative case: **Chevening does not want a CV at all** — proof that the safest architecture checks each program's stated requirement *before* offering the applicant any template. Treat the format engine as: (1) does the program state a requirement? obey it; (2) if not, apply the destination skin; (3) if no destination signal, apply the universal default.

**Where there is no guidance and taste must decide — so you own these, not the evidence.** Four decisions have no admissions-side backing and are legitimately yours: **(1) serif vs sans-serif.** No one who actually reads applications was found claiming serif "looks more academic"; that is folklore. Pick for legibility and print/screen robustness (Garamond and Calibri both defensible), and don't market it as an admissions advantage. **(2) The exact typographic hierarchy** — name size (16–18 pt is the observed range), heading treatment (bold small-caps is a safe, common choice), and inter-section spacing — is unconstrained beyond "consistent and skimmable." **(3) Micro-layout of the language block** outside Europass: convention only tells you to pair a CEFR code with a plain word and avoid bars, so the visual treatment is yours. **(4) French and Nordic *admissions* specifics** rest on commercial and cultural sources, not university-primary pages, so your French one-pager and Nordic minimalist skin are informed guesses, not documented mandates — label them internally as such and revisit if a primary source appears.

**One caution to hard-code into the team's thinking.** The loudest, most confident CV advice on the open web is *employment* advice — the one-page rule, ATS keyword stuffing, the "recruiter spends six seconds" trope (an Adobe figure). None of it governs an admissions committee, which reads for academic fit and, even at its fastest (the US undergraduate CBE data), spends minutes rather than seconds and rewards structure over salesmanship. Every time a design instinct traces back to "recruiters prefer," stop and check whether an admissions reader was ever the source. Usually they weren't.
