# CV Preparation for Master's Applications: A Source-Grounded Rules and Findings Report

## TL;DR
- **For master's admissions the CV is almost always a *required but secondary* document** — it sits below transcript/degree, statement of purpose/motivation letter, and (where used) references and test scores in nearly every published rubric found. It is binding to *submit* at most European universities, but its *content* is rarely prescribed and its *weight* in the decision is low; a peer-reviewed STEM-admissions mapping review (Kurysheva et al., *Int. J. STEM Education*, 2023) found the CV among methods "not given substantial weight in selection decisions."
- **Track B's premise is unevidenced.** There is no peer-reviewed study, audit experiment, or admissions-office outcome data showing that a CV tailored to a specific master's programme outperforms a strong generic one. The only on-the-record institutional statement demanding tailoring is Sciences Po's; everything else is university careers-service guidance, testing-body advice, or coaching folklore. All rigorous *tailoring/writing-quality* evidence comes from job recruitment and does not transfer.
- **The genuinely binding, primary-sourced rules are narrow:** submit a CV where the programme lists it; German/Swiss universities frequently require a *gapless, tabular* CV (ETH Zürich, DAAD, uni-assist), with omissions causing exclusion in DAAD's case; and all UK/EU institutions treat fabrication as grounds for cancellation of the application or offer. Making an unknown institution legible is handled institutionally through credential evaluation and transcripts, not through the CV.

## Key Findings

1. **The CV is required to submit but light on prescribed content.** uni-assist, ETH Zürich, TU Delft, Lund, Uppsala, KTH, Sciences Po and the DAAD all name the CV as an application document, but almost none specify its internal fields. Where content *is* specified, it is a format/completeness rule (tabular, gapless), not a content rule.
2. **Reviewers are academics/committees, not a keyword machine.** Master's file review is done by faculty, programme committees or admissions staff — not automated ATS keyword screening. ATS keyword screening is a recruitment-world practice; no primary evidence places it in university master's admissions.
3. **No published time-per-file figure exists for European master's admissions.** The well-known "8-minute" and two-reader figures are all US *undergraduate* admissions folklore/industry claims; a University of Florida psychology graduate-admissions slide deck offers "~15 minutes or less" per folder for PhD-level review, but nothing comparable is documented for European master's.
4. **Tailoring: one policy source, zero studies.** Sciences Po explicitly requires it; a peer-reviewed STEM review suggests the CV barely moves decisions; the closest rigorous recruitment experiment (NBER 30886) tests writing *quality*, not tailoring-to-role.
5. **Fabrication is binding and punished; "emphasis vs fabrication" is not distinguished in writing.** UK universities (Manchester, UCL, Bristol, Edinburgh, City) and UCAS have explicit admissions-fraud policies with application/offer cancellation and de-registration as consequences. None found draws a written line between legitimate emphasis and misrepresentation.
6. **Institutional legibility is an institutional job, not a CV job.** ENIC-NARIC, the Diploma Supplement, ECTS, WES and uni-assist exist precisely to interpret unfamiliar institutions and grades; admissions offices ask for these, not for the applicant to self-assert rankings or equivalence on the CV.

## Details

### TRACK A — How a master's committee reads a CV

**A1. Weight relative to other documents (per-country).**

- **Germany (uni-assist route):** uni-assist forwards documents; the CV is listed under "other documents" that "may be required" depending on university and course. uni-assist's own guidance: "Some universities require a CV without any omissions which is current up to the date of application… Do not leave out any period." This is a *completeness/format* rule (binding where the university requires the CV), not a weighting statement. The decisive documents in the German pipeline are the degree/transcript (converted to the German grade via the VPD) and language proof. Source tier: (a) published policy.
- **Netherlands (TU Delft, Erasmus/EUR):** CV is a listed required document alongside transcripts, motivation/essay, references and language proof. Admission is gated first on a hard threshold — TU Delft requires "A Bachelor's Cumulative Grade Point Average (CGPA) of at least 75% of the scale maximum, unless specific requirements are defined for the country in which you obtained your bachelor's degree." The CV is supporting. Tier: (a).
- **France (Sciences Po):** Graduate application requires CV + ≤1,000-word motivation letter (or 800-word cover letter for the College route). Sciences Po is the one institution that states the CV "is essential" and "must be tailored." French master's admissions are described (by a French applicant, on The Student Room — folklore) as career/goal-oriented, consistent with Sciences Po's own motivation-letter guidance. Tier: (a) for the CV requirement/tailoring statement.
- **Nordics (Sweden — Lund, KTH, Uppsala):** Applications go through the national portal universityadmissions.se. CV is *programme-specific* — required for some master's programmes, not all. Lund/LUSEM: "There is no specific format requirement or template for the CV." KTH: "All applicants who meet the requirements will have their merits rated on a scale of 1 to 75. The merit rating is done individually by each programme according to their set selection criteria." The Statement of Purpose is repeatedly emphasised over the CV. Tier: (a).
- **UK:** Taught master's admissions typically centre on degree classification (a 2:1 is the common threshold), transcript, personal statement and references; the CV is variably requested. No single UK rubric found makes the CV central. Careers-service pages (e.g., Edinburgh) advise tailoring — guidance, not requirement. Tier: (a)/(d).
- **Switzerland (ETH Zürich):** CV is a *general required document* for all master's applications and must "contain a full and continuous history (no biographical gaps)" starting from high school, written in the language of instruction. This is a binding format rule. Programme-specific documents (portfolio, GRE, references) vary. Tier: (a).
- **Austria (University of Vienna):** CV commonly required among master's application documents; no distinctive content rule located from a primary source in this run (gap). Tier: unverified.
- **Erasmus Mundus consortia:** Selection is by a consortium committee against published, often *points-based* criteria. Example (EMJM Emergency & Critical Care Nursing): work/research experience and publications scored only "if directly related to the Master's" — e.g., 0.5 points per six months' relevant work up to 4 points; 0.5 points per relevant publication up to 4. The CV is the evidence vehicle for these points but the *criteria*, not the CV format, drive scoring. Europass CV is frequently requested/required by Erasmus Mundus programmes. Tier: (a).
- **US (contrast):** Graduate admissions is faculty/committee-driven and holistic; the SOP and letters are described by faculty and university sources as "absolutely critical," often carrying "as much weight as your transcript" (University of Washington English dept.). The resume/CV is supporting. (Note: a commonly cited "Kaplan 2014: 44% examine transcript first" figure could not be verified; Kaplan's 2014 survey of 152 US graduate admissions officers instead found "41% of graduate schools say that a low GRE score is the biggest 'application killer,'… far outdistancing a low undergraduate GPA at 27%." Use the verified figure.) Tier: (a)/(b)/(c).
- **Canada (contrast):** Resume or CV is commonly requested; provincial/institutional practice varies. Guidance sources are largely careers-advice/coaching. Tier: (d) mostly.

**A2. Who reads it, and for how long.** Master's review is done by faculty, programme committees, and/or admissions staff (Erasmus Mundus: a Selection Committee "composed of a representative of each Partner university and the coordinator"). The much-cited "8 minutes per file," two-reader split, and 4-minute Penn figures are **US undergraduate** admissions claims, sourced to consultancies/blogs (Ivy Coach, Oriel, Cosmic), not policy. A University of Florida psychology slide deck (graduate, US, PhD-oriented) estimates "15 minutes or less" per folder and a "1 to 2 minute" per-component rule. **No published time-per-application figure was located for European master's admissions.** This is a genuine null.

**A3. Automation / ATS.** ATS keyword screening is documented only in **recruitment** (and in university *staff/faculty hiring* — HigherEdJobs, UConn careers). Multiple recruitment-industry sources (Lever; HiringThing citing Assaf's research) call the "ATS auto-rejects 75% of resumes" claim a myth that benefits resume services; "There is no universal ATS score." **No primary evidence was found that university master's *admissions* screen CVs by ATS/keyword.** The assertion is CV-builder marketing; treat as folklore and, on current evidence, false for admissions.

**A4. Hyperlinks.** All on-the-record hyperlink-following behaviour found is **recruiter** behaviour (GitHub, portfolios). No admissions-office statement was located about master's readers opening DOIs/GitHub/portfolio links. Where portfolios matter (design/architecture programmes), they are requested as separate required uploads, not as CV hyperlinks. Null for admissions.

### TRACK B — Does programme-specific tailoring change outcomes? (PRIMARY)

**The honest state of the evidence: the premise is unevidenced.** No peer-reviewed study, audit/correspondence experiment, randomized trial, or admissions-office outcome dataset was found measuring whether a tailored CV outperforms a strong generic one in master's admissions.

- **B1 (empirical evidence):** None in admissions. The closest empirical signal is Kurysheva, van Rijen, Stolte & Dilaver, "Validity, acceptability, and procedural issues of selection methods for graduate study admissions in… STEM: a mapping review," *International Journal of STEM Education* 10(1), Sep 2023 (DOI 10.1186/s40594-023-00445-4), which places the CV among selection methods "not given substantial weight in selection decisions" ("Other selection methods, even if required, were not given substantial weight in selection decisions… Among them are… curriculum vitae (CV), photographs, essays…") — evidence that cuts *against* the CV mattering, not for tailoring. In **recruitment** (non-transferable), the one rigorous RCT — van Inwegen (Wiles), Munyikwa & Horton, NBER Working Paper 30886, Jan 2023 — used a field experiment with n=480,948 jobseekers; the paper reports "Treated jobseekers were hired 8% more often," and MIT Sloan's summary specifies +7.8% hires, 8.4% higher wages ($18.62/hr vs $17.17/hr control). But that tests writing *quality* (algorithmic writing assistance), not tailoring-to-role. Commercial "tailoring" numbers (ResumeGo "31%", Huntr "115%", TalentWorks 2.8×, Jobscan) are vendor/marketing, observational, methodologically opaque, and several unverifiable at primary source. Recruitment ≠ admissions; do not transfer.
- **B2 (what offices say):** Only **Sciences Po** explicitly demands tailoring, on its *unsuccessful-candidates* page: "The CV is essential… A CV must be tailored for its purpose and highlight the aspects of your experience that relate to your choice of Master's programme." **No admissions office was found stating tailoring makes no difference.** Other "tailor it" statements are university *careers services* (Edinburgh, Pittsburgh, CSU Fresno) and testing bodies (ETS: "each resume should be tailored to each program you're applying to") — institutional but advisory, and none cites evidence.
- **B3 (what is being tailored):** Four operations, in descending order of defensibility:
  1. **Reordering existing material** so relevant items appear first — supported by Sciences Po ("should not be a mere chronological listing") and careers-service guidance. Advisory, no outcome evidence.
  2. **Selecting which items appear** under space limits — supported by the same guidance; the master's-CV consensus is 1–2 pages. Advisory.
  3. **Rewriting descriptions in the programme's vocabulary** — coaching advice; the same family of claims as D4. Folklore; no admissions evidence.
  4. **Adding skills/claims not otherwise present** — **no published guidance recommends this, and it shades into the fabrication territory that IS explicitly policed** (Track B4). Treat as prohibited-by-implication.
- **B4 (risk side):** UK universities and UCAS operate explicit **admissions-fraud** regimes. Manchester reserves the right to cancel an application or withdraw an offer for information "fraudulent, untrue, inaccurate, incomplete and/or misleading." UCL: failure to satisfactorily explain apparent misrepresentation results in the application/offer being withdrawn, "no right of appeal," and post-enrolment discovery can mean de-registration. Bristol, Edinburgh and City have parallel policies; UCAS screens for "false, missing, or misleading information." **Consequence is binding.** No institution found draws a *written* distinction between legitimate emphasis and fabrication — the policies target falsity/omission, leaving "emphasis" implicitly permitted but undefined.

### TRACK C — Making an unfamiliar institution legible

**C1. What official guidance asks for.** The interpretive burden is carried by institutional machinery, not the CV:
- **Grading scale statement / class rank / percentile:** US graduate portals and credential evaluators use these; internationally, the transcript itself is expected to carry the scale. Applicant self-conversion is discouraged (College Essay Guy quotes an admissions office: "Translating grades yourself will often result in a mismatched evaluation"). Binding only where a specific portal field requires it.
- **ECTS / Bologna alignment / Diploma Supplement:** EHEA institutions issue the Diploma Supplement automatically and free; ECTS and the Diploma Supplement are the official transparency tools (ENIC-NARIC). Applicants from EHEA institutions supply the Supplement; those outside supply transcripts for evaluation.
- **National accreditation status:** assessed by the receiving institution or ENIC-NARIC, not asserted by the applicant.
- **Credential evaluation (WES, uni-assist VPD, ENIC-NARIC):** These are the binding mechanisms. uni-assist's VPD converts foreign grades to the German scale; WES course-by-course/ICAP is required by many US/Canadian graduate programmes (e.g., Boston University SPH via SOPHAS: "SOPHAS will only accept WES ICAP evaluations"). ENIC-NARIC comparability statements interpret degrees under the Lisbon Recognition Convention. **These are asked for by offices; the rest is applicant-side coaching.**

**C2. Institution website URL in the education entry.** The **Europass** schema does contain a *website* field on education entries, but no university, funder or admissions office was found recommending that applicants fill it to aid legibility. Filling it is *an available field, not expected practice*. Recommending the URL to make an unknown university legible is **coaching advice**, not primary-sourced.

**C3. Institutional rankings on the CV.** No university, funder or admissions office was found recommending (or prohibiting) stating your university's ranking in the education entry. Support for doing so is **coaching folklore only**. Given that offices evaluate institutions via ENIC-NARIC/credential evaluation, self-asserted rankings carry no official weight.

**C4. Coursework/module lists.** Careers-service guidance (Edinburgh, Fresno) advises listing relevant coursework/projects, especially for career-changers or thin research output. TU Delft-type prerequisite gating means listing prerequisite modules can be materially useful where admissibility depends on specific prior coursework. This is **advisory** (careers-service tier), with a defensible mechanism where prerequisites are explicit. It displaces stronger material only if it crowds out research/experience — a judgement call, not a rule.

### TRACK D — Skills sections

- **D1. Generic competencies ("critical thinking", "teamwork"):** No admissions-side evidence that listing them helps, and no evidence they are held against applicants. The master's-CV professional guidance (Across Careers, on-the-record ex-committee member for Carnegie Mellon's MS Computational Finance) stresses *evidenced* accomplishments over asserted traits. Honest verdict: on current evidence these are **inert** in admissions — neither demonstrated to help nor to hurt. Convention, not evidence.
- **D2. Evidenced vs declared skills:** Guidance (Across Careers; careers services) consistently favours skills demonstrable from a project/role/publication over bare assertion ("Strong bullets answer… What did you do? How? What was the outcome?"). No institution or funder found *explicitly* addresses unevidenced skill claims in a binding rule — except insofar as fabrication policy (Track B4) applies to false claims. Advisory.
- **D3. Self-rated proficiency ("advanced Python"):** No primary-source admissions guidance located on self-rating technical proficiency. CEFR for languages is the separate, standardised case (Europass uses CEFR A1–C2). Self-rating technical skills is coaching territory; null for admissions.
- **D4. Programme-specific vocabulary (e.g., describing work in "AI" terms for an AI programme):** This is the same claim as B3's third bullet. No primary-source support; **folklore**. It is defensible only as accurate description of genuinely relevant work; if it restates work in terms the work does not support, it approaches misrepresentation.

## Recommendations

**Stage 1 — Build the base (do first, always).** Produce one complete, accurate, gapless, tabular master CV, 1–2 pages, reverse-chronological, in the language of instruction. This satisfies the only widely binding content rules (ETH Zürich gaplessness; uni-assist "no omissions"; DAAD tabular ≤3 pages). Benchmark to change: if a target programme publishes a specific CV template or field list, follow it verbatim — programme instructions override generic format.

**Stage 2 — Make the institution legible through official channels, not the CV.** Secure the Diploma Supplement (EHEA) or a credential evaluation (uni-assist VPD for Germany; WES ICAP for US/Canada where required; ENIC-NARIC comparability statement where accepted). Put the grading scale on/with the transcript, not as CV editorialising. Do **not** rely on self-stated rankings or self-converted GPAs. Benchmark: if the portal has an explicit grade/rank field, complete it exactly as the transcript reads.

**Stage 3 — Tailor only by reordering and selecting, never by adding.** Where the programme (like Sciences Po) or its careers guidance invites tailoring, reorder and select existing, evidenced material so programme-relevant items lead; mirror the programme's vocabulary *only* where it accurately names work actually done. Do not add competencies not evidenced elsewhere. Benchmark that changes this: none — there is no evidence tailoring changes outcomes, so effort here should be capped and never allowed to introduce unverifiable claims.

**Stage 4 — Human counselor QA against the fraud line.** The counselor's highest-value check is not polish but verifiability: every claim must be corroborated by transcript, certificate, publication or reference. This is the one area with binding, enforced consequences (UK/UCAS fraud policies). Benchmark: any claim the applicant cannot document should be cut or downgraded to defensible wording.

## Caveats
- **Austria** and a granular UK master's rubric are the thinnest cells; the Austrian content rule is unverified in this run.
- The "time per file" and "ATS in admissions" answers are **nulls** — absence of published evidence, which for this product is itself the finding. Do not let downstream copy re-import US-undergraduate or recruitment figures.
- Sciences Po is a single institution; its tailoring requirement should not be generalised into a pan-European rule.
- Recruitment evidence (NBER 30886; vendor tailoring stats) is included only to show what does *not* transfer.
- The Kaplan "44% examine transcript first" figure circulating in coaching content is unverified; the verified Kaplan (2014) datapoint concerns the GRE as biggest "application killer" (41%).

## Artefact 2 — Findings note (empirical, non-prescriptive)

**Lead sentence: Track B's premise is unevidenced — no empirical study, audit experiment, or admissions-office outcome data was found showing that a CV tailored to a specific master's programme outperforms a strong generic one.**

**Track A2 (who reads, how long):** Master's review is committee/faculty/staff work (documented for Erasmus Mundus consortia and, by contrast, US graduate committees). There is **no published time-per-application figure for European master's admissions**. The famous 8-minute/two-reader/4-minute numbers are US-undergraduate consultancy claims and do not transfer; a US psychology graduate slide deck's "~15 minutes or less per folder" is the nearest analogue and is PhD-oriented. Evidence quality here: weak; the honest answer is that nobody has published this for European master's.

**Track A3 (automation):** ATS/keyword screening is a recruitment (and university *hiring*) practice. Recruitment-industry sources themselves debunk the "ATS auto-rejects most resumes" claim. No primary evidence puts ATS screening in university master's *admissions*; on current evidence the CV-builder assertion is false for this context.

**Track A4 (hyperlinks):** Link-following is documented only for recruiters. Null for admissions.

**Track B (tailoring):** One institutional policy source demands tailoring (Sciences Po). University careers services and ETS advise it without evidence. Kurysheva et al. (*Int. J. STEM Education*, 2023) find the CV is "not given substantial weight." No office says tailoring is useless; none provides outcome data either way. The most rigorous recruitment experiment (van Inwegen, Munyikwa & Horton, NBER 30886, 2023; n=480,948; "Treated jobseekers were hired 8% more often") tests writing *quality*, not tailoring-to-role, and is non-transferable; vendor tailoring stats are low-reliability. Of the four tailoring operations, only reordering and selecting have even advisory support; rewriting into programme vocabulary is folklore; adding unevidenced skills shades into policed misrepresentation.

**Evidence-quality verdict:**
- **Track A:** Rests on **published policy** for *what documents are required* (strong) but on **coaching folklore/US-undergraduate carryover** for *how files are read and timed* (weak). Weakest link: A2/A3 — no European master's data on review time or automation; do not import recruitment/undergraduate figures.
- **Track B:** Rests on **one published policy** (Sciences Po), **careers-service/testing-body advisory guidance**, and **coaching folklore**; the only *empirical* input (Kurysheva et al. 2023 review) bears on CV *weight*, not tailoring. Weakest link: the total absence of any admissions-specific study of tailoring — the core question is unmeasured.
- **Track C:** Rests on **published policy** (ENIC-NARIC, Diploma Supplement, WES, uni-assist VPD) for the institutional legibility mechanisms (strong), but the CV-side tactics (website URL, rankings) are **coaching folklore** with no primary support. Weakest link: C2/C3 — Europass has a website field but no source expects it filled, and rankings-on-CV has zero institutional backing.
- **Track D:** Rests almost entirely on **convention and coaching**, with one **on-the-record admissions professional** (Across Careers) favouring evidenced over asserted skills. No binding institutional rule on generic/self-rated skills except that false claims fall under fraud policy. Weakest link: D1/D3 — the "inert" verdict on generic competencies and the silence on self-rated proficiency are inferences from absence, not measured findings.
