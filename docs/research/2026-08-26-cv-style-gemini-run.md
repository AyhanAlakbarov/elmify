## **Deliverable 2: Companion Design Note**

### **Introduction: The Paradigm Shift in Graduate Admissions Design**

Developing an automated template system for international master's degree application CVs requires a fundamental conceptual pivot. The vast majority of commercially available guidance regarding resume construction is strictly oriented toward the corporate employment market. This corporate paradigm is defined by Applicant Tracking System (ATS) optimization, aggressive one-page length restrictions, heavy keyword stuffing, and visual elements designed to capture the attention of a human resources recruiter in a six-second scan1.  
However, academic admissions committees, principal investigators (PIs), and graduate scholarship reviewers operate under an entirely different evaluative framework. Graduate admissions personnel are not corporate recruiters, and their evaluative tools are not ATS parsers. They are active academics assessing the scholarly potential, methodological rigor, and intellectual maturity of prospective junior colleagues4. Consequently, importing corporate resume dogma—such as highly stylized graphical templates, skill-rating charts, or strict page caps—into an academic application actively harms the applicant's credibility. The academic curriculum vitae (CV) is characterized by an expansive, comprehensive record of scholarly achievements, unconstrained by artificial page limits and stripped of marketing-oriented visual embellishments4.  
Furthermore, standard United States undergraduate admissions advice, which frequently emphasizes well-rounded extracurricular involvement and narrative summaries, does not transfer to the master's level. At the graduate tier, the architectural hierarchy of the document must immediately foreground academic pedigree, research experience, methodological competencies, and publications4.  
The mandate for the template designer is to engineer a visual architecture that minimizes cognitive load for the evaluator while satisfying localized, regional conventions. It is critical to recognize that almost all guidance regarding visual style in academia is advisory rather than binding. Explicit, documented typographic rules from funding bodies or universities are exceptionally rare6. Therefore, the recommendations detailed in this report represent the optimal, evidence-backed visual strategies designed to project academic rigor and facilitate seamless cognitive processing when a receiving institution provides no mandatory formatting constraints.

### **Track A: Destination Conventions and Regional Visual Paradigms**

A generically "international" CV does not exist. Academic and visual norms vary significantly across geographical regions. An applicant submitting a deeply entrenched North American format to a German university will inadvertently signal a lack of cultural awareness. The template system must be capable of adapting to these distinct regional visual paradigms.

#### **The DACH Region (Germany, Austria, Switzerland): The Grid-Based Architecture**

The dominant visual structure for academic and professional applications in the DACH region is the *tabellarischer Lebenslauf* (tabular CV). German universities such as LMU Munich and Heidelberg specifically request this format for master's admissions8.

* **Visual Structure:** The *tabellarischer Lebenslauf* is not merely a descriptive term; it dictates a rigid, two-column visual grid. It explicitly rejects the North American convention of hanging indents and continuous horizontal flow. Instead, the document is bisected into a narrower left column and a wider right column.  
* **Label Placement:** The left column is exclusively reserved for category labels and corresponding dates. The right column houses the substantive descriptions, institutional names, and locations11. This creates a sharp, vertical dividing line down the center of the page.  
* **Chronological Direction:** Historically, German CVs were written in strict chronological order. However, modern guidance indicates that reverse-chronological ordering is now widely accepted and frequently preferred for academic positions, as it immediately surfaces the most relevant recent experience11.  
* **Oddities for the Local Reader:** A local DACH evaluator would find a CV utilizing centered headers, prose paragraphs, or misaligned columns highly disorganized. The tabular alignment must be mathematically precise, utilizing dark, highly readable text11.

#### **France and Western Europe (The Netherlands, Nordics): The Typographic Gap**

In France, both the highly selective *grandes écoles* and the public university systems (such as Sorbonne Université and Université Paris Cité) maintain extensive, rigid requirements for the *content* of an application dossier, but exhibit a near-total absence of published guidance regarding the *visual layout* of the master's CV13.

* **Length Constraints:** While the North American academic CV is generally uncapped, certain French graduate school programs and early-career funding transitions (e.g., the G.E.N.E. Graduate School mentor program) occasionally mandate a strict "1 page max" limit for the CV17.  
* **Visual Expectations:** Because explicit typographic rules represent a documented gap in the literature, the designer must rely on universal principles of cognitive ease. French reviewers expect a dense but highly organized document.  
* **The Netherlands and the Nordics:** Similar to the French system, leading institutions such as the University of Amsterdam, Utrecht University, KTH Royal Institute of Technology, and the University of Copenhagen mandate the inclusion of a CV but offer absolutely no published guidelines on its visual layout or typographic structure19. To align with broader Scandinavian and Dutch design sensibilities, a clean, unadorned, and minimalist design, devoid of graphic clutter, is strongly advised.

#### **The United Kingdom and Ireland: The Rise of the Narrative**

The academic CV in the UK and Ireland has traditionally mirrored the North American model—a reverse-chronological structure utilizing bullet points, with lengths extending to two pages or more, and a strong preference for left-aligned roles and right-aligned dates23. However, the template designer must account for a significant, systemic divergence currently underway in this region.

* **The Narrative CV (R4RI):** Driven by UK Research and Innovation (UKRI) and the Royal Society, the "Résumé for Research and Innovation" (R4RI) is rapidly becoming the binding standard for research funding and early-career academic applications across the UK and Ireland25.  
* **Visual Implications:** If a master's applicant is required to submit a Narrative CV for a specific funding scheme, the visual layout fundamentally changes. Bulleted lists of publications and experiences are entirely discarded. Instead, the document is structured into four distinct modules (e.g., "Contributions to the generation of new ideas," "Contributions to the wider research community"), which are answered using continuous prose paragraphs28.  
* **Design Requirement:** The template system must provide a distinct layout option that supports highly readable, multi-line prose. This requires careful attention to line lengths (ideally 45-75 characters per line) and increased line spacing to prevent visual fatigue, as opposed to the tight, vertical scanning optimized for bullet points.

#### **North America (US and Canada): The Uncapped Ledger**

The most critical structural distinction in the US and Canada is the rigid separation between a corporate "resume" and an "academic CV." A corporate resume is restricted to one or two pages. An academic CV for graduate admissions is treated as a comprehensive ledger of scholarly activity and explicitly possesses **no page limit**4.

* **Visual Layout:** Leading institutions such as the University of Pennsylvania, Harvard GSAS, and the University of Toronto strongly advise placing the most critical hierarchical information (Name of Institution, Degree, Role) flush against the left margin, while shifting associated dates to the far right margin4. Placing dates on the left margin is explicitly discouraged, as it visually forces the evaluator to focus on the chronological timing of an event rather than the academic achievement itself4.  
* **Hierarchy:** For master's level applicants, the sections for "Education" and "Research Experience" must be elevated to the top of the first page, immediately following the contact block, entirely superseding general employment history4.

#### **Major Non-Western Destinations (Japan, Türkiye, Azerbaijan)**

* **Japan:** Japanese corporate applications rely heavily on the *Rirekisho*, a highly standardized, legally binding, grid-based form that is often handwritten31. However, for international applicants seeking admission to master's programs at institutions like the University of Tokyo or Tokyo Institute of Technology, this requirement is waived. Universities generally accept a "free format" CV32. The primary requirement for the template is not visual, but procedural: applicants must rigorously adhere to requested file naming conventions (e.g., 1\_\[YourName\]\_CV.pdf) and, if applicable, provide their name in both the Roman alphabet and Katakana32.  
* **Türkiye & Azerbaijan:** Universities such as Middle East Technical University (METU) in Türkiye and ADA University in Azerbaijan mandate the submission of CVs and Motivation Letters, but do not publish visual formatting constraints35. A standard, conservative Western academic layout is universally accepted and expected.

### **Track B: The Reader’s Side—Cognitive Load and Evaluative Patterns**

To engineer a highly effective CV template, the designer must understand the physiological and cognitive mechanisms of the academic evaluator. Admissions committees process hundreds of applications per cycle, leading to severe visual fatigue. The template must be designed to accommodate heuristic scanning.

#### **B1. Stated Preferences of Admissions Committees**

When graduate admissions offices do publish guidance on CV appearance, their demands uniformly prioritize readability and cognitive ease over creativity or aesthetic flair. The University of Oxford’s Careers Service instructs applicants to ensure the document is "easy to read and scan quickly; clear font of 10 pt or 11 pt; some blank spaces; not too narrow a margin," explicitly noting that dense, unbroken text dilutes the impact of the applicant's achievements23. Similarly, the University of Colorado Boulder advises a "reasonable font type and size, consistent throughout," advocating for explicit use of single spacing with clear blank lines separating headings38. Texas Tech University echoes this, arguing that adequate white space allows "the reader's eye to rest"6. The overarching mandate from evaluators is uncompromised clarity, conciseness, and absolute formatting consistency6.

#### **B2. The Physiology of Evaluation: Eye-Tracking and ERP Research**

While academic admissions committees are not corporate recruiters, human visual processing mechanisms remain identical across domains. Extensive eye-tracking research provides invaluable, quantitative insights into how CV documents are parsed:

* **The F-Pattern:** Foundational eye-tracking studies demonstrate that evaluators scan documents in a distinct "F-pattern." The reader's eyes move horizontally across the top of the document, then vertically down the left margin, making increasingly shorter horizontal scans to the right as they move down the page2.  
* **Design Implications of the F-Pattern:** Because the right side and bottom of the page receive exponentially less visual attention, the template must front-load critical identifying information (Name, Current Institution, Degree) on the top and flush against the left margin2. Centered elements or critical data buried on the right side of the page are frequently skipped entirely during the initial rapid review2.  
* **Cognitive Load and Fixation Variability:** Studies published in MDPI on visual attention variability utilizing Areas of Interest (AOI) metrics indicate that complex layouts increase the coefficient of variation (CV) of fixation duration39. In practical terms, a convoluted layout forces the evaluator to spend precious cognitive energy deciphering the architecture of the document rather than assessing the applicant's underlying academic merit39.  
* **Neurophysiological Bias (ERPs):** Event-related potentials (ERPs) research examining resume evaluation indicates that reviewers dynamically allocate attention based on perceived academic prestige. Evaluators exhibit heightened early attentional resources (captured via P200 and N300 amplitude modulation) when processing candidates from elite institutions41. Consequently, the visual hierarchy of the template must ensure that the names of previous academic institutions are immediately visible, isolated, and prominent, rather than buried within prose descriptions.

#### **B3. Markers of "Template-Generated" or Careless Design**

Academic readers possess a strong, culturally ingrained aversion to visual gimmicks borrowed from the corporate tech sector or graphic design portfolios. The template designer must strictly prohibit the following elements to preserve the applicant's intellectual authority:

* **Visual Skill Rating Systems:** The use of graphical progress bars, star ratings, or dot matrices to represent language proficiency or technical software skills is highly stigmatized in academia42. These visual elements are viewed as inherently subjective, statistically unverifiable, and intellectually unserious43.  
* **Visual Clutter and Iconography:** The inclusion of icons (e.g., a telephone icon next to the phone number, or a mail icon next to the email address), infographics, or photographs (unless legally mandated by regional anti-discrimination or identification laws, such as in certain European contexts) introduces visual noise1. Furthermore, these elements frequently cause catastrophic parsing errors if the application is ingested by institutional software systems1.  
* **Typographic Carelessness:** Cramped leading (line spacing), widowed pages (a single line of text carrying over to a new page), and inconsistent alignment signal a lack of attention to detail and a failure to understand the norms of academic publishing6.

### **Track C: Element-Level Conventions for the Template Architecture**

The granular typographic and structural decisions made by the designer directly dictate the perceived professional authority of the document. The following rules represent the dominant, evidence-backed conventions for constructing the micro-elements of an academic CV.

#### **C1. Section Headers**

Section headers serve as the primary navigational anchors for the evaluator navigating the F-pattern.

* **Alignment:** Headers should be uniformly left-aligned to capture the reader's vertical gaze path2.  
* **Typography:** Headers must be visually distinct from the body text. Standard academic practice involves utilizing ALL CAPS, boldface, or scaling the font size 2pt larger than the surrounding body text7.  
* **Rules and Borders:** A subtle horizontal rule (a solid bottom border extending across the page) situated directly under a section header is a highly effective, non-distracting method to delineate sections and organize white space.  
* **Underlining:** The template must never utilize text underlining for emphasis. Underlining cuts through the descenders of letters (such as 'p', 'q', 'y'), harming baseline readability and introducing visual clutter7.

#### **C2. Entry Layout: Dates and Descriptions**

* **Date Placement:** For North American, UK, and general international academic CVs, all dates should be pushed to a right-aligned column or placed inline at the end of the header4. Placing dates on the left-hand margin forces the reader's eye to fixate on the chronology of events rather than the prestige of the institution or the nature of the research4. (The strict exception is the DACH *tabellarischer Lebenslauf*, where dates form the left column11).  
* **Bullets vs. Prose:** For standard academic CVs, bullet points are universally preferred over dense paragraphs of prose23. Bullet points optimize vertical scanning and allow for the rapid extraction of data. Furthermore, sentences within these bullets should utilize active verbs and omit personal pronouns (e.g., writing "Analyzed 300+ archival documents" rather than "I analyzed...")7.

#### **C3. The Name and Contact Block**

* **Name Typography:** The applicant's name must be the largest element on the page, typically scaled between 14pt and 18pt, and rendered in boldface to establish the top of the visual hierarchy1.  
* **The ORCID iD:** While corporate resumes rely on LinkedIn profiles, the academic CV increasingly requires the inclusion of an **ORCID iD** (Open Researcher and Contributor Identifier). The ORCID iD is a 16-digit unique digital identifier that disambiguates authors and acts as a persistent electronic CV49. It should be placed prominently in the header or contact block50.  
* **Hyperlinks:** Hyperlinks within the generated PDF must be functional. The URL text itself should be clean and readable (e.g., orcid.org/0000-0000-0000-0000) rather than embedding the link into generic anchor text, ensuring the link is accessible even if the document is printed50.

#### **C4. Publication Entries**

Master's applicants may have limited publication histories, but any entries present must be formatted with absolute academic rigor.

* **Citation Consistency:** The specific citation style chosen (APA, MLA, Chicago, Vancouver) is irrelevant to the committee; what matters is that a single style is maintained flawlessly throughout the entire document28.  
* **Bolding the Applicant's Name:** It is a universal, highly expected academic convention to **bold the applicant's name** within the author list of any publication28. This allows the evaluation committee to instantly verify the applicant's author order and first-author status without having to hunt through dense blocks of citation text.  
* **Categorization:** Published, peer-reviewed works must be strictly separated into a distinct category from works that are merely "in preparation," "under review," or uploaded to preprint servers. Blurring these categories is viewed as academically dishonest24.

#### **C5. Language Skills**

As established in Track B, visual skill bars are unacceptable. Instead, language proficiency should be represented using globally recognized, standardized frameworks.

* **The CEFR Framework:** Outside of the United States, the Common European Framework of Reference for Languages (CEFR) is the definitive gold standard for categorizing language proficiency (ranging from A1 for beginners to C2 for mastery)53.  
* **Layout:** Languages should be listed in a simple, unadorned text format. If standardized test scores are highly relevant to the application (e.g., TOEFL, IELTS, Cambridge), they should be listed plainly adjacent to the corresponding language52.

#### **C6. Typography and Whitespace**

The typographic foundation of the template determines its ultimate readability and the perceived professionalism of the applicant.

* **Font Families:** The template must utilize conservative, highly legible fonts. Standard serif options (Times New Roman, Garamond, Georgia) effectively signal traditional academic authority, while clean sans-serif options (Arial, Calibri, Helvetica) offer exceptional on-screen legibility1. The choice between serif and sans-serif is ultimately a matter of aesthetic taste; there is no published institutional rule favoring one over the other, provided the font belongs to these standard families6.  
* **Font Size:** Body text must strictly remain between 10pt and 12pt7. Text smaller than 10pt causes severe eye strain for evaluators reading on screens, while text larger than 12pt appears juvenile and wastes valuable space.  
* **Margins:** Margins should be equal on all four sides, ranging from 0.75 inches to a maximum of 1.0 inch6. This provides the necessary white space to prevent the document from appearing overly dense.  
* **File Format:** The final generated output must invariably be a PDF. Submitting a Word document risks severe formatting corruption, font substitution, and layout collapse across different operating systems and software versions, which reflects poorly on the applicant's technical competence and attention to detail6.

### **Conclusion for the Template Architect**

The successful master's application CV template is an exercise in rigorous restraint. The template designer must actively resist the urge to innovate visually. In the academic sphere, graphic embellishments, novel layouts, and corporate marketing aesthetics are perceived as distractions that attempt to mask a lack of genuine scholarly substance.  
Given the global nature of the product, the underlying architecture must be modular. The default state should rely on a left-justified, right-dated structure to satisfy dominant North American and UK conventions. However, the system must offer a strict two-column toggle to generate a compliant *tabellarischer Lebenslauf* for DACH institutions, and a prose-friendly block layout for UK Narrative CVs. By prioritizing cognitive ease, enforcing strict typographic hierarchy, and integrating expected academic conventions (such as ORCID inclusion and the bolding of authorship), the designer provides the international applicant with an invisible but highly potent advantage in the competitive graduate admissions landscape.
