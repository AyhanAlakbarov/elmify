# Three remaining gaps

Not a research run: three gaps named in earlier register documents and not acted on, one of which breaks an assumption the screen is built on. [src: 2026-08-31-three-remaining-gaps.md] Follows [register questions closed](register-open-questions-closed.md) and [open admission](open-admission.md). Pages: [Türkiye](../countries/tr.md), [Germany](../countries/de.md), [United Kingdom](../countries/gb.md).

- TEQSA's register counts 144 of 219 Australian higher education providers as not self-accrediting, so degree-awarding status is answerable in Australia; it also lists three unregistered entities. [src: 2026-08-31-three-remaining-gaps.md §1]
- The UK's OfS register covers degree-awarding powers per provider, but a bulk download is unconfirmed. [src: 2026-08-31-three-remaining-gaps.md §1]
- YÖK keeps a list of 41 institutions whose graduates are refused equivalence, and it names institutes, centres and chairs inside otherwise reputable universities, so a screen keyed to institutions cannot express it. [src: 2026-08-31-three-remaining-gaps.md §2]
- The list and its sub-units come only from Turkish news and consultancy sites; the primary YÖK source was not reached, so none may be encoded. [src: 2026-08-31-three-remaining-gaps.md §2]
- Norway is not a second tuition-free destination: the official portal says non-EEA degree students must normally pay fees, and its study-permit requirement of NOK 170,368 a year exceeds Germany's €11,904 blocked account. [src: 2026-08-31-three-remaining-gaps.md §3]
- Consequences: record self-accrediting status only where a regulator publishes it, design a sub-institutional exclusion record now, and treat Germany as the only free floor. [src: 2026-08-31-three-remaining-gaps.md §4]
- Not established: the YÖK list from a primary source, the OfS CSV, other markets with sub-institutional exclusions, and Malaysia's MQA. [src: 2026-08-31-three-remaining-gaps.md §5]
