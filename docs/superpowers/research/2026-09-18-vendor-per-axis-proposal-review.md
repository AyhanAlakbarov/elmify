# The "one vendor per axis" search proposal, checked — 2026-09-18

Before the funding-reader holder-axis work began, the owner brought an AI-written architecture for
the discovery engine: Diffbot for holders and families, Exa for institution-side kinds, Brave for
government and bilateral, Tavily to answer questions that fill the cross-cutting labels
(obligation, coverage, route, eligibility), Crawl4AI / Firecrawl / Scrapling plus a JSON-schema LLM
for extraction, and Google Programmable Search over curated university domains. The ask: is our
design the best possible — investigate it, do not defer to it.

**Method.** Each claim against (a) the repo's own measurements —
`2026-09-17-search-tool-probe.md`, `2026-09-17-search-capability-ablation.md`,
`2026-09-17-cell-saturation-measurement.md`, `2026-09-17-diffbot-perplexity-recon.md`,
`2026-09-18-tool-capability-oversights.md`, the roster in the discovery design §4.13 and the yield
measurement in §4.13c — and (b) the vendors' **current** documentation, fetched 2026-09-18 (the
facts and URLs are listed at the end). No provider call was spent; nothing was stored.

## Verdict

**The proposal changes nothing about the architecture.** Every claim in it is one of three things:
already adopted here in a *measured* form; contradicted by a measurement in this repo; or
contradicted by the vendor's own documentation. It omits the five parts of the design the results
actually come from. And it does not touch the measured bottleneck: discovery already reaches
funders — **the reader served 2 awards in 58 funder hosts** (§4.13c) because it had no word for a
funder's own award, which is what the holder-axis plan fixes.

What the review *did* produce: two technique-layer knobs owed a measurement before adoption, two
dropped with their reasons, and one real gap the owner caught in the build plan (Baidu and Naver,
below).

## What the proposal never mentions — and is where the results come from

1. **Registries first.** Wikidata, DEQAR, ROR, EACEA, VGM (CC0 / PDDL / open notice) are the
   denominator and the spine; search pays only for the set-difference (§4.1–4.2). The holder axis
   is enumerable there by class × country at no cost.
2. **Local-language queries and depth** — the only two capabilities that measurably win (local
   beat English on local funders in all four countries; Brave 3 → 10 pages +47/+68/+27/+104%;
   Exa 25 → 100 results ×3.4/2.0/1.7/2.2).
3. **The technique layer** — aggregator denylist, Goggle boost, *no* provider filters: +18 to +27
   points on three providers, vendor-neutral.
4. **Provenance from the origin, nothing persisted from a provider, profile-free queries with the
   per-provider allow-list** (§4.6, §8, funder spec D.6).
5. **Exa does not decay; Brave and Tavily do** (147 / 100 / 84 first-discoverers; Exa flat across
   nine batches while Brave fell 81 → 11) — so a sweep drops engines by tally, never by axis.

## The gap the owner caught: Baidu and Naver

The roster adopted **Baidu** (China) and **Naver** (Korea) on 2026-09-17 — ablation §7: Baidu
+7 Chinese institutions from three searches at 76% precision, the highest measured anywhere in that
exercise; Naver +5 Korean funders including two foundations — and the register plan that builds
the adapters (`2026-09-18-organisation-register-and-provider-protocol.md`, Tasks 6–8) never
included them. The roster also contradicted itself: the SerpApi row said "no host may enter the
catalog because SerpApi pointed at it" while the Baidu and Naver rows were catalog sources *via
SerpApi*. Both are fixed today: the rule is narrowed to **Google via SerpApi** (`R4_SEARCH:google`
never written), and Baidu and Naver write `R4_SEARCH:baidu` / `:naver` — the engine — with the
reseller recorded once in the spec's §8 terms table.

**Owner decision 2026-09-18: build both through SerpApi on its free tier (250 searches a month),
now.** The position is stated plainly because it is the one the roster ruled out for Google:
SerpApi resells indexes it has no rights to grant, and its U.S. Legal Shield ("up to $2 million in
coverage for the scraping and parsing of search engine data, as long as your use of the data or
service is not illegal") applies **only from the Production plan, $150/month** — verified on
`serpapi.com/pricing` today; Free, Starter and Developer carry none. The owner accepted that with
the concern raised. Two remedies stay open: **Naver's own developer API**, a direct contract and the
route the ablation said to prefer (`developers.naver.com` refused this session's fetch, so its terms
and daily limit are read on the cited host before it is preferred); and the Production tier if
volume or exposure grows.

**What keeps this general.** A regional engine is added for a country only where the measured
rule holds — the national web is **large** *and* **partly outside the global indexes' reach**
(policy-separated like China, platform-concentrated like Korea) — never because a country has a
popular engine. Measured out: Yandex for Azerbaijan (0 new), Seznam, Coc Coc, Google via SerpApi
(+2 / +1). Adding a country is one row in `REGIONAL_ENGINES` and, when the engine's parameter names
differ, one row in the adapter's engine table. Task 11 of the register plan builds it.

## Vendor facts fetched 2026-09-18

- **Exa `/search`** (`exa.ai/docs/reference/search`): `includeDomains` — "Each entry can be a
  hostname (for example, `example.com`), a hostname with a path prefix (for example,
  `example.com/docs`), or a wildcard subdomain (for example, `*.example.com`)"; `type` ∈
  `instant, fast, auto, deep-lite, deep, deep-reasoning`; no `includeText`/`excludeText` in the
  schema; no query-string operators documented; every response carries `costDollars.total`.
- **Brave operators** (`search.brave.com/help/operators`): `AND`, `OR`, `NOT` as "logical
  operators"; `+`, `-`, `""`, `site:`, `intitle:`, `inbody:`, `inpage:`, `ext:`/`filetype:`,
  `lang:`, `loc:`; the `site:` example is `site:brave.com` and no wildcard form is documented.
- **Tavily search** (`docs.tavily.com/documentation/api-reference/endpoint/search`):
  `include_answer` — "A short answer to the user's query, generated by an LLM" — `true`/`basic` or
  `advanced`, no extra credit; `include_domains` ≤ 300 with `restrict` / `prefer` modes;
  `exclude_domains` ≤ 150; `basic`/`fast`/`ultra-fast` 1 credit, `advanced` 2.
- **Diffbot Organization ontology** (`diffbot.com/docs/ontology/organization`): `categories`
  hierarchical business categories; `descriptors` "a list of free-form keywords"; `isNonProfit`,
  `isPublic` booleans; `naicsClassification` / `sicClassification`; no field for a nonprofit's
  subtype, religion, politics, profession or government status.
- **Google Custom Search JSON API** (`developers.google.com/custom-search/v1/overview`): "The
  Custom Search JSON API is closed to new customers. Vertex AI Search is a favorable alternative
  for searching up to 50 domains"; existing customers have until 1 January 2027; 100 queries/day
  free, $5 per 1,000 after.
- **SerpApi pricing** (`serpapi.com/pricing`): Free 250/month; Starter $25 / 1,000; Developer $75
  / 5,000; Production $150 / 15,000; Big Data $275 / 30,000 — U.S. Legal Shield from Production up.
- **SerpApi Baidu** (`serpapi.com/baidu-search-api`): `GET https://serpapi.com/search`,
  `engine=baidu`, query in `q`, `pn` offset, `rn` ≤ 50 per page; results `organic_results[].link`.
  **SerpApi Naver** (`serpapi.com/naver-search-api`): `engine=naver`, query in `query`,
  `where=web`, `start` / `page`; results `web_results[].link`.
- **Naver developer API** (`developers.naver.com`): the host refused this session's fetch —
  **unverified**; read on the cited host before it is preferred.

## Addendum, same evening — "can we use Google's index? we build no competing index"

The owner's premise is the *Exa/Brave* condition (no competing-search training), and it is met.
It is not what keeps Google out. Three doors exist to Google's index and each is shut on its own
terms, read 2026-09-18:

- **Custom Search JSON API** — "closed to new customers", service ends 1 Jan 2027 (above).
- **Gemini API "Grounding with Google Search"** (`ai.google.dev/gemini-api/terms`, section
  *Grounding with Google Search*): "You will not, and will not allow your end user or any third
  party to, cache, frame, syndicate, resell, analyze, train on, or otherwise learn from Grounded
  Results or Search Suggestions" — and, naming our pipeline exactly: "it is a violation of these
  terms to use Grounding with Google Search to extract or collect one or more of these components
  for another purpose (for example, using programmatic or automated means to collect Links, using
  Links to build an index, or using Links to identify destination pages for crawling or
  scraping)." Taking a URL and fetching the origin *is* "using Links to identify destination pages
  for crawling". This door is closed by its wording, not by the competing-index question.
- **Google via SerpApi** — scraping, the subject of Google's own suit against SerpApi (the 2026-08-30
  reviews cite SerpApi's blog on it); no rights to grant; and **measured lift ≈ 0**: +2 in China,
  +1 in Korea, +1 in Azerbaijan over Brave + Exa (ablation §6–7). It stays the transient yardstick.

So: not used, for want of a lawful door and of any measured value — not because of the index rule.

## The owner's case, same evening — ChEMoinformatics+ and the intake without scholarship

Traced in `funding_judge.awards` on the reader branch: negation lines are counted and dropped, so a
page carrying both "about 20 Erasmus Mundus scholarships" and a dated "the 2026-2028 intake will be
without scholarship" **would be served as funded today**, and a re-read that finds no YES leaves the
row served. Fixed in design as funder spec §3.5 (offered is a fact about an award *for an intake*;
negations join their award; a dated or intake-scoped NO wins for that intake and is served as a
warning; re-reads may withdraw; the EMJM register asserts `I5` only inside the grant period) and as
reader plan Task 8. The owner then made the requirement explicit: **not this page — every served award
must say whether it is open for the student's intake.** Then, brainstormed to a decision: **no counsellor in
this loop; the system verifies by itself and a student sees only awards `VERIFIED` for their intake**
(states `OPEN / UPCOMING / CLOSED_FOR_INTAKE / SUSPENDED / ENDED / UNVERIFIED`, a pure function over
dated evidence from the organisation's own pages or the one application page they link to). The owner
then caught that "open now" alone would hide a March–May scheme from a student planning in September:
**`UPCOMING`** — verified to recur from two observed cycles or an "every year" statement, shown with
the projected window as a dated roadmap item, re-verified weekly before the projected opening,
degraded if no call appears within 45 days of it. Evidence comes up a ladder —
own pages, Firecrawl site crawl on a cycle vocabulary, one hop, Exa search on the domain — each step
only when the previous found nothing decisive; every verification records its own `cost_usd`
(estimate: $0.15–0.40 per scholarship per cycle where the ladder runs, ~$0 where the page states its
own call, ~$0.05 once the cheap first read passes); proof is a 40-organisation truth set plus Wayback
historical cases, gate ≥ 95% precision on `VERIFIED`. Funder spec §3.5; reader plan Tasks 8–10.
