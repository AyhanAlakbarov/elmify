# **Graduate Admissions Data and Enrollment Prepayment Regulations in East Asia**

## **Section 1: Admission and Award Data Publication**

The evaluation of admissions transparency across East Asian higher education systems reveals a systemic preference for publishing aggregated enrollment volumes and broad policy targets rather than granular, nationality-specific applicant data. The overarching data environment indicates that while some outcomes are published, mathematical selection rates for international candidates are largely unobtainable due to missing baseline application figures.  
**1a. International Applicant and Admitted Counts at Universities** An exhaustive review of institutional practices confirms that essentially none of the universities in Japan, South Korea, or China break out international applicant and admitted counts for master's programmes separately from their domestic figures. While institutions do publish domestic competition indicators (倍率 in Japan, 경쟁률 in South Korea, 报录比 in China), these metrics remain highly aggregated. Official university admissions portals in these jurisdictions do not isolate application flows or admission thresholds by foreign applicant nationality.  
**1b. Major Scholarship Schemes** The publication patterns of the primary government-funded scholarship schemes in these three countries strictly provide either raw award numerators or abstract policy ratios, consistently omitting the application denominators required to compute a true selection rate.

* **MEXT (Japan):** The Ministry of Education, Culture, Sports, Science and Technology does not publish raw applicant-to-award counts by nationality. Instead, it publishes outright stated selection rates (倍率) as historical aggregates or policy objectives in internal evaluations. For instance, a 2026 policy document (https://www.mext.go.jp/content/20260825-ope\_dev02-000051779-12.pdf) states outright that the selection rate for programs applied through overseas diplomatic missions exceeds 20 times (20倍を超える)1. Other documentation establishes that the general selection rate for master's programs is roughly 2 to 3 times (修士課程においては概ね2～3倍)2, with an overall policy goal of maintaining a highly competitive rate of 10 times (競争倍率１０倍) for targeted cohorts3.  
* **GKS / Global Korea Scholarship (South Korea):** The National Institute for International Education (NIIED) publishes forward-looking award quotas and final lists of successful candidates, but applications are not published. For the 2026 graduate degree programme, the published quota is 2,000 international students4. Final selection lists are published by candidate number and nationality for a given year (e.g., 2026 results at https://www.studyinkorea.go.kr/ko/notice/scholarshipsRead.do?nttId=4509\&bbsId=BBSMSTR\_000000000461)5. Because awards are published but applications are not, a selection rate cannot be computed from this numerator alone.  
* **CSC / Chinese Government Scholarship (China):** The Chinese Ministry of Education publishes retrospective, aggregate counts of currently enrolled scholarship students rather than annual new awards or application counts. The Ministry's statistics (http://www.moe.gov.cn/jyb\_xwfb/gzdt\_gzdt/s5987/201904/t20190412\_377692.html) report 63,041 total CSC students enrolled in 2018 (representing 12.81% of all international students)6, up from 49,022 in 20167. Applicant counts are not published, precluding the calculation of a selection rate.

**1c. National Statistical Datasets** While robust national statistical datasets exist, they strictly represent *statistical* enrollment series rather than *admissions-competition* series. None of these datasets publish graduate admissions or rejection rates by applicant nationality.

* **Japan (e-Stat):** The School Basic Survey (https://www.e-stat.go.jp/surveyitems/items/141010438) tracks "留学生以外の外国人学生数" (foreign students other than standard international students), providing census-style enrollment statistics8.  
* **South Korea (KEDI / 대학알리미):** The Higher Education in Korea platform (https://m.academyinfo.go.kr/) tracks overall foreign student counts (외국인 학생 수) and aggregated new student competition rates (신입생 경쟁률)9. This is an institutional statistical series, not an admissions-competition dataset cross-tabulated by nationality.  
* **China (MOE):** Ministry of Education releases such as the "2016 Statistics of International Students in China" (http://www.moe.gov.cn/jyb\_xwfb/xw\_fbh/moe\_2069/xwfbh\_2017n/xwfb\_170301/170301\_sjtj/201703/t20170301\_297677.html) track total macro-level enrollment metrics across the country7.

**What Remains Unknown (Section 1\)** The exact denominator (number of applications) submitted by international candidates for master's programs and major government scholarships remains wholly unpublished across Japan, South Korea, and China. Consequently, exact mathematical selection rates by nationality cannot be computed.

## **Section 2: Adjudication of Financial Commitments Prior to Enrollment in South Korea**

**Adjudication of the Contradiction** The two prior research runs reflect a conflation of legally distinct financial obligations. Both statements are factually anchored but address different mechanisms. The claim that the entrance fee (입학금) is being phased out is legally correct but applies strictly to domestic and undergraduate students, exempting graduate programs. Conversely, the claim that a full first semester's tuition must be prepaid to secure a visa document is the authoritative and legally binding operational reality for international students. Source (b) is the authoritative governing mechanism for international enrollment.  
**2a. National Rule Governing Prepayment** The requirement to prepay tuition prior to receiving a Certificate of Admission (표준입학허가서) is mandated by a national Ministry of Education regulation, the "Standard Work Processing Guidelines for International Students and Language Trainees" (외국인 유학생 및 어학연수생 표준업무처리요령) (https://www.moe.go.kr/boardCnts/viewRenew.do?boardID=350\&boardSeq=105733\&lev=0\&searchType=null\&statusYN=W\&page=1\&s=moe\&m=0309\&opType=N)11. This directive explicitly prohibits the issuance of visa documents without full tuition payment, stating: "유학비자 발급신청에 필요한 표준입학허가서는 합격 후 등록금을 완납한 자에게만 발급되며..." (The standard certificate of admission required for study visa application is issued only to those who have paid the tuition fee in full after acceptance)12. It further reinforces this sequence with the rule: "표준입학허가서는 등록금 납부 완료 후 발급 가능" (The certificate of admission can be issued after tuition payment is completed)13.  
**2b. University Practices and Publications** Major South Korean universities uniformly enforce this national mandate, sequencing the tuition prepayment before the issuance of the Certificate of Admission, while providing standardized refund protocols in the event of visa denial or pre-semester withdrawal. No single university's specific tuition amount serves as a national norm.

**2c. Current Status of 입학금 (Entrance Fee)** The entrance fee (입학금) has been subject to a phase-out policy, but this policy is partial and does not protect graduate applicants. Following amendments to the Higher Education Act (고등교육법) Article 11 (https://www.law.go.kr/LSW//lsLawLinkInfo.do?chrClsCd=010202\&lsJoLnkSeq=1000448168\&lsId=000899\&print=print)25, the abolition of university entrance fees was completed by 202327. However, the legislation explicitly exempts graduate schools from this ban (제29조의3에 따라 통합된 학위과정에 입학 또는 편입학하는 사람은 제외 한다)28. Consequently, international master's applicants will still routinely encounter a mandatory entrance fee alongside their tuition bill.  
**2d. Post-Payment Scholarship Treatment** While admission guides explicitly note that university-awarded scholarships granted at the time of admission are immediately deducted from the generated tuition invoice22, the subsequent administrative treatment of a student who is later awarded an external scholarship covering tuition *after* the mandatory prepayment has been made is **not published** in any provided source.  
**What Remains Unknown (Section 2\)** The internal administrative mechanics of how a university processes a retroactive external scholarship awarded after the mandatory tuition prepayment is completed is not published in standard admission guides.  
*Encoding Adjudication:* The requirement to prepay a full first semester's tuition (등록금) to secure a Certificate of Admission (표준입학허가서) is an authoritative national rule and can be encoded as a mandatory structural reality for all international D-2 visa applicants in South Korea. However, the exact financial amount required—which will include a retained graduate entrance fee minus any upfront institutional scholarships—must be verified per university.
