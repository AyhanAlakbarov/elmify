# Nationality-keyed master's funding — synthesis across four runs

**Date:** 2026-08-29. **Inputs:** four independent runs —
`2026-08-29-nationality-keyed-schemes-review-{1,2}.md` (the open search) and
`2026-08-29-nationality-keyed-gapfill-review-{1,2}.md` (the derived-enumeration pass).

**What it answers:** whether a nationality-keyed axis pays for the launch markets, and which schemes
a student from Azerbaijan or Türkiye can actually initiate.

---

## 1. The answer to the question the whole run existed to ask

**Yes, the nationality-keyed axis pays, and it pays most where the catalogue is blindest.** Four
schemes are now confirmed `DIRECT` or effectively self-initiated and full-cost for our launch
markets, none of which any programme census would surface:

| Scheme | For | Route | Cost |
|---|---|---|---|
| **ADB–Japan Scholarship Program** | 8 of 9 priority countries — **not Türkiye** | Apply to a designated institution; ADB approves | Full |
| **TEV-Garring/DTU**, **Chevening-TEV**, **TEV-UK Imperial/Cambridge** | Türkiye | `DIRECT` (Cambridge nominated) | Full |
| **Jean Monnet Scholarship** | Türkiye | `DIRECT`, EU-funded | €700–€22,000 |
| **IsDB** | 7 of 9 — not Georgia, Armenia | `DIRECT` | Full (Qard Hasan on some tracks) |

Before these runs, IsDB was the only known `DIRECT` full-cost route for any launch-market student,
and Türkiye had none at all. Türkiye now has four.

## 2. The contested absence is overturned — and then narrowed

**ADB-JSP is open to eight of the nine priority countries.** Both gap-fill runs independently
confirm it from ADB's own pages: Armenia, Azerbaijan, Georgia, Kazakhstan, Kyrgyz Republic,
Tajikistan, Turkmenistan and Uzbekistan appear on the citizenship list; **Türkiye does not**, because
it is not an ADB borrowing member. The earlier conclusion rested on a partnership report about a
climate-financing tranche — a different instrument — and was wrong.

**But run 2 found conditions run 1 omitted entirely, and they change who can use it.** From ADB's
own criteria:

- **At least two years of full-time professional work experience after the first degree.** A
  fresh graduate cannot apply. This alone excludes most of our student population.
- **Age under 35** at application.
- **Means-tested**: family income ≤ USD 50,000/year and individual income ≤ USD 25,000/year,
  verified by municipality-certified tax returns or salary certification.
- No dual citizenship with a developed country; not already enrolled in a graduate programme.
- **A signed commitment to return home for at least two years** after study.

Run 1 presented ADB-JSP as a "first-tier full-cost option" with none of this. **Take run 2 on the
conditions.** ADB-JSP is a strong option for a working professional two or more years past their
bachelor's and a non-option for a graduating student — and the pack must say which, because the
difference decides whether it belongs in a portfolio at all.

The **return-service commitment** also puts ADB-JSP in the same class as Bolashak and YLSY: a bonded
award, materially different from an unbonded one, and the student must be told before accepting.

## 3. Quota tables exist, and who publishes them is the finding

**GKS publishes per-country quotas — the funder itself, verbatim, for all nine countries.** From the
2026 NIIED graduate guideline, Embassy Track: **Armenia 2, Azerbaijan 5, Georgia 4, Kazakhstan 13,
Kyrgyzstan 9, Tajikistan 5, Turkmenistan 7, Türkiye 11, Uzbekistan 13.** University/R-GKS Track
adds materially different odds — Kazakhstan 5/18 and Uzbekistan 6/6 against Armenia 1/1.

This is a **numerator with a knowable denominator per country**, and it is the first scheme in the
whole research programme where per-nationality scarcity can be compared directly. Note the route
split: **Embassy Track is `NOMINATED`, University Track is `DIRECT`** — so for a student who needs a
self-initiated option, the University Track quota is the one that matters.

**Stipendium Hungaricum publishes nothing centrally, but the sending side does.** Azerbaijan's State
Agency disclosed **104 Azerbaijani awards for 2026/27, of which 80 at master's level**. Hungary
publishes no per-country figure at all.

**That asymmetry is the transferable finding.** Where a funder is silent, the *sending* government
may not be — it has its own reason to publicise what it negotiated. Any future quota question should
be asked on both sides, and a sending-side number must be attributed to the sending side rather than
presented as the funder's.

## 4. Where the runs disagree

| Fact | Run 1 | Run 2 | Position |
|---|---|---|---|
| **ADB-JSP conditions** | Not mentioned | Work experience, age, means test, return service | **Run 2.** A sourced positive over an omission, and it changes eligibility fundamentally |
| **ADB-JSP route** | `NOMINATED`/`HYBRID` | "no separate application to ADB is necessary" — apply to the institution, which ranks and forwards | **Run 2**, which is more precise. Practically self-initiated: the student needs no nominating body, only an institution |
| **Jean Monnet** | Absent | Found — EU-funded, ~150 scholars, 2026-27 deadline 4 Nov 2025 | **Run 2.** Run 1 missed it entirely |
| **TEV detail** | Eight schemes with verbatim coverage clauses and cycles | Narrative treatment | **Run 1**, which is far more encodable |
| **Estonia** | Covered — Harno, EUR 350/mo | Absent | **Run 1** |
| **Türkiye Bursları quotas** | Not published | Not published | Agreed — allocation is not public |

**Neither run alone would have served Türkiye.** Run 1 found the TEV portfolio; run 2 found Jean
Monnet. Both are needed, and Türkiye was the market both earlier runs served worst.

## 5. Confirmed absences — stop re-checking these

- **EBRD** — no master's scholarship. Its International Professionals Programme is a 24-month
  graduate employment scheme, not funding for study.
- **UNDP** — internships and fellowships, no degree scholarship.
- **OTS / TÜRKSOY / Turkic Academy** — cultural and research mandates, no scholarship of their own.
  The Heydar Aliyev Turkic World Scholarship is run by Ibn Haldun University, not by OTS.
- **TÜBİTAK 2215** — for non-Turkish citizens studying *in* Türkiye. Not an outbound route.
- **Türkiye Bursları** — inbound only; Turkish citizens are ineligible.
- **YTB–IsDB joint programme** — funds other nationalities *into* Türkiye, not Turkish citizens out.
- **DAAD In-Country/In-Region** — names Sub-Saharan Africa, Central and South America, South and
  Southeast Asia, and the Middle East. **The South Caucasus and Central Asia are excluded.**
- **JJ/WBGSP** — eligible only for **Kyrgyz Republic, Tajikistan, Uzbekistan**. Armenia, Azerbaijan,
  Georgia, Kazakhstan, Turkmenistan and Türkiye are **not** on the 2027 list.
- **Swedish Institute** — Armenia, Azerbaijan, Georgia and Uzbekistan only. The other five,
  including Türkiye, are off the current list.

## 6. Did the derived enumeration work?

**Partly, and its weakness is stated by the run itself.** The 1a membership enumeration produced a
usable scaffold — UN/UNESCO, World Bank, IMF, ADB, IsDB/OIC, EBRD, OTS, TÜRKSOY, Turkic Academy,
Erasmus+ partner framework, Council of Europe, CIS/EAEU — with the ADB and IsDB membership splits
that decide eligibility. The 1b "who has a reason to pay" axis surfaced the Aga Khan Foundation,
which no membership list would reach.

But run 1 closes with: *"individual MFA citations were not separately fetched for every membership
claim within the search budget, so treat 1a as an audit scaffold to be confirmed line-by-line before
publication."* The enumeration is therefore **not yet the auditable artifact the prompt asked for** —
it is a plausible list without per-claim sourcing.

**The method is still right and should be kept.** It produced ADB-JSP, Jean Monnet and the TEV
portfolio, none of which a named list would have contained. But an enumeration whose membership
claims are unsourced cannot support a coverage argument, and the next use of this pattern should
budget for the sourcing rather than treating it as free.

## 7. Design consequences

1. **`covers_commitment_payment` and bonding need a return-service field.** ADB-JSP, Bolashak and
   YLSY all bind the graduate to return home. The portfolio spec models commitment payments and
   post-study work rights but has no field for an obligation attached to the *award*. Three
   confirmed instruments now carry one.
2. **Eligibility is not only nationality.** ADB-JSP's two-year work requirement and means test mean
   a scheme can name a student's country and still exclude them. The instrument model needs
   applicant-attribute conditions — work experience, age, income — not just a nationality list.
3. **GKS University Track is the first `DIRECT` scheme with a published per-country quota**, which
   makes it the only instrument in the catalogue where a per-nationality selection *ratio* could be
   computed if applicant counts ever appear. Record the quota with its year.
4. **Ask both sides for a quota.** The sending-side asymmetry generalises beyond Hungary.
5. **Türkiye's outbound landscape is TEV-shaped**, not state-shaped. YTB and TÜBİTAK are confirmed
   dead ends for outbound master's study; a private foundation is the principal channel. That is the
   opposite of the Azerbaijani picture and should not be modelled the same way.

## 8. What all four runs missed — the Fulbright Foreign Student Program

**Found 2026-08-29 by a cheap cross-check with `gptr-mcp`, not by any of the four runs.** It is the
largest single omission in this thread, and it is a *family*, not a scheme.

The **Fulbright Foreign Student Program** funds citizens of other countries to take a **master's
degree in the United States**. Per `foreign.fulbrightonline.org`, it "operates in more than 160
countries worldwide" and "awards approximately 4,000 grants annually to foreign students to study
for a Master's or Doctorate degree." Benefits: J-1 sponsorship, funding support, health plan.

**It is nationality-keyed by construction**, which is exactly why it belonged in this run: *"If you
are a non-U.S. citizen looking to apply for a Fulbright grant to study in the United States you will
apply to the Fulbright Commissions/Foundations or U.S. Embassy in your home country"*, and an
applicant must *"reside in the country of nomination at the time of application."* There is no single
global competition — there are ~160 national competitions with their own terms, deadlines and
priority fields.

Confirmed operating for our population:

| Country | Evidence |
|---|---|
| **Türkiye** | Turkish Fulbright Commission; `educationusa.state.gov` lists "Fulbright Master's and PhD Grants – Türkiye". Turkish citizens; dual US/Turkish **not** eligible |
| **Uzbekistan** | `uz.usembassy.gov` — "citizens of Uzbekistan", bachelor's, **minimum two years of professional experience**; priority fields named per cycle |
| **Georgia** | `ge.usembassy.gov` — "full financial support for students pursuing a master's degree at a U.S. university"; 2027-28 deadline 1 July 2026 |
| **Kazakhstan** | Confirmed running |
| **Armenia, Azerbaijan** | On the eligible-country list; per-country terms to verify |

**Why four runs missed it, and it is not simply carelessness.** Our prompts are organised by *funder
type* — bloc, IGO, corporate, our countries' own outbound provision — and Fulbright is none of
those. It is a **third country's government funding our citizens outbound to itself**, which is
structurally destination-inbound and was therefore pushed to
`PROMPT-government-inbound-sweep.md`. That prompt has not been run yet, so this is a **not-yet-run
gap rather than a permanent one**.

But the taxonomy still failed, in a way worth fixing: prompt 1 excluded "general destination-inbound
schemes with **no** nationality restriction," and Fulbright *does* have one — per-country commissions,
per-country competitions, a residence requirement. It met prompt 1's own in-scope test and was
excluded anyway because it looked like a famous general scheme. **The same reasoning would have
excluded Chevening, which run 1 then found operating as Chevening-TEV, a Türkiye-specific track.**

Two consequences:

1. **The inbound sweep's US row must capture the commission structure**, not record "Fulbright
   exists." Nine separate national competitions with different deadlines, priority fields and
   experience requirements is nine instruments, not one.
2. **A third scheme with a professional-experience gate.** ADB-JSP requires two years, JJ/WBGSP
   requires "relevant professional experience", Fulbright Uzbekistan requires two years. This is a
   **pattern, not a coincidence**: the large development-flavoured schemes systematically exclude
   fresh graduates. §7's point about applicant-attribute conditions is now load-bearing rather than
   speculative — a catalogue that models only nationality will offer students schemes they cannot
   enter.

**Method note.** The cross-check cost three cheap searches and found what four deep-research runs
did not. The lesson is not that the runs were poor — they were good — but that **a cheap
adversarial pass over a finished synthesis is worth running**, because it probes the taxonomy rather
than working inside it.

  research/FLTA. Nine national competitions, verified individually.

  exclusion assumed away.
