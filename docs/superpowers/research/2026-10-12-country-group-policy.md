# Group phrases: what funders actually mean (CIS, developing, Global South, emerging)

Researched 2026-10-12. Builds on `country-groups-research.md` (read first). Every claim carries a URL.
**[UNVERIFIED]** = from a search snippet, an aggregator or news, not read on the owner's page.

## Q1. CIS / страны СНГ

### Who writes it
- **Rossotrudnichestvo (Russian Government quota)** writes "CIS countries" with no list. MISIS's quota page:
  "by December 15, 2026 — for CIS countries, by January 15, 2027 — for all other countries".
  https://en.misis.ru/applicants/scholarships/quota/
- **The quota's legal texts do not define CIS.** Selection order Minobrnauki No. 1378 (3 Nov 2020) says only "государствами — участниками
  Содружества Независимых Государств" (for olympiad winners) and names no state:
  https://legalacts.ru/doc/prikaz-minobrnauki-rossii-ot-03112020-n-1378-ob-utverzhdenii/ . Quota decree No. 2150 (18 Dec 2020)
  has no CIS text in the part I could read. https://base.garant.ru/75091924/ (partial read)
- **The same funder's operational page does enumerate it.** education-in-russia.com (Rossotrudnichestvo's portal),
  news of 01.04.26, "Проведение онлайн-тестирования для стран СНГ, Украины и Южной Осетии". The per-country groups listed are:
  Азербайджан, Армения, Белоруссия, Казахстан, Киргизия, Молдова, Молдова (Приднестровье), Таджикистан,
  Туркменистан, Узбекистан, plus Южная Осетия. Ukraine appears in the timetable, **but the title puts it outside "СНГ"**.
  **Georgia does not appear at all.**
  https://education-in-russia.com/news/provedenie-proktoringa-dlya-stran-sng-ukrainy-i-yuzhnoy-osetii (JS page, rendered with a browser)
- **Belarus** universities do not say "СНГ" for their equal-access rule. They name RU, KZ, KG and TJ, which come from the 1996
  integration treaty, not from the CIS. https://www.bsmu.by/abiturientu/pravila-i-poryadok-priema/poryadok-priema-grazhdan-rossiyskoy-federatsii-kazakhstana-kyrgyzstana-i-tadzhikistana/
  (page read; the treaty date and Russian decree No. 662 of 1999 come from a search snippet **[UNVERIFIED]**)
- **Azerbaijan** (Heydar Aliyev IEG 2026-27, MFA and MSE) does not use CIS. It uses "OIC; Non-Aligned Movement; SIDS; Countries sharing
  common cultural, ethnic and historical ties with Azerbaijan", and its **Annex 2 "List of eligible countries"** resolves the vague
  4th clause. The annex is not in the published PDF. https://studyinazerbaijan.edu.az/H.Aliyev_IEG_CALL_18.04.pdf
- **Kazakhstan**'s scholarship for foreigners is described as open to all countries **[UNVERIFIED, aggregators only]**. No CIS
  phrase was found in Kazakh grant rules (adilet.zan.kz returned a JS shell).

- ExCom list (11, still includes UA and MD, with no note on Moldova's notice): https://eccis.org/topic/2
- The 1997 CIS education-space agreement has signature blocks for 12 states, but **UA, TM, UZ and GE are left blank**, and Moldova
  signed with a reservation. https://eccis.org/document/657 . So "CIS" in education law is narrower than the ExCom list.

**Conclusion Q1.** A careful reader uses these lists:
- **Russian funder** (quota, Russian universities): AZ AM BY KZ KG MD TJ TM UZ. Ukraine is **not** in it; Ukraine and South Ossetia are
  named separately when they are eligible. After 8 Apr 2027, Moldova goes to review.
- **Other funders** (rare outside Russia; none found in Azerbaijan or Kazakhstan): the same 9 states.
- **Genuinely disputed: Ukraine** (ExCom lists it; Russia's own funder excludes it; Ukraine rejects it) and **Moldova after Apr 2027**.
- Turkmenistan is a de-jure footnote that no funder acts on. **Georgia is settled: out.** Türkiye is never CIS.

**Findings Q2**
- **Literal DAC list: 2 of 11 sampled live programmes**, both DAAD (EPOS and Helmut-Schmidt). 8 of 11 publish their own list;
  1 (MOFCOM) gives none.
- **"DAC list as the default for a DAC-donor agency" is half right.** Donor money counted as ODA must go to the DAC list
  (OECD: https://www.oecd.org/en/topics/sub-issues/oda-eligibility-and-conditions/dac-list-of-oda-recipients.html). So for a donor,
  the DAC list is a reliable **upper bound**: a country *off* it is out. It is not the actual set. Of 5 donor programmes,
  3 (SI, CSC, NORPART) use strict subsets. For our students the upper bound decides almost nothing: the 2025 DAC list includes
  **AZ AM BY GE KZ KG MD TJ TM TR UA UZ, every platform country except Russia** (checked on the PDF above).
- **Non-donor funders do not mean DAC.** The World Bank, ADB, TWAS and Faculty for the Future each mean their own narrower list,
  usually income-, HDI- or LDC-based. China's MOFCOM means "wherever our embassy runs the call", and China itself is on the DAC list.
- **"Global South"** appears as funder prose beside a published list (NORPART, Helmut-Schmidt), never as the criterion alone.
  It is mostly aggregator vocabulary (search sample). **"Emerging"** means the DAC list at DAAD and a low-HDI list at
  Faculty for the Future: the same word with opposite breadth.

## Q3. Recommended policy per phrase

The two errors cost different amounts here. A wrong "eligible" sends a student to write a CV and letters for nothing, and it
breaks the platform rule. A wrong "not eligible" hides one award among many. So: auto-decide only where the evidence is
one-sided, and send the rest to the queue.

| Phrase | Funder list present | No funder list |
|---|---|---|
| CIS / СНГ / государства-участники СНГ | Use it | **Expand to AZ AM BY KZ KG TJ TM UZ.** MD: include while the page date is before 2027-04-08, review after. **UA: review**, never auto-include (Russia's own funder excludes it). GE, TR: **not eligible**. A Russian funder that names Ukraine/South Ossetia separately is explicit, so honour it |
| developing countries / developing and emerging (DAAD) | Use it (most cases; check linked PDFs and "list of countries" tabs first) | **Off DAC list → not eligible (auto).** On DAC list → **review**, even for a donor (3 of 5 donors are narrower; non-donors 0 of 5 use DAC). Exception: the page itself says DAC/ODA → expand to DAC |
| Global South | Use it | **Review.** There is no owner list. Do not auto-exclude Russia either (contested self-identification) |
| emerging economies / newly industrialised | Use it | **Review.** DAAD's own usage = DAC; elsewhere undefined (IMF and UNCTAD lists disagree, prior §15) |
| low- and middle-income | Use it (CSC and FFTF enumerate narrower lists) | Expand to World Bank LMY **only when the phrase is the whole criterion**. Any qualifier ("…where women are underrepresented") → review |
| "cultural/historical ties" and similar | Use the annex | Review, and fetch the annex (Azerbaijan's Annex 2 was not online) |

Other rules the sample showed:
- Bind the list to the call year (the DAAD EPOS PDF says "validity 2024-2025"; Moldova changes in 2027).
- Some funders test the **country of residence**, not citizenship: DAAD Helmut-Schmidt does. Others bar dual citizens of
  developed or non-DAC countries (SI, ADB, FFTF).

**Queue load (thin evidence).** In this 13-programme sample, the no-list cases are MOFCOM ("developing") and the Azerbaijani
annex: 2 of 13, about 15%. The sample is biased toward large programmes that publish lists. Long-tail university and foundation
pages are likelier to say "developing countries" bare. CIS review only fires for Ukrainian or Moldovan users, a small share of
this audience. **Measure on the real catalogue before trusting the 15% cap:** count the awards whose only geographic criterion is
a bare group phrase, by phrase. Not done here.

Where evidence is thin:
- The OPEC Fund and Kazakhstan claims (aggregators).
- Moldova's and Ukraine's status (news, plus an ExCom page that has not been updated).
- How often each phrase occurs (no corpus count).
- Whether the CSC list is DAC ∩ Commonwealth (not stated).
