# Deep research prompt — Azerbaijan and Türkiye outbound state scholarships

**Status:** ready to run. Written 2026-08-29. Run twice, independently, so the results can be
adjudicated.

**Scope note.** This is the deep half of a split. Two countries, maximum depth. The broad survey of
~50 countries' **inbound** schemes is a separate run
(`PROMPT-government-inbound-sweep.md`) — do not attempt it here, and do not widen this one. The
value of this run is entirely in its depth.

**Why this exists.** Azerbaijan and Türkiye are the launch markets. Their **outbound** state
provision — a country funding its own citizens to study abroad — is the single most
decision-relevant instrument set for our students, and it is the family with no index anywhere.

Our current model of the Azerbaijani programme is **known to be wrong**: it conflates the outbound
state programme with the inbound Heydar Aliyev scholarship for foreign students, and a downstream
rule bars Azerbaijan from ever being a portfolio floor on the strength of the *inbound* one. This
run is what corrects it.

---

## Prompt

Research the **state-funded outbound scholarship provision** of **Azerbaijan** and **Türkiye** —
programmes by which each government funds its own citizens to take a **master's degree abroad**.

Doctoral and postdoctoral funding is out of scope except where a programme covers master's *and*
doctoral study under one instrument, in which case say so and give the master's terms.

### Part A — Azerbaijan

Establish, from primary sources:

1. **The State Programme for the education of Azerbaijani youth abroad** — its current status. Is it
   open, suspended, closed, or replaced by a successor? **Do not assume continuity**; a programme
   with a live-looking web page may have run no cycle for years. Give the most recent cycle you can
   evidence, with its date.
2. **The administering body today**, and any transfer of responsibility between ministries or
   agencies over time.
3. **Eligible destinations** — any country, a named list, or a condition tied to university ranking
   or accreditation. If a list of approved universities is published, say where and how often it is
   updated.
4. **Eligible fields**, and whether a priority-fields list is published per cycle.
5. **What it covers** — tuition, stipend, travel, insurance, dependants — and any cap.
6. **The return-service obligation.** How many years, to which employer or sector, what happens on
   non-return, and whether repayment is financially enforced. **Quote the operative provision.**
7. **The application route and calendar** — direct application or nomination, whether prior admission
   is required, and the annual timing.
8. **Numbers**, only if published: awards per cycle, applicants, or neither.

**Then, separately and explicitly: the inbound Heydar Aliyev scholarship for *foreign* students.**
Same fields. Present the two **side by side** and state plainly how they differ — who is eligible,
who pays, which direction the student travels. Prior research merged them, which is the specific
error this run exists to correct.

### Part B — Türkiye

The same eight fields for Türkiye's outbound state provision.

**Enumerate before you check.** Türkiye's outbound funding is spread across several public bodies,
so first list **every Turkish state institution that funds a citizen's study abroad** — ministries,
agencies, research councils, state banks, and the state-linked foundations that operate alongside
them. Derive that list from Turkish-language government sources, not from an English summary.

Two are known and must be covered regardless, as a floor rather than the scope:

1. **YLSY** (Milli Eğitim Bakanlığı's overseas graduate scholarship) as the principal programme, and
   any successor, parallel or replacement scheme.
2. Any **TÜBİTAK**, ministry or university-linked outbound award that funds a master's abroad.

**A body your enumeration adds is a more valuable finding than either of these**, since both are
already on our radar and neither has been characterised.

Give particular attention to:

- **The return-service obligation**, which is reported to be substantial and bonded. Establish the
  number of years, the employing institution (whether the sponsoring ministry, a named university,
  or a general public-service requirement), what happens on non-completion or non-return, and
  whether a financial guarantee or surety is required at the point of acceptance. **Quote the
  provision.** A bonded award is a materially different product from an unbonded one, and if the
  obligation is severe enough to change whether the award is worth taking, say so directly.
- Whether the **eligible-university and eligible-field lists** are published per cycle, where, and in
  what format.
- Whether the scheme distinguishes between candidates who already hold an offer and those who do not.

### Part C — comparison and encoding

A short section stating, for both countries:

- Whether the programme is `DIRECT` or `NOMINATED`.
- Whether an applicant can hold it *alongside* a destination scholarship, or whether it is exclusive.
- The single most decision-relevant fact a counselor would need to tell a student before they apply.
- Anything that is **not published** and would have to be established by asking the administering
  body directly.

### Sourcing rules

1. **Primary sources only** — the ministry, the agency, the official gazette or legal database.
   Study-abroad blogs and forums may help you *find* a programme; never use one to establish a fact
   about it. This family is heavily covered by outdated third-party summaries.
2. **Quote the operative provision** for the return obligation, eligibility and application route. A
   paraphrase of a bonding clause is not usable.
3. **Date every fact**, and give the cycle year it belongs to.
4. **Do not present a programme as live without a current-cycle source.** If the most recent
   evidenced cycle is 2022, say that, rather than describing the programme in the present tense.
5. **Where something is unpublished, say so.** Do not infer award counts, and do not infer the
   severity of an obligation from its existence.
6. **Work in Azerbaijani and Turkish**, not only English. The authoritative texts are in those
   languages and the English pages are frequently older.
7. Where sources conflict, give both with dates.

### What would change our plans

Flag explicitly: either programme being **suspended or closed**, which would remove the instrument
entirely; a return obligation severe enough that the award is not worth taking for most students; an
exclusivity rule preventing the award being held alongside a destination scholarship; a published
approved-university list, which becomes a hard constraint on what we may recommend; and any
difference between the Azerbaijani outbound programme and the Heydar Aliyev grant beyond the
direction of travel.
