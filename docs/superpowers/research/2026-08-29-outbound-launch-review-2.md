# **State-Funded Outbound Scholarship Provisions of Azerbaijan and Türkiye for Master's Degrees Abroad: A Comprehensive Policy Analysis**

From a Baku vantage point, the strategic deployment of state capital to finance outbound higher education reflects a paramount macroeconomic imperative: the rapid transition from resource-dependent economic models to knowledge-based paradigms. For both the Republic of Azerbaijan and the Republic of Türkiye, outbound master’s scholarships serve as statecraft instruments designed to bridge domestic human capital deficits, foster technological transfer, and integrate local institutions into global academic networks. This report delivers an exhaustive investigation into the master's degree funding mechanisms of both states, relying exclusively on primary source legal databases, official gazettes, and ministerial publications active up to the 2025/2026 and 2026/2027 academic cycles.

## **Part A: The State Programme of the Republic of Azerbaijan**

The primary instrument for Azerbaijani outbound master's education is the "State Programme for the Education of Azerbaijani Youth in Prestigious Foreign Higher Education Institutions for 2022–2028" (hereafter referred to as the DP)1. This initiative operates entirely distinctly from the HTP framework. The DP is subject to rigorous legislative parameters that dictate eligibility, financial dispersion, and post-graduation labor obligations, explicitly anchoring the state's mandate to convert hydrocarbon revenues into sustainable human capital.

### **Administering Body and Operational Cycles**

The DP is administered jointly by the Ministry of Science and Education of the Republic of Azerbaijan (Elm və Təhsil Nazirliyi) and the specifically designated State Programme Management Group (Dövlət Proqramının İdarəetmə Qrupu)4. Financial backing is drawn directly from the State Oil Fund of the Republic of Azerbaijan (SOFAZ), as codified in the state budget allocations6. The program's continuity is actively maintained and documented; official ministerial announcements confirm the operational status of the 2025–2026 academic cycle9 and the 2026–2027 academic cycle5. The framework explicitly prohibits assuming future continuity without active-cycle documentation, yet current sources firmly establish the DP's active status through 202812.

### **Financial Coverage and Allocation Mechanics**

The financial coverage of the DP is exhaustive and meticulously legislated by Cabinet of Ministers Decree No. 218, dated June 1, 20225. Funding guarantees are exclusively issued via direct institutional transfers or highly regulated reimbursements in Azerbaijani Manats (AZN), calculated at the official exchange rate on the date of transaction processing5. The program explicitly finances the following structural pillars of the student experience:

> 1. **Tuition Fees:** Transferred directly to the foreign university's bank account. This requires the student to complete institutional registration and submit an official, stamped invoice to the Management Group via email (dpinfo@edu.gov.az). The state covers the core tuition fee, registration fees, and any mandatory institutional charges5.  
> 2. **Visa and Registration Expenses:** Reimbursed retroactively to the student's bank account. Covered costs include the official diplomatic visa tariff, mandatory medical examination expenses tied to the visa, and local registration fees upon arrival5.  
> 3. **Medical Insurance:** Covers the mandatory premium required by the host country or university. Crucially, the 2022 decree extends this coverage to force majeure circumstances (e.g., natural disasters, war) and multi-country degree requirements5.  
> 4. **International Transport:** Covers the price of one economy class plane ticket (including one piece of checked baggage) or other international public transport per academic year. The Management Group can pre-order these tickets directly from a partner travel agency given a one-month advance electronic application, or the student may seek reimbursement by submitting electronic tickets and boarding passes5.  
> 5. **Bank and Card Fees:** Covers the annual service fee for the state-issued scholarship card and the specific bank service fees incurred during the transfer of tuition and living stipends5.

The most nuanced element of the coverage is the **Monthly Living Allowance**. The Ministry dictates specific stipends across 49 geographic jurisdictions, utilizing a tiered system that adjusts for local purchasing power parities and urban concentration costs5. ### **Application Route and Requirements**

The application pipeline operates through the centralized portal.edu.az digital infrastructure. For a master's application to be vetted in the 2026/2027 cycle, the candidate must satisfy stringent ex-ante criteria:

* **Academic Standing:** Hold an unconditional admission offer from a listed university or qualify under the Top-10 exception5.  
* **GPA Threshold:** Possess an undergraduate cumulative GPA (ÜOMG) of at least 81/100, or its translated foreign grading equivalent5.  
* **Linguistic Certification:** Demonstrate a CEFR C1 proficiency in the language of instruction via recognized testing regimes (e.g., IELTS, TOEFL, TestDAF, DELF)5.  
* **Recommendations:** Submit at least two letters from academic faculty; if currently employed, one letter may originate from the employer5.

### **Statistical Realities**

The numerical data provided by the Ministry separates theoretical ceilings from realized outcomes, underscoring the attrition rates inherent in high-stakes outbound scholarship pipelines. Up to the 2026 updates, the DP published the following statistics:

* **Award Count (hüququ qazanıb):** 1,252 students won the right to study across 151 higher education institutions in 21 countries12. This figure represents the number of candidates who successfully passed the selection funnel and were granted the financial mandate.  
* **Realized Enrolment/Graduation (məzunu olub):** 186 individuals have completed their education and officially attained the status of State Programme alumni across 64 prestigious universities in 14 countries12.

### **The Return-Service Obligation**

The bedrock of the DP is the post-graduation labor covenant designed to mitigate national brain drain. Upon graduation, candidates are bound by a bilateral agreement executed prior to departure under the July 7, 2022, Order No. F-4365.  
The operative provision establishing the duration and nature of the return service for candidates is codified verbatim as follows:  
> **Original Azerbaijani:** "... olmaqla ən azı 5 (beş) il müddətinə əmək fəaliyyətinə cəlb edilməsi üçün əmək müqaviləsi bağlamağa razılığının olması;"15.  
> **English Translation:** "... having the consent to conclude an employment contract to be involved in labor activity for a period of at least 5 (five) years;"  
Additionally, complementary legal frameworks stipulate the enforcement mechanism:  
> **Original Azerbaijani:** "... 5 (beş) il müddətinə əmək fəaliyyətinə cəlb edilməsi üçün əmək müqaviləsi"15.  
> **English Translation:** "... an employment contract to be involved in labor activity for a period of 5 (five) years"  
The enforcement mechanism relies on this formal labor contract ("əmək müqaviləsi"), integrating the graduate back into the domestic economy18. Unlike historical iterations of the program (such as the 2019-2023 PhD track, which explicitly mandated return service at local higher education institutions, phrased as "yerli ATM-də")20, the current master's directives do not publish a pre-assigned employer list. The primary sources indicate a reliance on broader economic integration managed by the state upon the student's return, provided the 5-year threshold is met within the borders of Azerbaijan17.

## **Part B: State-Funded Outbound Master's Provisions of Türkiye**

Türkiye approaches human capital financing through a highly structured, heavily legislated bureaucratic apparatus. Unlike Azerbaijan's centralized single-program approach, Turkish funding involves multiple state organs with strictly demarcated mandates.

### **Institutional Enumeration and Scope Determination**

A comprehensive scan of Turkish state organs active in the 2025/2026 timelines yields four primary institutions engaged in postgraduate academic funding. These must be accurately delineated to isolate true outbound master's funding:

> 1. **Atatürk Yüksek Kurumu (AYK):** Provides domestic (yurt içi) master's and doctoral scholarships, but restricts its outbound (yurt dışı) funding strictly to post-doctoral research for those who have already completed their doctorates27. It is therefore fundamentally out of scope for outbound master's degrees.  
> 2. **TÜBİTAK (The Scientific and Technological Research Council of Türkiye):** Under the 2210 program suite (2210-A, 2210-D), TÜBİTAK provides extensive master's funding, but it is explicitly confined to domestic (Yurt İçi) education29. Furthermore, its international outbound program (2213-A) is restricted exclusively to doctoral (PhD) studies31. Consequently, TÜBİTAK does not directly fund outbound master's degrees.  
> 3. **Türkiye Cumhuriyet Merkez Bankası (TCMB \- Central Bank of the Republic of Türkiye):** Funds outbound master's and doctoral education strictly for internal employees as part of its institutional human resources strategy. In the 2025 reporting cycle, the bank deployed 9 employees as foreign scholarship holders and 2 on unpaid leave for postgraduate education abroad32.  
> 4. **Ministry of National Education (MEB \- YLSY):** The Yurt Dışına Lisansüstü Öğrenim Görmek Üzere Gönderilecek Adayları Seçme ve Yerleştirme (YLSY) program, governed by the historic Law No. 1416, represents the absolute floor and ceiling of general public outbound master's funding in Türkiye33.

Therefore, the investigation of Turkish state-funded outbound master's degrees centers overwhelmingly on the MEB's YLSY program, alongside the localized employee model of the TCMB. (Note: Programs such as the Jean Monnet Scholarship, while referenced in the data, operate under European Union frameworks rather than sovereign Turkish state funding and do not mandate traditional mecburi hizmet in the same legal manner, placing them outside sovereign provisioning37).

### **The YLSY Mechanism: Eligible Universities, Fields, and Offer Requirements**

The YLSY program is explicitly designed to fulfill the academic faculty requirements of Turkish universities and the highly specialized personnel needs of various public institutions38. It does not operate on a post-admission funding model like Azerbaijan; rather, it is a highly competitive pre-placement exam system.  
Candidates are selected via a multi-stage process routed through the Official Scholarship Student System (REBUS). The 2026 cycle processed applications between December 19, 2025, and January 9, 202636. The fundamental offer requirements include:

* **Citizenship and Academics:** The candidate must be a Turkish citizen holding a bachelor's degree with a minimum GPA of 2.5/4.00 or 65/10039.  
* **Standardized Testing (ALES):** The candidate must possess an Academic Personnel and Postgraduate Education Entrance Exam (ALES) score of at least 75, obtained within the last 5 years39.  
* **Institutional Placement:** Candidates scoring above the 75-point threshold submit preferences for the specific public institution or university they will be legally bound to serve upon return36. Placement is determined by the supremacy of the YLSY placement score36.

YLSY funding covers tuition, monthly stipends, health expenses, and a highly notable language-training component. Acknowledging linguistic barriers, the program offers up to 18 months of language training support (up to 12 months domestically and 6 months abroad) for scholars who do not yet meet the destination university's linguistic thresholds before beginning their master's degree39. Master's study is generally funded for up to 2 years, with exact field and destination university parameters set dynamically by the specific public institution holding the employment quota for that candidate, rendering the approved university list a decentralized function of institutional preference rather than a single unified document like Azerbaijan's DP36.

### **The Return-Service Obligation and Financial Sureties**

The Turkish YLSY program relies on arguably one of the most stringent legal bonding mechanisms in global public scholarship policy, anchored in Law No. 1416 ("Ecnebi Memleketlere Gönderilecek Talebe Hakkında Kanun"). The state views the scholarship as an upfront capital investment requiring mathematically exact labor restitution or punitive financial extraction.  
The duration of the required service is calculated using a strict multiplier, codified in the official communiqués regulating payments:  
> **Original Turkish:** "(2) Yurt dışı öğrenimde mecburi hizmet süresi, okul ödemeleri ve/veya aylık yurt dışı burs ödemelerinin yapıldığı öğrenim sürelerinin iki katı olarak hesaplanır."41.  
> **English Translation:** "(2) The compulsory service period for abroad education is calculated as twice the education periods during which school payments and/or monthly abroad scholarship payments were made."  
Thus, a master's degree requiring 24 months of overseas funding instantly generates a 48-month continuous labor obligation42. If a student utilized 6 months of overseas language preparation alongside an 18-month master's, the 24 months total still yield a 48-month service requirement42.  
The enforcement of this obligation is established through a legally binding deed of obligation and guarantor bond ("taahhütname ve kefaletname")34. The operative provisions of Law 1416 demanding immediate return state:  
> **Original Turkish:** "Tahsilini ikmal ile avdet eden talebe üç ay zarfında taahhütname ile merbut olduğu makama müracaata mecburdur."34.  
> **English Translation:** "The student who returns having completed their education is obliged to apply to the authority to which they are bound by the deed of obligation within three months."  
During this waiting period, to prevent financial distress that might prompt the candidate to seek private employment, the state provides a reduced holding stipend:  
> **Original Turkish:** "Müracaatından itibaren mensup olduğu makamca bu talebeye bir vazifeye tayin edilmedikçe üç ay müddetle ecnebi memleketlerde tahsilde bulunan talebe için itası mukarrer asgarî aylığının yarısı aylık tahsisat olarak verilir."35.  
> **English Translation:** "Unless appointed to a duty by the authority to which they belong from the time of their application, for a period of three months, a monthly allocation equal to half of the minimum monthly salary determined for students studying in foreign countries is given to this student."  
Should the specific state organ (the initial employer designated during the REBUS application phase) fail to absorb the graduate, the law grants the state overarching authority to deploy the individual elsewhere, ensuring the return on investment is realized:  
> **Original Turkish:** "Taahhütname ile merbut olduğu makam tarafından kendisine müracaatından itibaren iki ay zarfında iş gösterilmeyen talebeyi üçüncü ay hitamına kadar Devletin herhangi bir dairesi hizmete tayin edebilir ve bu takdirde talebenin taahütnamesindeki mecburiyeti kendisini hizmete tayin eden daireye intikal eder."35.  
> **English Translation:** "A student who is not provided a job within two months from their application by the authority to which they are bound by the deed of obligation may be appointed to service by any department of the State until the end of the third month, and in this case, the obligation in the student's deed of obligation transfers to the department that appointed them to service."  
Failure to complete the degree within the maximum allotted time, or resignation from the state post prior to fulfilling the 2x multiplier service duration, triggers immediate indemnification proceedings against the student and their financial guarantors, forcing the repayment of all foreign currency disbursed, compounded with statutory interest39.

## **Part C: Summary, Classification, and Administrative Friction**

To synthesize the operational realities of Azerbaijani and Turkish outbound state funding, it is necessary to classify the nature of the application funnels, the exclusivity of the capital, and the unwritten administrative frictions that define the candidate experience.

### **Classification: Direct vs. Nominated Programs**

The architecture of a scholarship program fundamentally dictates its accessibility to the broader talent pool. The analyzed master's programs fall into two distinct classifications:  
**1\. DIRECT:** Programs where any qualifying citizen meeting the baseline academic criteria can independently initiate an application, without requiring prior institutional affiliation or internal nomination.

* **Azerbaijan DP (Master's):** Classified as DIRECT. The operative establishment of this pipeline is explicitly tied to an open digital funnel where candidates independently secure their admission and then seek state capital. This is established by the sentence: "To receive reimbursement \[or initiate funding\], the participant must submit an electronic request to the State Programme Management Group via email at dpinfo@edu.gov.az" alongside their portal.edu.az application5.  
* **Türkiye YLSY:** Classified as DIRECT. Any Turkish citizen meeting the ALES 75 threshold can enter the open REBUS placement system to compete for institutional quotas. This is established by the sentence: "İlk aşamada adaylar REBUS (Resmi Burslu Öğrenci Sistemi) üzerinden çevrimiçi başvurularını gerçekleştirir." ("In the first stage, candidates make their online applications via REBUS")36.

**2\. NOMINATED:** Programs entirely closed to the public, functioning as internal human capital development protocols for specific state agencies.

* **Türkiye TCMB (Central Bank):** Classified as NOMINATED. The program is restricted exclusively to existing staff, established by the sentence: "Yurt dışı lisansüstü eğitim kapsamında 2025 yılında 9 çalışan dış burs bursiyeri olarak, 2 çalışan ise ücretsiz izinli olarak yüksek lisans ve doktora eğitimine..." ("Within the scope of abroad postgraduate education in 2025, 9 employees as external scholarship holders, and 2 employees on unpaid leave to their master's and doctoral education...")32.

### **Exclusivity and Co-Holding Constraints**

A critical decision-relevant factor for top-tier candidates is whether state funding can be co-held alongside destination university merit scholarships or other institutional grants.  
In Türkiye, exclusivity is ruthlessly enforced. The state apparatus considers public funds mutually exclusive with external grants that cover the same parameters. While TÜBİTAK was ruled out for outbound master's, its domestic guidelines reveal the Turkish state's baseline stance on co-holding:  
> "Bursiyerlikleri kesinleşmeden... yurt dışı yüksek lisans bursu kazanmış olanların... yurt dışı yüksek lisans burslarını iptal ettirmesi gerekir" ("Those who have won an abroad master's scholarship before their scholarship status is finalized must cancel their abroad master's scholarship")29.  
Under YLSY regulations, unauthorized absences or alternative funding statuses can lead to a suspension of payments or a recalculation of the debt burden, explicitly tying the student strictly to state dependency for the duration of the 1416 law coverage45.  
In contrast, the Azerbaijani DP regulations do not contain an explicitly published exclusivity clause forcing the rejection of university tuition waivers. If a student secures a tuition waiver from the destination university, the state simply does not process the tuition invoice, but continues to disburse the tiered monthly living allowance5. This effectively allows a synergy of funding mechanisms, prioritizing the reduction of SOFAZ expenditure as long as double-invoicing for the exact same expense does not occur5.

### **Decision-Relevant Facts, Suspensions, and Bonding Constraints**

The most severe constraint across both nations is the legal bonding architecture.

* **Türkiye (YLSY):** The constraint is mathematical and unforgiving. The 2x time multiplier (e.g., 2 years of master's requires 4 years of service)41, backed by a legal deed ("taahhütname") and financial guarantors, means failure results in catastrophic foreign-currency debt35.  
* **Azerbaijan (DP):** The constraint is temporal—a flat 5-year labor contract requirement17—and administrative. The threat of Article 320.2 of the Criminal Code looms over the submission of invoices and boarding passes5.

There are currently no indications in the primary sources that either the Azerbaijani DP 2022-2028 or the Turkish YLSY programs are suspended; both show highly active regulatory frameworks extending into the 2025/2026 and 2026/2027 timelines10.

### **Published Lists vs. Direct Contact Requirements**

Finally, navigating these bureaucracies requires understanding the limits of published information and where institutional friction occurs.  
**Published Lists:**

* Azerbaijan publishes an exhaustive list of the 49 eligible countries, specific tier-based monthly stipends (e.g., $1800 vs $2300 in the USA), the 15 priority fields, and the precise Top 10 university exceptions (excluding UCL) for the 2026/2027 cycle5.  
* Türkiye publishes the minimum ALES thresholds (75), GPA requirements (2.5/4.00), and the exact calculation metrics for mecburi hizmet (2x)39.

**Unpublished Items Requiring Direct Contact:**

* **Azerbaijan DP Visa Support:** Embassies frequently require official government letters confirming financial solvency. These letters are not generated automatically in the portal.edu.az system; the student must explicitly email dpinfo@edu.gov.az to request the diplomatic guarantee letter5.  
* **Azerbaijan DP Flight Pre-Orders:** To utilize the state's direct payment to travel agencies for the annual round-trip flight (rather than paying out of pocket and seeking reimbursement under the currency exchange rate risk), candidates must contact the Management Group at dpinfo@edu.gov.az exactly one month prior to departure5.  
* **Türkiye YLSY Institutional Quotas:** The exact number of master's candidates a specific state organ (e.g., Ministry of Health or a specific provincial university) requires in a given year is not static law; it requires direct access to the cyclical REBUS system guides during the specific application window to view the decentralized quotas36.

The state-funded outbound master's frameworks of Azerbaijan and Türkiye reflect divergent geopolitical philosophies regarding human capital. Azerbaijan's DP relies on a highly curated, elite-institution model heavily weighted toward non-oil sector diversification, offering variable living stipends but demanding a flat 5-year domestic labor return. Türkiye’s YLSY operates as a vast, decentralized bureaucratic machine, funneling talent directly into specific state institutions using an ALES-based competitive matrix and enforcing an aggressive 2x time-multiplier bonding constraint. These programs represent sophisticated legal and financial instruments designed to counter Eurasian brain drain while simultaneously funding deep integration into the global knowledge economy.
