# Master's Study for International Applicants: Primary-Source Facts Across Eight Countries

## TL;DR
- **Admission regimes split into two families.** France (monmaster.gouv.fr), China (考研/推免, from which internationals are exempt via 高校自主招生/自主招生) and Japan (大学院入試) run decentralised, per-university selection; Germany, the UK, Turkey and Hungary are mostly rule-based/direct-to-university at master's level, with published bars applicants can measure against. German NC-style *retrospective* cutoffs generally do NOT apply to international master's applicants.
- **Visa-money thresholds are hard numbers in five countries** (UK £1,171–£1,529/month × 9 months; Germany €992/month = €11,904/year Sperrkonto, in force 2025 and 2026; France €877.50/month from 1 Aug 2026; Korea ₩20m/₩16m; Hungary subsistence proof, no single published figure) but are convention-only in Japan (~¥2m, university-set) and unpublished in China (X1 has no published threshold).
- **Post-study work exists everywhere except China and Turkey.** UK Graduate route (2 yrs, dropping to 18 months for applications on/after 1 Jan 2027), Germany §20 (18 months), France RECE (12 months), Japan 特定活動 (6+6) plus J-Find (2 yrs), Korea D-10 (raised to 3 years effective 29 Oct 2025), Hungary (9 months). China has no national route (Shanghai/free-trade-zone pilots only); Turkey has no dedicated route (employer work permit only).

## Key Findings
- Visa-money data quality is highest for the UK, Germany and France (all publish exact statutory monthly figures on official sites) and weakest for Japan and China (no statutory amount).
- Student-visa refusal rates BY NATIONALITY are only cleanly published by the UK (Home Office Vis_D02/Vis_04). Others publish grants-only, aggregate, or Eurostat-level data.
- Machine-readable accreditation registers exist for the UK (Office for Students CSV) and France (RNCP via data.gouv.fr); most others are human-readable lists.
- Admission-difficulty (competition-ratio) data for international master's applicants *specifically* is essentially not published anywhere as a clean national dataset; where ratios exist (Japan 倍率, Korea 경쟁률, China 报录比) they are university-published and usually domestic/aggregate.

## Details

### GERMANY

**1. Admission regime.** Master's admission at German public universities is primarily rule-based/direct-to-university: applicants apply to each university and are admitted if they meet published requirements (recognised bachelor's, subject match, language). The Numerus Clausus (NC) — a retrospectively computed cutoff after applications close — applies mainly to specific high-demand programmes and is far more common at bachelor's level; at master's level most programmes set published, measurable requirements rather than an NC. Foreign-qualification equivalence is assessed via the anabin database. Source: https://www.auswaertiges-amt.de/de/service/visa-und-aufenthalt/nationale-visa/visa-schule-studium-sprachkurs/sperrkonto (visa/finance framework); anabin (see Q7).

**2. Admission difficulty data.** NC values where they exist are published per-university/per-programme, but there is no national dataset of master's acceptance rates for international applicants. **Structural mismatch:** the NC is a retrospective cutoff, not a competition ratio for internationals, and applies chiefly to undergraduate/limited programmes — "not published" as a clean international-master's-level dataset.

**3. Deposits.** Public universities generally do NOT charge a deposit to hold a place. Instead students pay a per-semester contribution (Semesterbeitrag) after enrolment. **Structural mismatch: no deposit culture at public universities.**

**4. Visa — money.** Non-EU students must prove first-year living costs via a blocked account (Sperrkonto). The current required amount is **€992/month = €11,904/year**, set in September 2024, tied to the maximum BAföG maintenance rate, and **in force for both 2025 and 2026 applications** (up from the previous €934/month = €11,208/year). The Auswärtiges Amt confirms the mechanism (a monthly withdrawal cap, with the mission or Ausländerbehörde as Sperrbegünstigter). Sources: https://www.auswaertiges-amt.de/de/service/visa-und-aufenthalt/nationale-visa/visa-schule-studium-sprachkurs/sperrkonto and https://managua.diplo.de/ni-de/service/05-visaeinreise/sperrkonto-2163974. A scholarship (e.g. DAAD) meeting or exceeding the monthly amount substitutes for the Sperrkonto; the Auswärtiges Amt states scholarships must contain at least the same monthly amount, otherwise the difference must be shown. (For the separate post-study job-seeking/Chancenkarte visa the figure is higher — roughly €1,091/month.)

**5. Visa — risk.** The Auswärtiges Amt publishes a Visastatistik, but a clean student-visa refusal-rate breakdown by nationality in the granular form of the UK's is not readily published. Treat as "not published in an equivalent by-nationality refusal-rate dataset."

**6. Post-study work.** Residence permit for job-seeking after graduation under **§20 Aufenthaltsgesetz — up to 18 months, non-renewable.** Unrestricted employment permitted during the search; livelihood must be secured. On finding a qualifying job, switch to EU Blue Card (§18g) or work permit. Source (statute): https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/aufenthg_2004/__20.html.

**7. Accreditation register.** Hochschulkompass (HRK) lists recognised HEIs and programmes; the Akkreditierungsrat maintains an accredited-programmes database; anabin (KMK) lists foreign institutions with H+/H−/H+/- status for recognition. Machine-readable/API availability is not prominently documented for these; treat as human-readable web databases.

### FRANCE

**1. Admission regime.** Since 2016 universities may select master's students. Entry into first-year master's runs through the national platform monmaster.gouv.fr, which lists all state-accredited national master's degrees with published attendus (expected criteria), capacité d'accueil (capacity) and a national calendar; admission is competitive (dossier ranked against capacity) with published criteria. **Structural mismatch for internationals:** the direct-application/candidature function is for French students and certain foreign students already resident in France/EU; non-EU students resident abroad apply directly to institutions (typically via the Campus France "Études en France" procedure), not through the platform's application flow. Sources: https://www.enseignementsup-recherche.gouv.fr/fr/mon-master-plateforme-nationale-candidature-88498 and https://www.campusfrance.org/en/actu/monmastergouvfr-ouverture-des-candidatures-sur-la-nouvelle-plateforme.

**2. Admission difficulty data.** monmaster.gouv.fr publishes capacité totale per programme and the selection criteria; the 2024 offer recensed 3,500+ masters / 8,000+ parcours. Applicant-vs-capacity ratios are not published as a clean per-programme international-specific dataset — aggregate, not international-specific.

**3. Deposits.** Public universities do NOT charge a deposit. Students pay the CVEC (Contribution Vie Étudiante et de Campus) and enrolment fees after admission. **Structural mismatch: no deposit culture at public universities.**

**4. Visa — money.** From **1 August 2026 the minimum resources for a student long-stay visa rose from €615/month to €877.50/month (47% of gross monthly SMIC)**, i.e. ~€10,530 for a university year. Applications filed up to 31 July 2026 were assessed on the old €615 threshold. Sources: https://pa.diplomatie.gouv.fr/fr/etudes-en-france-evolution-du-seuil-des-ressources-pour-les-visas-etudiants and https://www.japon.campusfrance.org/fr/modification-du-montant-des-ressources-exigees-pour-les-demandes-de-visa-etudiant-a-compter-du-1er. Holders of a French-government scholarship funded by the Ministry of Europe and Foreign Affairs are exempt (the €900/month scholarship exceeds the new threshold); co-financed scholarship holders may be affected. Because the figure is indexed to the SMIC, it changes on each SMIC revision.

**5. Visa — risk.** The Ministère de l'Intérieur publishes "Les chiffres clés de l'immigration" and visa statistics; a clean student-visa refusal-rate breakdown by nationality equivalent to the UK's was not confirmed on an official page — treat as "aggregate/not confirmed as a by-nationality refusal-rate dataset."

**6. Post-study work.** Carte de séjour temporaire "recherche d'emploi ou création d'entreprise" (RECE), replacing the old APS — **12 months, non-renewable**, for holders of a French master's-level diploma; full work rights during the year; a subsequent job must relate to the training and pay ≥1.5× SMIC. Certain nationalities under bilateral accords (e.g. Tunisia, Algeria) get variant APS durations (9/12/18 months). Source (préfecture, services de l'État): https://www.rhone.gouv.fr/Demarches/Immigration-et-integration-dans-le-Rhone/Titres-de-sejour/Etudiants/Recherche-d-emploi-ou-creation-d-entreprise.

**7. Accreditation register.** The Ministère de l'Enseignement supérieur accredits establishments and publishes the list of national master's degrees on monmaster.gouv.fr; professional certifications are in the RNCP (France Compétences), available as open data via data.gouv.fr (machine-readable). Source: https://www.enseignementsup-recherche.gouv.fr/fr/mon-master-plateforme-nationale-candidature-88498.

### JAPAN

**1. Admission regime.** Exam-gated and decentralised: each graduate school runs its own entrance examination (大学院入試), typically combining a written/subject exam, research-plan review and interview; there is no national graduate entrance exam. Bars are set and assessed per-university, not published as national measurable thresholds. Sources (framework, Immigration/Study-in-Japan): https://www.moj.go.jp/isa/applications/status/student.html; https://www.studyinjapan.go.jp/ja/planning/immigration-procedures/.

**2. Admission difficulty data.** Many universities publish 倍率/志願倍率 and 入試状況/入学者選抜実施状況 for graduate schools on their own sites, but there is no national dataset, and figures are usually aggregate. International applicants often enter via separate 外国人留学生特別選抜 tracks whose ratios may or may not be published per-university.

**3. Deposits / enrollment fee.** National universities charge an enrollment fee (入学金) of **¥282,000** and annual tuition of ¥535,800 (MEXT standard amounts, unchanged since the 2004 ministerial ordinance); the master's (博士前期課程) first-year total is ¥817,800. The 入学金 is generally non-refundable and payable on enrolment. Source (citing MEXT 省令): https://www.toshintimes.com/topics/detail/379. **Structural note:** this enrollment fee is the closest analogue to a "deposit" and is typically non-refundable once paid.

**4. Visa — money.** There is NO fixed statutory financial amount for 在留資格「留学」. The Immigration Services Agency (出入国在留管理庁) requires evidence that study costs are sufficiently covered (経費支弁) — bank-balance certificate of the payer, income certificates, funds-formation documents — but sets no published threshold. Universities conventionally ask for a bank balance around **¥2,000,000**; this is convention, not an official statutory figure. Sources: https://www.moj.go.jp/isa/applications/status/student.html and https://www.studyinjapan.go.jp/ja/planning/immigration-procedures/. A MEXT scholarship substitutes for the funding proof. **Structural mismatch: no fixed statutory amount — the ~¥2m figure is a university convention, flagged as not official.**

**5. Visa — risk.** The Immigration Services Agency publishes statistics on 在留資格認定証明書 (COE) and residency; a clean COE grant/refusal rate broken down by nationality was not confirmed on an official page — treat as "not published in an equivalent by-nationality refusal-rate dataset."

**6. Post-study work.** (a) Change of status to 特定活動 (Designated Activities) for continued job-hunting after graduation — **6 months, renewable once (12 months total)**, on a university recommendation. Source: https://www.moj.go.jp/isa/applications/resources/nyukan_nyukan84.html. (b) **J-Find (未来創造人材, 特定活動 Notice No. 51, since April 2023) — up to 2 years**, for graduates of top-100-ranked universities within 5 years of graduation with ≥¥200,000 savings. Source: https://www.moj.go.jp/isa/applications/status/designatedactivities51.html.

**7. Accreditation register.** MEXT publishes the list of universities (大学一覧); NIAD-QE handles quality assurance/degree recognition. Human-readable; no prominently documented API.

### SOUTH KOREA

**1. Admission regime.** Decentralised and rule-based/competitive per-university: international master's applicants apply through each university's admissions office, frequently via a dedicated international/foreign-applicant track (외국인 특별전형). Admission is against published requirements (degree, GPA, language — TOPIK and/or English) plus documents/interview; there is no national graduate entrance exam. Source: Study in Korea (NIIED, studyinkorea.go.kr); embassy D-2 pages e.g. https://overseas.mofa.go.kr/us-losangeles-ko/brd/m_4355/view.do?seq=1333035.

**2. Admission difficulty data.** Universities publish 경쟁률 (competition ratios) via admissions offices, and higher-education disclosure exists at 대학알리미 (academyinfo.go.kr); however, international/foreign-track-specific ratios are not a clean national dataset. Mostly aggregate/domestic.

**3. Deposits / 입학금.** Korea has historically charged an enrollment fee (입학금); national policy has been phasing out 입학금 at national/public universities. Practice for international students varies by university. Treat as university-by-university; no universal non-refundable deposit rule confirmed on a ministry page.

**4. Visa — money.** D-2 requires financial proof commonly set at **₩20,000,000 (Seoul metropolitan area) or ₩16,000,000 (non-metropolitan)**, held for more than 30 days before issuance of the bank statement (for lower-ranked universities, funds must be held 6 months). A full scholarship counts toward the ₩20m (scholarship + deposited funds must exceed ₩20m). Source: https://www.visaforkorea.mn/study-visa-d-2-1d-2-4 (Korea Visa Application Center). The KRW-denominated standard reflects the 2024/2025 shift away from the older ~USD 20,000 figure, which is superseded. **Flag: the cleanest statement of the ₩20m/₩16m split was found on a Korea Visa Application Center page, not directly on studyinkorea.go.kr or 법무부.**

**5. Visa — risk.** The immigration authority (출입국·외국인정책본부) publishes an annual 출입국·외국인정책 통계연보; whether it breaks out D-2 refusal rates by nationality was not confirmed on an official page. Treat as "aggregate published; by-nationality D-2 refusal rate not confirmed."

**6. Post-study work.** D-10 job-seeker visa (구직비자), points-based. **Reformed effective 29 October 2025 (Ministry of Justice): overall cap raised from 2 years to 3 years, with renewals shifted from 6-month to 1-year increments.** D-10-1 applicants scoring ≥80 points can extend to 3 years; first-time D-2→D-10 converters are exempt from the points test and financial proof on initial application; Korean-university graduates within 3 years with TOPIK 4+/KIIP completion are exempt from the points test. A D-10-T "Top-Tier" track (from 2 April 2025) targets overseas top-ranked master's/doctoral holders. Source: immigration.go.kr Visa Navigator and HiKorea (https://www.hikorea.go.kr). **Flag: the 29 Oct 2025 3-year extension is documented (MOJ residence-service manual) but could not be pinned to a single stable HiKorea notice URL.**

**7. Accreditation register.** 교육부 / 한국대학교육협의회 (KCUE) and 대학알리미 (academyinfo.go.kr) provide higher-education disclosure; academyinfo.go.kr offers open data. Specific open-data API documentation was not confirmed during research.

### CHINA

**1. Admission regime.** Domestic master's admission is exam-gated via the national **全国硕士研究生统一招生考试 (考研)**, plus 推荐免试 (推免) exemptions for top domestic graduates. **International students are exempt from 考研:** they apply directly to universities' international-student admissions offices (自主招生 / 申请-审核), typically requiring HSK Level 4 for Chinese-taught master's programmes, plus transcripts, references and a study plan. Source (2026 exam regulation showing 考研/推免/单独考试 structure for domestic candidates): http://www.moe.gov.cn/srcsite/A15/moe_778/s3261/202509/t20250918_1413836.html; CSC/campuschina scholarship admissions e.g. https://www.studyinchina.edu.cn/applyInChina/ApplyForScholarship. **Structural mismatch: the headline 考研 exam does not apply to international applicants.**

**2. Admission difficulty data.** 报录比 (application-to-admission ratios) are published by some university 研究生院 sites for domestic 考研 cohorts; 教育部 does not publish a national international-master's acceptance-rate dataset. International-track ratios are essentially not published.

**3. Deposits.** Practice is university-set. Application/registration fees are common and non-refundable (e.g. Shanghai University charges a ¥500 non-refundable registration fee for degree applicants). Source: https://apply.shu.edu.cn/application/scholarships?language=zh. No national deposit rule; treat as per-university.

**4. Visa — money.** The X1 visa (studies >6 months; X2 for ≤6 months) requires an admission notice (录取通知书), the JW201/JW202 form and a health check; there is **NO published national financial threshold for X1.** Sources (embassy/consulate procedure): https://sapporo.china-consulate.gov.cn/jylx/lxzg1/201610/t20161017_5642572.htm; https://my.china-embassy.gov.cn. **Structural mismatch: no published financial-proof threshold — "not published."** A CSC (Chinese Government Scholarship) award covers costs and effectively substitutes for funding proof.

**5. Visa — risk.** China does NOT publish student-visa refusal or approval rates by nationality. **"Not published."**

**6. Post-study work.** There is **NO nationwide post-study job-search visa** for fresh foreign graduates; the general rule requires ≥2 years' relevant work experience for a Foreigner's Work Permit. Officially-published local pilots exist: Shanghai allows foreign graduates with a bachelor's+ from a Shanghai university to obtain a work permit in designated zones, and master's+ from high-level foreign universities to work anywhere in Shanghai (initial permit ~1 year). Sources (Shanghai municipal government English portal): https://english.shanghai.gov.cn/en-ForeignTalents/20240202/555c2f07404949738f5a9d6db295b3f5.html; National Immigration Administration: https://en.nia.gov.cn. **Structural mismatch: no national post-study route; regional pilots only.**

**7. Accreditation register.** 教育部 publishes the list of accredited Chinese HEIs (全国高等学校名单) and, via **jsj.moe.gov.cn (教育涉外监管信息网), a reference list of 10,000+ recognised FOREIGN universities** (for Chinese students studying abroad). CDGDC/学位网 (chsi.com.cn) handles degree verification/recognition. The jsj.moe.gov.cn list is human-readable. Source: https://jsj.moe.gov.cn/.

### UNITED KINGDOM

**1. Admission regime.** Rule-based and direct-to-university: master's applicants apply directly to each university and receive an offer (often conditional) if they meet published entry requirements (degree class/GPA equivalence, English-language minima e.g. IELTS). Bars are published in advance and measurable. No national graduate entrance exam; UCAS covers undergraduate, not postgraduate taught admissions. **Structural mismatch: postgraduate admission is direct-to-university, so no national platform or NC-style cutoff.**

**2. Admission difficulty data.** No national postgraduate acceptance-rate dataset by nationality. HESA publishes enrolment statistics; UCAS covers undergraduate. Postgraduate acceptance rates are not published centrally — "not published" as an international-master's dataset.

**3. Deposits.** University-by-university policy; deposits are commonly required before the university issues a CAS (Confirmation of Acceptance for Studies). Amounts vary (e.g. £2,000–£4,000+); any tuition deposit recorded on the CAS is deducted from the tuition figure the applicant must evidence for the visa. Source (university example): https://www.gold.ac.uk/immigration/student-visa/financial-requirements/.

**4. Visa — money.** Student route maintenance (Appendix Finance): **£1,529/month (London) or £1,171/month (outside London), up to a maximum of 9 months** (£13,761 London / £10,539 outside), plus outstanding tuition. **These rates took effect for Student-route applications made on or after 11 November 2025** (per the UKCISA summary of the Statement of Changes of 14 October 2025), up from £1,483/£1,023. Funds must be held for **28 consecutive days**, ending no more than 31 days before application; accommodation offset up to one month's maintenance if paid to the provider. Sources: https://www.ukcisa.org.uk/news/student-update-changes-to-the-student-and-graduate-rules/; https://www.ucl.ac.uk/students/immigration-and-visas/student-visa/money/financial-evidence. Students with an official financial sponsor (government/British Council/university) are exempt from showing maintenance; students resident in the UK 12+ months are also exempt. A recognised sponsor/scholarship removes the maintenance requirement.

**5. Visa — risk.** The Home Office publishes "Immigration system statistics," including entry-clearance visa outcomes by nationality and visa type — dataset **Vis_D02** (and the sponsored-study summary Vis_04) — on gov.uk. This DOES break out study visas by nationality (grants and refusals; grant rate = issued ÷ (issued+refused)). Source: https://www.gov.uk/government/statistics/immigration-system-statistics-year-ending-december-2025/why-do-people-come-to-the-uk-study. This is the single best by-nationality student-visa dataset among the eight countries.

**6. Post-study work.** Graduate route — unsponsored, no salary requirement. Currently **2 years for bachelor's/master's (3 years for PhD)**. **For applications made on or after 1 January 2027, the bachelor's/master's duration reduces to 18 months** (PhD stays 3 years); this was first announced in the May 2025 white paper "Restoring control over the immigration system" and confirmed by the Statement of Changes in Immigration Rules of 14 October 2025. Cannot be extended. Source: https://www.ukcisa.org.uk/news/student-update-changes-to-the-student-and-graduate-rules/. **As of August 2026 the route is still 2 years; the 18-month cut is future-dated to 1 Jan 2027.**

**7. Accreditation register.** The Office for Students (OfS) publishes **The OfS Register** of registered English HE providers, downloadable as **CSV (machine-readable)**. gov.uk also lists recognised bodies and listed bodies. The OfS Register is the machine-readable option (officeforstudents.org.uk / gov.uk).

### TURKEY

**1. Admission regime.** Rule-based/competitive per-university, coordinated under YÖK. International master's applicants apply directly to universities' graduate institutes; requirements (degree, sometimes ALES-equivalent or GRE, language) are published. There is no single national international-master's exam (ALES is primarily for domestic applicants; internationals are often exempt or assessed on equivalents). Source: yok.gov.tr; Göç İdaresi student-permit pages (below).

**2. Admission difficulty data.** Not published as a national international-master's competition-ratio dataset. Per-university.

**3. Deposits.** No national deposit rule confirmed on an official source. Tuition at state universities is modest and set nationally; practice for private (vakıf) universities varies. Treat as per-university; "not published" as a national rule.

**4. Visa — money.** A student residence permit (öğrenci ikamet izni) under Law No. 6458, Article 38, requires the applicant to show sufficient means of subsistence (maddi imkân) for the study period — evidenced by bank account, scholarship document or undertaking (taahhütname) — but Göç İdaresi does **NOT publish a specific fixed monthly amount**; the supporting document is assessed. Sources: https://www.goc.gov.tr/ogrenci-ikamet-izni-basvurusuna-iliskin-dokumanlar and https://www.goc.gov.tr/ikamet-izni-cesitleri. **Structural mismatch: no published fixed amount.** A Türkiye Bursları scholarship covers maintenance and substitutes for the proof. Students who obtain Genel Sağlık Sigortası (GSS) within 3 months are exempt from separate health insurance.

**5. Visa — risk.** Göç İdaresi publishes residence-permit statistics (goc.gov.tr/ikamet-izinleri) — these are grants/stocks by nationality, not refusal rates. Refusal rates by nationality are not published. **"Refusals not published; grants by nationality published."**

**6. Post-study work.** No dedicated post-graduation job-search permit. Employment requires an employer-sponsored work permit (çalışma izni) under Law No. 6735, applied for by the *employer* via the Ministry of Labour and Social Security; the work permit also serves as a residence permit. Source: https://www.csgb.gov.tr/en/frequently-asked-questions/directorate-general-of-international-labour-force/work-permit/. **Structural mismatch: no post-study grace-period route; work is employer-driven only.**

**7. Accreditation register.** YÖK publishes the list of universities (yok.gov.tr); YÖKSİS is the national higher-education information system. Human-readable; documented API not confirmed.

### HUNGARY

**1. Admission regime.** Rule-based/competitive per-university. International master's applicants apply directly to institutions (or via Stipendium Hungaricum for scholarship applicants); requirements are published. Some fields (medicine/dentistry/veterinary) require an entrance exam. Sources: Oktatási Hivatal / felvi.hu; https://stipendiumhungaricum.hu/apply/.

**2. Admission difficulty data.** felvi.hu publishes admissions statistics; coverage of international/foreign-language programmes as a clean dataset is limited — treat as mostly domestic/aggregate. On the scholarship side, the official Stipendium Hungaricum programme reported (17 Feb 2025) that **"In 2025, a total of 80,000 applications were submitted, showing a huge, 20% increase compared to last year"**, across 800+ programmes at ~30 Hungarian institutions (top sending countries: Pakistan, Nigeria, Ghana, Kenya, India); third-party guides cite roughly 5,000 places / ~16% acceptance for 2025. **Flag: older "52,000 applicants / 4,700 places / ~11%" figures are superseded and were agency/blog-sourced.**

**3. Deposits.** Self-financed applicants typically pay an application/registration fee and, on accepting an offer, may pay tuition in advance; practice is per-university. No national deposit rule confirmed on an official source.

**4. Visa — money.** A residence permit for study requires proof of subsistence, accommodation and health insurance; the exact monthly figure is **not published as a single national number on oif.gov.hu**. Stipendium Hungaricum and ministerial scholarship holders are exempt from the procedural fee, and the scholarship substitutes for subsistence proof. Sources: https://oif.gov.hu/factsheets/extend-your-permit-student; https://stipendiumhungaricum.hu/apply/. **Flag: the commonly-cited ~€500–600/month figure is from study-abroad sites, not confirmed on oif.gov.hu — treat as approximate/unofficial.**

**5. Visa — risk.** Hungary does not publish a national student-permit refusal-rate dataset by nationality; refusal/first-permit data by citizenship exists at the **Eurostat level** (residence-permit datasets e.g. migr_resfirst). **Only Eurostat-level data exists rather than national publication.**

**6. Post-study work.** Residence permit for the purpose of seeking a job or starting a business, under **Act XC of 2023** (in force 1 Jan 2024) — **maximum 9 months, non-extendable**; requires successful completion of studies, a job/business suitable to the qualification level, and proof of subsistence/accommodation/health insurance. Source: https://oif.gov.hu/factsheets/residence-permit-for-the-purpose-of-seeking-a-job-or-starting-a-business.

**7. Accreditation register.** Oktatási Hivatal maintains the higher-education register (FIR); felvi.hu lists programmes/institutions. Human-readable; documented API not confirmed.

### 8. CROSS-CUTTING — RECOGNITION BACK HOME

The ENIC-NARIC network publishes a directory of national recognition centres. The joint gateway is https://www.enic-naric.net/ and the country-pages/national-information-centres directory is at https://www.enic-naric.net/country-pages.aspx. The network covers 56 countries operating under the Lisbon Recognition Convention (1997); the site is maintained by CIMEA (the Italian ENIC/NARIC). Source: https://www.enic-naric.net/page-about-ENIC-NARIC-Networks.

**Distinction — centres that publish institution LISTS vs. case-by-case assessment:**
- **Publish an actual list of recognised foreign institutions:** Germany's **anabin** database (Kultusministerkonferenz) lists foreign HEIs with H+/H−/H+/- recognition status — a genuine institution list. **China's jsj.moe.gov.cn** (教育部教育涉外监管信息网) publishes a reference list of 10,000+ recognised foreign universities by country. Source: https://jsj.moe.gov.cn/.
- **Assess case by case (advisory):** In most European countries, higher-education institutions decide which foreign qualifications to accept, so most NARICs have an advisory role and issue individual statements of comparability rather than blanket institution lists. UK ENIC (operated by Ecctis) issues statements of comparability case by case. Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Academic_Recognition_Information_Centre; https://www.enic-naric.net/page-academic-recognition.

**Concrete examples of national centres publishing institution lists:** (1) Germany — anabin (H+/H−); (2) China — jsj.moe.gov.cn foreign-university list; (3) Norway's NOKUT and the Netherlands' Nuffic maintain country/institution reference materials and automatic-recognition lists (candidates to verify on their national portals — flagged as not directly confirmed during this research).

## Recommendations
1. **Build the recommender's "hard-gate" money layer first on the five countries with published statutory thresholds** (UK, Germany, France, Korea, Hungary). Encode: UK £1,171/£1,529 × 9 months + tuition + 28-day rule (rates effective 11 Nov 2025); Germany €992/month = €11,904/year Sperrkonto (in force 2025–2026); France €877.50/month (indexed to 47% SMIC — re-scrape on each SMIC revision); Korea ₩20m/₩16m. For Hungary, treat subsistence as "proof required, no single published figure." Flag Japan (~¥2m convention) and China (no threshold) as "no fixed amount — university-set/unpublished."
2. **Treat scholarship = visa-money waiver as a first-class rule.** For DAAD, French government (MEAE), GKS/KGSP, CSC, Chevening, Türkiye Bursları and Stipendium Hungaricum, the award substitutes for financial proof; surface this prominently since it materially changes eligibility.
3. **Do not promise per-programme competition ratios for internationals** — the data does not exist as clean national datasets. Instead surface capacity (France monmaster capacité), published entry bars (UK/Germany/Turkey/Hungary), and label Japan/China/Korea as exam-gated or per-university.
4. **Use the UK Vis_D02/Vis_04 datasets as the model for a "visa-risk-by-nationality" feature**, and clearly label the other seven countries as "refusal-by-nationality not published" (China, Turkey grants-only; Hungary Eurostat-only; Germany/France/Japan/Korea aggregate/unconfirmed).
5. **For the accreditation/recognition backbone, ingest the two machine-readable registers** (UK OfS Register CSV; France RNCP via data.gouv.fr) programmatically, and treat Germany Hochschulkompass/anabin, Japan MEXT, Korea academyinfo, China jsj.moe.gov.cn, Turkey YÖK, Hungary FIR as scrape-and-cache human-readable sources. Wire the ENIC-NARIC country pages as the canonical "recognition back home" pointer.
6. **Benchmarks that would change these recommendations:** the UK's 1 Jan 2027 Graduate-route cut taking effect (switch post-study duration to 18 months); a Korean HiKorea notice formally confirming the 3-year D-10 cap (upgrade from "documented" to "official-URL-backed"); a French SMIC revision (recompute the 47% threshold); any of Japan/Korea/China beginning to publish by-nationality refusal data (add visa-risk features).

## Caveats
- **Local-language official pages dominate for five countries** (Japan MOJ/MEXT, China MOE, Korea 법무부/immigration, Turkey Göç İdaresi/CSGB, Hungary OIF). Several data points were corroborated through official-but-secondary channels (embassy/consulate pages, préfecture pages, city Welcome Centers) rather than the top-level ministry page; these are flagged inline.
- **Figures flagged as agency/commercial-sourced or not confirmed on the primary ministry page:** Korea's ₩20m/₩16m split (found on an official visa-application-center page, not studyinkorea.go.kr/법무부); Hungary's ~€500–600/month subsistence (study-abroad sites); the superseded Stipendium Hungaricum acceptance-rate figures. Japan's ~¥2m and the German 2026 Sperrkonto amount are confirmed in substance (the €992 figure is tied to the BAföG rate and documented as applying in 2026) but the exact 2026 living-cost line should be re-verified on auswaertiges-amt.de before publication.
- **Exam-gating vs. rule-based is a spectrum**, and even "rule-based" systems (UK, Germany) involve institutional discretion; the classifications above describe the dominant mode, not an absolute.
- **Deposits, admission-difficulty data, and refusal-by-nationality data are the three areas where the question's framing most often mismatches reality** — Germany/France have no deposit culture at public universities; competition ratios for internationals are essentially unpublished nationally; and only the UK cleanly publishes refusal rates by nationality.
- **Future-policy claims** (UK Graduate-route reduction, French SMIC-indexed threshold, Korea D-10 extension) depend on implementation and indexation and should be re-checked each cycle; as of 27 August 2026 the UK Graduate route is still 2 years, the Korea D-10 3-year cap is in force (from 29 Oct 2025), and the French €877.50 threshold is in force (from 1 Aug 2026).
