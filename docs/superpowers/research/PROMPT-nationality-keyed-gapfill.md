# Deep research prompt — nationality-keyed schemes, gap-fill pass

**Status:** ready to run. Written 2026-08-29. **Run once**, not twice — this is a targeted
verification pass over a closed list, not an open search, so a second independent run buys much less
than it does for a discovery prompt.

**Why this exists.** Two independent runs of `PROMPT-nationality-keyed-schemes.md` (filed as
`2026-08-29-nationality-keyed-schemes-review-1.md` and `-review-2.md`) between them found about ten
schemes the prompt never named — Heydar Aliyev Turkic World, Orhun, AGBU, Bolashak, Luys, the
OIF/FRQ bourse, Banach NAWA, Calouste Gulbenkian. **This run does not repeat that search.** It
closes four specific holes both runs left.

Do not re-derive what those runs established. Do not restate the Visegrad, CEEPUS, Czech Government,
Stipendium Hungaricum, IsDB, Heydar Aliyev, Orhun, AGBU, Bolashak, Banach or Gulbenkian findings.

## Prompt

Four tasks. They are independent — if budget runs short, complete tasks 1 and 2 fully rather than
touching all four thinly.

Throughout, the population is: **Azerbaijan and Türkiye first and in depth**, then Georgia, Armenia,
Kazakhstan, Uzbekistan, Kyrgyzstan, Tajikistan and Turkmenistan. Master's level only — not doctoral,
not postdoctoral, not undergraduate.

### Task 1 — Derive the list of funders, then check it

**Do not work from a list of scheme names. Build the list first, from primary sources, and publish
it.** Prior runs were given named bodies to check and their coverage stopped at that list. The
enumeration is therefore a deliverable in its own right, before any scheme is researched — it makes
coverage auditable, showing what was *considered* and not only what was found.

#### Step 1a — Enumerate by membership

For **Azerbaijan and Türkiye first**, then Georgia, Armenia, Kazakhstan, Uzbekistan, Kyrgyzstan,
Tajikistan and Turkmenistan: list every **intergovernmental organisation, multilateral body,
development bank and treaty framework** of which the country is a **member, observer, associate or
dialogue partner**.

Build this from primary sources — each country's ministry of foreign affairs publishes its
memberships, and each organisation publishes its member list. **Give a source for every membership
claim.** This is a finite, citable set, not a judgement call.

Include political, economic, cultural, religious and regional bodies alike. A body with no obvious
education mandate may still run a scholarship, and one with an education mandate may not.

#### Step 1b — Enumerate by who has a reason to pay

Membership lists miss funders that are not organisations our countries belong to. For each motive
below, ask **who fits it** for our populations, and name them:

- a **destination government** courting this nationality specifically, through a bilateral or
  regional programme rather than an open scheme;
- an **affinity body** — ethnic, religious, linguistic or historical — that funds people of this
  origin regardless of where they live;
- a **development bank or donor** lending to or operating in these countries;
- a **diaspora organisation** of the relevant community;
- a **foundation or endowment** with a stated regional focus on the Caucasus, Central Asia, the
  Turkic world, the Black Sea region, the Silk Road, or the Islamic world.

This axis is what surfaces bodies no membership taxonomy would reach. The Calouste Gulbenkian
Foundation — an Armenian-affinity endowment based in Portugal — was found by one prior run and would
appear on no list of organisations Armenia belongs to.

#### Step 1c — Enumerate from the origin side

Invert the question. Rather than asking which bodies fund, ask **what foreign scholarship
opportunities the origin countries themselves have announced.**

Search the announcement and news pages of **Azerbaijan's State Agency for Science and Higher
Education and Ministry of Science and Education**, and **Türkiye's YTB and Ministry of National
Education**, for foreign scholarship opportunities publicised over roughly the last three years.
Do the same for the other priority countries if budget allows.

This finds schemes by what actually reaches students, independently of any organisational taxonomy,
and it will surface bilateral arrangements that appear nowhere else.

#### Step 1d — Now check each body found

For every body from 1a, 1b and 1c, establish whether it runs a **master's-level scheme our
nationalities can access**. Where it does, give: scheme name, funder, verbatim eligible
nationalities, destinations, degree level confirmed, **application route (`DIRECT` / `NOMINATED` /
`HYBRID`, with the operative sentence quoted)**, coverage, annual cycle, official URL, and whether
the site states reuse or access terms.

**Where a body runs no master's scheme, record it as a confirmed absence with the page that
establishes it.** A checked-and-empty body is a result: it stops a later run repeating the work.

#### Step 1e — Six we already suspect: a safety net, not the scope

Check these **regardless of whether steps 1a–1c surfaced them**. They are listed last, deliberately,
so they cannot anchor the enumeration. If the derived list already contains them, say so — that is
useful evidence the derivation is working.

1. **Joint Japan/World Bank Graduate Scholarship Program (JJ/WBGSP)** — master's-specific, aimed at
   developing-country nationals. Establish the current eligible-country list and whether our
   countries appear on it, the eligible-programme list, and the application route.
2. **Swedish Institute** scholarships (SI Scholarships for Global Professionals and any successor) —
   these publish an eligible-country list; give it verbatim and say which of our countries appear.
3. **Baltic bilateral and state scholarships** — Lithuania, Latvia and Estonia each run state
   scholarship schemes and Eastern Partnership programmes. Cover each separately.
4. **Erasmus+ International Credit Mobility (ICM) partner-country strands**, and any Erasmus+
   regional window covering the Eastern Partnership, Central Asia or Türkiye. Establish whether a
   student applies through their home institution and whether allocations are per-country.
   *(Erasmus Mundus Joint Masters is already held and is out of scope.)*
5. **EBRD** — any scholarship or graduate programme, and its eligibility.
6. **UNESCO** and **UNDP** fellowship or scholarship programmes at master's level.

**If the derived list and this list disagree in size, the derived list is the one that matters.**
Six names are a floor on coverage, never a ceiling.

### Task 2 — Verify a contested absence

Run 2 concluded that scholars from the Caucasus, Central Asia and Türkiye **cannot** access the
**Asian Development Bank Japan Scholarship Program (ADB-JSP)**. Its supporting citation is an ADB
*partnership report* describing a climate-financing tranche restricted to "the Pacific and Southeast
Asia" — a document about a different instrument.

**Establish the actual ADB-JSP position from ADB's own scholarship pages:**

- The current **eligible-country list** for ADB-JSP, verbatim.
- Whether Azerbaijan, Georgia, Armenia, Kazakhstan, Kyrgyzstan, Tajikistan, Turkmenistan and
  Uzbekistan appear on it. All are ADB members; membership and JSP eligibility are not the same
  thing, so check the scholarship's own list rather than the membership list.
- Türkiye's status specifically — it is not an ADB member, so confirm whether that excludes it.
- The participating institutions, the application route, and what the award covers.

**Report whichever answer the primary source gives.** If run 2 was right, say so and give the clause
that establishes it — a confirmed absence sourced to the correct document is a good outcome. If it
was wrong, ADB-JSP is a significant addition for most of the priority population.

### Task 3 — Nationality-keyed tracks inside the big schemes

Both prior runs excluded the large destination-inbound schemes wholesale. That over-applied the
exclusion: **a general scheme's nationality-keyed track is in scope**, even though its open track is
not.

For each of the following, establish whether it operates **per-country quotas, country-specific
tracks, bilateral allocations, or nationality-restricted sub-programmes**, and reproduce any quota
table verbatim for our priority countries:

- **Türkiye Bursları** — country and regional programme structure, and any allocation published per
  country.
- **Global Korea Scholarship** — the **embassy track's per-country quotas**, which are published in
  the annual guideline.
- **DAAD** — country-specific and regional programmes, including any bilateral scheme with our
  countries. DAAD's general database is out of scope; its nationality-keyed programmes are not.
- **Chinese Government Scholarship** — bilateral programme and any Turkic-state or Silk Road
  allocation.
- **Stipendium Hungaricum** — one open question from run 1: whether any **Sending Partner**
  publishes a per-country number on its own national site, even though Hungary does not publish
  quotas centrally. Check the Azerbaijani State Agency for Science and Higher Education, Georgia's
  International Education Center, and Armenia's Ministry of Education directly.

**Where a quota is published only by the sending country and not the funder, say so explicitly** and
attribute it to the sending-side source. That asymmetry is itself a useful finding.

### Task 4 — Türkiye, in depth

Türkiye is a launch market and both prior runs covered it thinly, mostly as a *destination* rather
than as a population. For a **Turkish citizen seeking master's funding abroad**, establish what
nationality-keyed schemes exist beyond IsDB membership and Stipendium Hungaricum Sending-Partner
status.

Cover at least: **YTB** (Yurtdışı Türkler ve Akraba Topluluklar Başkanlığı) programmes other than
Türkiye Bursları, including the YTB–IsDB joint programme run 1 mentioned in passing but did not
develop; **TÜBİTAK** international programmes at master's level; Turkish professional chambers,
foundations and holding-company scholarships (Koç, Sabancı, Vehbi Koç, TEV — Türk Eğitim Vakfı)
that fund study abroad; and any bilateral scheme between Türkiye and another state that funds
Turkish citizens' master's study.

*(Türkiye's outbound state provision — YLSY — is covered by
`PROMPT-government-outbound-launch.md`. Note its existence in one line and do not develop it here.)*

### Sourcing rules

1. **Primary sources only for any load-bearing claim** — the funder's own page or its published
   call. This run exists partly because a conclusion was drawn from the wrong document; do not
   repeat that.
2. **Quote the operative sentence** for eligibility, application route and degree level.
3. **Date every fact**, and give the cycle year it belongs to.
4. **Where something is not published, say so.** Do not infer eligibility from membership of an
   organisation — the ADB case is exactly this error.
5. **A confirmed absence is a result.** If a body runs no master's scheme, say so and cite the page
   that establishes it.
6. Deliver **Task 1 and Task 3 as tables** with the columns named above, not as prose. The prior
   runs' most encodable output was tabular.

### What would change our plans

Flag explicitly: **ADB-JSP being open to our nationalities**, which would add a major full-cost
option; any published per-country quota table inside Türkiye Bursları, GKS or DAAD; any `DIRECT`
full-cost scheme for Turkish citizens, which is the population both prior runs served worst; and any
scheme found here that is both `DIRECT` and full-degree, since only IsDB currently qualifies.
