# Diffbot and Perplexity — terms and economics before a key is spent

Read live 2026-09-17, before either provider is integrated. Neither could be called: no Diffbot
token exists, and the Perplexity key returns **HTTP 401 `insufficient_quota`**. What follows is
documentation and contract only — nothing here is a measurement.

## 1. Diffbot — the best terms of any paid provider surveyed, and the worst unit economics for our main job

**Terms** (`diffbot.com/company/terms/`). There is an **explicit grant**, which no other paid
provider gives us: *"you may display and use the facts generated by the Service as part of a
commercial application."* And then, checked one by one: **no** definitions section, **no** storage,
caching or retention clause, **no** derivative-database or competing-index prohibition, **no**
model-training clause, **no** automated-decision or high-risk clause. The restriction list is about
the Service, not its output: no sublicensing or reselling, no unlawful use, no reverse engineering,
no bypassing robot-exclusion headers.

That is a stronger position than Tavily's (whose grant is structural — "Services" excludes
"Output") and far stronger than Brave's or Exa's. Two cautions stand:

- **The document carries no effective or last-updated date.** An undated contract can change
  without a visible diff, so the text must be re-read and quoted into this repo before each use,
  not relied on from memory.
- **"Use the API or the Data in any manner that violates the rights of any person"** is the one
  clause that bites us, because the Knowledge Graph contains **people** as entities. We would query
  organisations only. That intent must be enforced in code (`type:Organization` and nothing else),
  not left to convention, and it is the same discipline the repo already applies to student data.

**What it actually offers.** `GET https://kg.diffbot.com/kg/v3/dql?token=…&query=…&size=…&filter=…`
DQL is a real query language over an entity graph: `type:Organization locations.city.name:"San
Francisco" nbEmployees>5000`, JSONPath field selection (`$.location.country.name`), `size=-1` for
all results, `from+size <= 10_000` on facet queries, and `csv`/`xlsx` export. Organisation entities
carry `name`, `homepageUri`, `nbEmployees`, `location`/`locations`, `description`.

**The economics decide it.** From `diffbot.com/pricing`:

| action | credits |
|---|---|
| Knowledge Graph — export 1 entity record | **25** |
| Knowledge Graph — 1 facet query record | 100 |
| Web Search — run 1 search | **1** |
| Extract 1 page | 1 |

Free plan: ~9,500–10,000 credits/month, no card, 5 requests/min. Startup $299/mo for 250,000;
overage $0.001/credit.

So **one organisation record costs 25 credits ≈ $0.025**, and the free tier is **~380 organisation
records a month**. Enumerating Türkiye's universities through Diffbot would cost roughly 230 × 25 =
5,750 credits — most of a month's free allowance — for a list **Wikidata returns for free under
CC0** (229 with a website, measured 2026-09-17), and DEQAR returns again under PDDL (234).
Diffbot cannot win the enumeration job on price against a register that is free.

**Two hypotheses it could win, both testable inside the free tier:**

1. **Funders no register holds.** `type:Organization` filtered to foundation/nonprofit categories
   and a country, for the classes the 2026-09-17 probe found split across four search indexes
   (Turkish *vakıf* foundations, Azerbaijani bodies). 380 free records is a real sample.
2. **Host → organisation resolution.** §4.1's register needs a name and a kind for a bare hostname;
   DQL can be queried by `homepageUri`. Today that costs a fetch plus a model call per host
   (§4.7). Whether 25 credits beats that is an arithmetic question worth one measurement.

**And a third, unexpected:** Diffbot sells **web search at 1 credit — about $0.001 per query**,
five times cheaper than Brave's $5/1,000 and seven times cheaper than Exa's $7/1,000. Its terms
cover it with the same explicit grant. If its index is any good for the no-index families, it is
the cheapest terms-clean search in the roster. Untested.

## 2. Perplexity — still unpurchased, and the clock matters

The key returns 401 `insufficient_quota`; Perplexity is prepaid pay-as-you-go with no free tier, so
a probe needs a top-up (a ~$5 balance covers a 20-query Search API run many times over; one
`sonar-deep-research` request in Perplexity's own sample cost $0.82).

Its terms are the weaker half of the pair, and unchanged from the 2026-09-16 survey: the Search API
Addendum defines *"Output"* as responses returned by the Search Service, states the customer **does
not own** it, lets Perplexity retain and reuse queries, and forbids personal data in queries; the
AUP bars high-risk automated decisions and adds a blanket EU-AI-Act clause. Nothing bars storage —
the position rests on silence over a non-owned object, which is exactly the posture `CLAUDE.md`
calls insufficient on its own.

**Sonar chat completions are supported only until 27 September 2026** — ten days from this note —
with the Agent API as successor. So anything built against Sonar today targets a sunset endpoint.
The **Search API** (`POST /search`, up to 5 queries per request, `country`,
`search_language_filter`, a 20-entry domain allow/deny list where a root domain matches all
subdomains) is not affected and is the only part worth probing.

## 3. Recommendation

Both are worth a probe and neither is worth a purchase yet.

- **Diffbot: create a free account** (no card, ~10,000 credits/month) and probe the two hypotheses
  above plus its web search, inside the free tier. Decide on the measurement.
- **Perplexity: a ~$5 top-up**, probe the Search API only, on the same 20-query corpus as the
  2026-09-17 four-provider probe so the numbers are comparable. Ignore Sonar.
- **Neither enters the roster on documentation.** The four-provider probe is the bar: origin-host
  share, novel hosts, cards fillable. A provider that cannot beat a free CC0 register at its own
  job does not get wired in because its contract is friendly.

## 4. Diffbot, measured — 2026-09-17, key in hand

Free tier. Real limits, from `/docs/rate-limits`: **400 DQL entities per month, 5 calls per minute**
(the "9,500 credits" on the pricing page is the same allowance, since an entity export is 25
credits). So counts were bought before entities: `size=1` returns `hits`, the full match count, for
one entity.

**What the graph holds.** `isNonProfit` is a first-class field, and DQL has comparison, range,
`not`, `or`, `has`, `contains`, `near`, `regex`, **`similarTo`**, facet queries and custom scoring.

| DQL query | hits |
|---|---|
| `type:Organization isNonProfit:true location.country.name:"Germany"` | **193,523** |
| `type:Organization location.country.name:"Germany" name:"Stiftung"` | **20,603** |
| …`isNonProfit:true` + `description:"scholarship"` | 108 |
| …`isNonProfit:true` + `description:"Stipendium"` | 29 |
| Turkey, `isNonProfit:true` | 81,275 |
| Azerbaijan, `isNonProfit:true` | 2,596 |

**The coverage is real and the funding signal is not.** 20,603 German *Stiftungen* is the long tail
no register holds — and only 137 of 193,523 German nonprofits mention a scholarship in their
Diffbot description. The graph knows the **organisation**, never what it funds. So Diffbot cannot
shortcut the expensive half: it would hand us 20,603 candidates and the reader still has to
establish which of them award anything.

**The set-difference test.** All 137 entities fetched (`$.name;$.homepageUri;$.summary`) and diffed
against the 353 German funders from `2026-09-17-cell-saturation-measurement.md`. 127 carried a
usable homepage domain; **26 overlapped** the sweep; 101 were new; of those, a single model pass
classified **28 as actually funding students (28%)**, 24 of them German or DACH.

**Marginal contribution: +24 on 353, a 7% lift — and two of the 24 matter a great deal.**

`aufstiegsstipendium.de` is a **federal German scholarship programme**, and `zeit-stiftung.de` is
ZEIT-Stiftung Bucerius, one of the country's larger foundations. Ninety-nine queries across four
engines with twelve German term families missed both. Also new: Otto Benecke Stiftung (`obs-ev.de`),
`zis-reisen.de`, `manchot.org`, `ruetgers-stiftung.de`, `klingler-stiftung.de`,
`knyphausen-stiftung.org`, `humanistische-stiftung.de`, the Karajan-Akademie and Live Music Now
(the `TALENT` eligibility key), `msjobstipendium.de` (industrial sponsorship), `tumfast.de` and
`manageandmore.de` (programme-attached), `uwcrobertboschcollege.de`, `dlgs-dresden.de`.

That the sweep missed a *federal programme* is the finding worth keeping. It says the sweep's
vocabulary has holes no amount of querying the same four indexes will close, and that a source with
a different bias — entity and business data rather than search ranking — closes some of them.

**Cost comparison, stated honestly.** The sweep: ~$2 of calls for 353 funders, about $0.006 each.
Diffbot: 137 entities (a third of the monthly free allowance; ~$3.43 at overage) for 24 new
funders, about $0.14 each — roughly twenty times dearer per funder. But the units are not
interchangeable: ZEIT-Stiftung and a federal programme are not the marginal 24th result of a
keyword sweep.

**Verdict: a standing complementary query per country, never a spine.** One `isNonProfit` +
`description:"<scholarship word in the local language>"` query per country per cycle is a few dozen
entities and yields a small, high-value supplement. It is *not* a route to enumerating foundations —
193,523 German nonprofits is not a candidate list anyone can read — and its 28% precision means
every hit still goes through the classifier and the reader like any other lead.

**Untested, and probably the better use: `similarTo`.** Feeding a known funder's entity and asking
the graph for similar organisations is the one operation neither search nor a register offers. About
250 entities of this month's allowance remain; that is the next test.
