# East Asian Graduate Admissions Data & Korean Pre-Enrolment Payments

## TL;DR
- **Q1 — scholarship numbers:** All three schemes publish an award *count* but no applicant count and no selection rate, so no true rate can be computed; GKS is the strongest — the official 2026 guideline gives **2,000 graduate awards with per-country quotas** — while MEXT and CSC publish only aggregate recipient/enrolment figures. No university publishes a matched international applicant-vs-admitted pair for master's. National datasets (e-Stat, KEDI/대학알리미, China MOE) are enrolment *statistical* series, not admissions-competition series.
- **Q2 — Korean pre-enrolment money:** Both earlier accounts are right about different things. **입학금 (entrance fee) is abolished for undergraduates from 2023 but graduate schools are legally excluded**; the real pre-enrolment commitment is the **full first semester's tuition, which must be paid before the Certificate of Admission (표준입학허가서) is issued** for a self-funded international student, and which is **refundable if the visa is denied** (published by Korea University and SKKU).
- **Encoding decision:** **No national figure can be encoded.** The prepayment is the full first-semester tuition, set per university and college — verify per university. Nationally consistent facts: entrance fee gone for undergrad; full tuition due before the COA; refund on visa refusal.

## Key Findings

**Question 1**
- **1a — university-level international applicant/admit counts: essentially none.** Korean 신입생 경쟁률 (freshman competition ratio) on 대학알리미 is institution-wide, not split into an international-applicant vs international-admitted pair. Korea University's own Spring 2026 international-track guide lists 모집인원 (quota) as "제한없음" (no fixed number) for most units — so there is no published admit denominator at all. Japan's 倍率 and China's 报录比 are domestic/aggregate. No university publishes a matched international applicant/admitted pair for master's.
- **1b — funder numbers:** GKS publishes award counts by nationality but no applicant count or rate; MEXT and CSC publish only recipient/enrolment aggregates; none publishes a selection rate. A numerator (awards) exists without a denominator (applications) in all three cases.
- **1c — national datasets exist but are the wrong *kind*.** e-Stat (Japan), KEDI/대학알리미 and data.go.kr (Korea), and moe.gov.cn (China) all publish foreign-student counts by nationality, but as **enrolment/stock statistical series**, not admissions-competition series.

**Question 2**
- No national rule sets a prepayment amount; the requirement flows from **Ministry of Justice immigration rules** (which require proof of tuition payment for the D-2 visa / COA) plus each **university's registration schedule**.
- **입학금** is abolished for undergraduate entry from the 2023 academic year under 고등교육법 Art. 11(2), **but graduate degree/research programmes are explicitly excluded** from the ban.
- The genuine pre-enrolment commitment for a self-funded international graduate student is **full first-semester tuition, paid before the 표준입학허가서 is issued**, refundable if the visa is refused.
- If a tuition-covering scholarship is confirmed **before** billing, it is simply **deducted from the bill (감면고지)** — no prepayment problem. If confirmed **after** payment, the standard mechanism is **cash disbursement / refund**, not forfeiture.

## Details

### Question 1 — published admission and award numbers

**1a. International applicant/admitted breakdowns for master's — essentially none.**
Korean universities publish 신입생 경쟁률 on 대학알리미 (Academyinfo), but the indicator is aggregate at institution level and is not disaggregated into an international-applicant vs international-admitted pair for master's programmes. Korea University's own graduate/undergraduate international application guide (Spring 2026, oia.korea.ac.kr) sets 모집인원 as "제한없음" (no fixed quota) for most international-track units — meaning there is not even a published admit-quota denominator. Japanese universities publish 倍率 per faculty and Chinese universities publish 报录比 for domestic 考研, but neither splits out foreign applicants. I located no university — among SNU, Yonsei, Korea, KAIST, SKKU or others surveyed — that publishes a matched international applicant/admitted count for master's programmes. **Answer: essentially none do.**

**1b. Scholarship schemes — which number is published.**

- **MEXT (Japan).** MEXT publishes annual recruitment guidelines (募集要項) on mext.go.jp but does **not** release applicant or successful-applicant counts for the scholarship. Tokyo Metropolitan University's official page states "採用者数はいずれも非常に少なくなっています" (the number selected is very small) with no figure; Waseda's official page confirms university-recommended numbers are set by fixed quota. The only concrete count is an enrolment stock: **9,223 MEXT scholarship recipients were studying in Japan as of May 1, 2015** (with roughly 102,000 MEXT scholars total since 1954, from about 160 countries), per the Embassy of Japan in the United States. That is an enrolment stock, not an award-per-year or applicant figure. **Published: recipient/enrolment stock only; no applicant count; no rate.**

- **GKS / Global Korea Scholarship (Korea).** NIIED publishes award quotas in its official application guidelines on studyinkorea.go.kr. For **2026 GKS-Graduate (GKS-G)**, the official notice states the government "plans to invite **2,000** international students through the Global Korea Scholarship (GKS) Program for graduate degrees," split into **Embassy Track 800** (General 776, Overseas Korean/Adoptee 24) and **University Track 1,200** (General 160, R-GKS 480, Research 5, International Organization 72, R&D 403, Global Network 80). **Awards are published by nationality** — the guideline lists per-country quotas (e.g., China 31, Vietnam 33, Indonesia 33, USA 16 in Embassy General). **No applicant count and no competition rate (경쟁률) appears anywhere in the official guideline**, which states that "the reasons for passing or failing at each stage of the screening process will not be disclosed." **Published: award counts, broken down by nationality; no applicant count; no rate.** GKS is therefore the only one of the three funders that gives a usable, nationality-resolved award-scarcity signal — but it still yields no true selection rate.

- **CSC / Chinese Government Scholarship (China).** China's MOE publishes annual inbound-student and government-scholarship *recipient* totals. For 2014 (released 2015-03-18) MOE reported **377,054 international students, of whom 36,943 (9.80%) held Chinese Government Scholarships** and 340,111 (90.20%) were self-funded; degree students totalled 164,394, including 35,876 master's and 12,114 doctoral students. More recent per-year CSC award or applicant figures are not published by MOE/CSC; third-party estimates range widely (Georgetown's CSET issue brief and study-abroad guides cite figures from ~30,000 to "more than 60,000 awards per year," all **unofficial**). **Published: aggregate recipient totals; no CSC applicant count; no rate.**

**1c. National statistical / ministry datasets.**
- **Japan — e-Stat 学校基本調査 (School Basic Survey).** Table 24, "国籍・地域別 専攻分野別 外国人学生数（大学院）" (foreign graduate students by nationality and field), updated 2024-12-18 (令和6年度). URL: `https://www.e-stat.go.jp/stat-search/database?query=学校基本調査&layout=dataset&statdisp_id=0003194360`. This is a **statistical (enrolment stock) series**, not admissions-competition. There is a companion table for 国費留学生 (government-scholarship students) by nationality/field (statdisp_id=0003017238), also an enrolment count.
- **Korea — KEDI / 대학알리미 / data.go.kr.** KEDI's "외국인 유학생 현황(대학)" dataset (`https://www.data.go.kr/data/15050054/fileData.do`) and the Ministry of Justice's monthly "외국인 유학생 국적(지역)별 현황" D-2/D-4 series (`https://www.data.go.kr/data/15100039/fileData.do`) are **statistical (enrolment/residence stock) series**. 대학알리미 (`https://www.academyinfo.go.kr`) publishes 신입생 경쟁률 as an institutional indicator, but not by applicant nationality. No admissions-competition-by-nationality series exists.
- **China — MOE (moe.gov.cn).** MOE publishes 来华留学 annual statistics (`http://www.moe.gov.cn/jyb_xxgk/xxgk/neirong/tongji/jytj_lxsj/`) and higher-education enrolment tables (postgraduate entrants/enrolment). These are **statistical series**; no admissions-competition-by-nationality series is published.

**What remains unknown (Q1):** No true international-applicant selection rate is computable for graduate admissions or for MEXT/GKS/CSC, because applicant denominators are unpublished. GKS graduate award counts with per-country quotas are the only usable funder numbers, but with no applicant count they cannot produce a rate.

### Question 2 — money committed before enrolling at a Korean university

**2a. Is there a national rule?**
There is **no Higher Education Act provision or MOE regulation setting a mandatory pre-enrolment deposit amount.** 고등교육법 Article 11 governs 등록금 (tuition/registration fees) and the 입학금 ban (see 2c), but sets no pre-enrolment deposit figure. The Certificate of Admission (표준입학허가서) is a Ministry of Justice immigration document required for the D-2 visa; universities issue it only after the admitted student registers and pays, because immigration requires proof of tuition payment (학비 납입 영수증) in the COA/visa package. Korea University's Korean-language-centre page lists the visa document set as "표준입학허가서, 학비 납입 영수증, 기관 사업자등록증." **The prepayment requirement therefore comes from immigration document rules plus each university's registration schedule — it is set per university, not by a national rule fixing an amount.**

**2b. What the five named universities publish (own pages):**
- **Seoul National University (SNU).** Admitted students must pay tuition during the designated registration period before the semester; unregistered admits are dropped ("미등록 제적"). SNU's registration page (`en.snu.ac.kr/academics/resources/registration`) states it is "the university's policy that students are responsible for registration and paying tuition fees by the due date. The university may deny registration to those students … who fail to do this." The Certificate of Admission for international students is issued after registration. Amount = full semester tuition (varies by college). **Full tuition at registration, before COA/visa.**
- **Yonsei.** The official Fall 2026 International Student Application Guide (`www2.yonsei.ac.kr`) states: "Successful applicants must pay the full amount of tuition during the designated registration period." Graduate and One-Stop-Service pages confirm the COA is issued only after full tuition payment — "Freshmen will receive the guideline email after full payment of tuition" (`gosc.yonsei.ac.kr`) and "등록금을 납부하신 분에 한해 표준입학허가서를 발급해드릴 수 있습니다" (the COA is issued only to those who have paid tuition). **Full semester tuition, before COA.**
- **Korea University.** The Spring 2026 international freshman guide (`oia.korea.ac.kr`) states registration is complete only when the full tuition is deposited into the assigned virtual account ("등록금 전액을 입금한 경우에만 등록이 완료됩니다"), with the visa sequence 추가 서류 제출 및 등록금 전액 납부 → 표준입학허가서 다운로드/발급. **Full tuition before COA.**
- **KAIST.** KAIST issues the Certificate of Admission and requires a receipt showing tuition has been paid as part of the D-2 document set (COA + Business Registration Certificate + proof of tuition payment). Many KAIST graduate students hold full scholarships covering tuition, reducing the billed amount. **Tuition (or a scholarship-covered zero bill) before COA/visa.**
- **Sungkyunkwan University (SKKU).** SKKU's admitted-student guide sequence is: submit original documents → pay tuition (a deposit/예치금 then main registration/본등록) → receive Certificate of Admission (COA applied for only after tuition payment). SKKU's Korean Language Center page states plainly: "We refund tuition to a student who receives disapproval in visa application." **Full tuition before COA; refundable on visa denial.**

**Refund on visa refusal — confirmed.** Korea University's Korean-centre registration page states: "비자 불허 시 개강 전날까지 취소 신청서를 작성하여 제출하면 전형료를 제외한 수업료 전액이 환불됩니다" (on visa denial, if a cancellation request is filed before the day before the semester starts, the full tuition minus the application/screening fee is refunded). SKKU's language-centre page likewise refunds tuition on visa disapproval. **The "prepay, refundable if the visa is denied" model from the second earlier research run is confirmed.**

**2c. Current status of 입학금 (entrance fee).**
**Complete abolition for undergraduate entrance from the 2023 academic year, but graduate schools are legally excluded.** 고등교육법 Article 11(2), amended in 2019, states a school's founder/operator "cannot receive an entrance fee (입학금) from a person entering or transferring into the school," but explicitly **excludes graduate degree/research programmes** (Article 29 학위과정·연구과정) and 대학원대학 (graduate-only universities under Article 30). The addendum states: "제11조제2항의 개정규정은 2023학년도 이후 학교에 입학 또는 편입학하는 사람부터 적용" (the entrance-fee prohibition applies to those entering from the 2023 academic year). National/public universities abolished 입학금 from 2018; private universities cut it 20% per year 2018–2022, fully gone by 2023. Dated reporting (경향신문, 2022-12-03; 전자신문, 2019-08-26) confirms the graduate-school carve-out: "대학원은 … 입학금 폐지 대상에서 제외됐다."

**Practical effect:** For undergraduate entry, 입학금 no longer exists. For graduate entry, universities may still legally charge it, but from the 2023 abolition many folded the residual entrance-fee cost into first-semester tuition — SKKU's 2022 tuition-committee minutes (skku.edu) note "입학금 폐지 조치가 시행됨에 따라 입학금 실비용을 … 첫 학기 수업료에 산입하여 납부." **Both earlier accounts are thus partly correct:** 입학금 is abolished (undergrad; folded into tuition where retained), and the real pre-enrolment commitment is the full first-semester tuition — roughly an order of magnitude larger than a former entrance fee (entrance fees historically averaged ₩637,000 vs. semester tuition typically ₩6–9 million).

**2d. If a tuition-covering scholarship is awarded later — refunded, credited, or kept?**
Depends on timing relative to billing, and only partly published:
- **Scholarship known before the bill:** deducted from the bill (**감면고지**); the student pays only the remainder — no prepayment to refund. SKKU's official scholarship page states scholarships are "paid as deductions in tuition-statements … they only need to pay what remains after the deduction" / "감면고지 … 그 차액만 납부하도록 고지." This is standard for internal/university scholarships (STEM, president fellowships, etc.).
- **Scholarship confirmed after payment** (later award, recipient change, or an external scholarship): SKKU directs disbursement as a **cash payment into the student's bank account** ("현금지급(계좌입금 또는 장학금 증서 교부) … 대외장학금을 지급하는 경우"), i.e. credited/refunded, not kept.
- **GKS specifically:** GKS tuition is paid by NIIED directly to the university, and GKS selection is normally confirmed before enrolment, so GKS scholars register with tuition already covered rather than self-paying and reclaiming. **Whether a self-funded student who prepays and is *later* awarded GKS is reimbursed is not published as a single national rule.**

## Recommendations
1. **For the likely/target/reach ranking (Q1):** Use GKS graduate award counts (2,000 total for 2026, with per-country quotas) as the only funder "scarcity" input, and label it explicitly as a **numerator with no denominator** — do not compute or imply a selection rate. Do not attempt a rate for MEXT or CSC; encode only their published recipient/enrolment aggregates with the year attached. For university-level graduate admissions, encode "no published international applicant/admit rate" rather than a proxy.
2. **For Korea money-commitment encoding (Q2):** Do **not** encode a single national figure. Encode the rule instead: *"Full first-semester tuition due at registration, before the 표준입학허가서 is issued; refundable if the visa is refused; amount varies by university/college."* Pull the specific tuition figure per university from each institution's 등록금 일람표 (e.g., korea.ac.kr/ko/582/subview.do; snu.ac.kr tuition page).
3. **Scholarship-timing decision rule:** If a scholarship result is known **before** a university's registration deadline, the student pays only the reduced (감면고지) balance and never fronts full tuition. If the result comes **after** the deadline, assume the student must **pay full tuition first**, then rely on the university's cash-disbursement/refund process — treat this as a cash-flow risk, not a loss.
4. **Benchmarks that would change the recommendation:** (a) If any university begins publishing a matched international applicant/admit count for master's, switch that program's ranking basis from prose to a computed rate. (b) If NIIED begins publishing GKS applicant counts or a 경쟁률, compute and encode a real selection rate. (c) If a targeted university's own page states scholarship-driven refunds are *not* granted (kept), flag that program as a prepayment-loss risk.

## Caveats
- **GKS 2026 figures** come from the official studyinkorea.go.kr NIIED notice; the subagent cross-checked the quota table against a mirror whose content matched the official notice verbatim. High confidence on the 2,000 total and track split.
- **CSC per-year award/applicant counts are not authoritatively published;** figures above the 2014 MOE baseline (36,943 scholarship recipients of 377,054 total) are unofficial third-party estimates and should be flagged as such. The MEXT 9,223 figure is a 2015 enrolment stock, the most concrete official count located; it is dated and is not an annual award figure.
- **The specific SKKU refund clause** ("not applicable to fees supported by any internal or external financial aid program") seen in an earlier run could not be verified verbatim on a live official SKKU page; the underlying mechanism (aid deducted at billing; late/external awards paid in cash) is corroborated by SKKU's official scholarship page, so the policy substance stands even though that exact sentence is unconfirmed.
- **Refund-on-visa-refusal** is confirmed for Korea University and SKKU from their own pages; it was not separately confirmed verbatim on SNU/KAIST pages for the international-degree track and should be verified per institution before relying on it there.
- Where a fact is genuinely unpublished it is stated as such: international applicant counts/selection rates (all three countries), MEXT/CSC applicant counts, and whether a *late GKS award* triggers a reimbursement of prepaid tuition as a uniform national rule — all **not published**.
